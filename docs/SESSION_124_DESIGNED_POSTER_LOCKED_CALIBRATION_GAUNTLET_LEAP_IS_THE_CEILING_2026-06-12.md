## Part 2 — Drawing-board locks (the session's real wins)

1. **Users:** D2C founders + local businesses. They can already make template creatives in Canva. **The edge:** AI lets us build concepts/visuals "impossible to create without a graphic designer and creative team" — days of agency work in minutes. The user's sentence is the floor test, verbatim, for the future skill.
2. **The Canva floor:** "could Canva have made this?" → if yes, we shipped nothing. Retroactively explains the shelf-ad dislike (it's a capture; Canva-adjacent).
3. **The unit = DESIGNED POSTER, built not captured.** Layers: scene + real product at hero scale (bound pixels) + hook as composed type + DR furniture (CTA chip, offer/availability) + bright commercial light. Remove a layer and it stops being one. "Poster" without furniture drifts to brand-poster — the furniture is welded into the definition.
4. **Three reference ads (user-supplied screenshots; saved in transcript, not on disk):**
   - **Anvika ACV** — zero-bend: bold product-hero, forced perspective, question hook ("Cravings Killing Your Diet?"), callouts, CTA. Nothing impossible. 
   - **Bansal's grocery** — medium bend: delivery girl hands the box THROUGH the social-app like-bar to a couple below. UI as portal.
   - **Nutrich kebabs (Zepto)** — full invented world: classical painting's king reaches OUT of the frame to grab a kebab. Gag IS the appetite claim.
   - Shared anatomy: hook as designed type / real product hero / one visual idea that dramatizes the CLAIM (never the company) / DR furniture always / bright commercial light.
5. **"Every ad must be impossible" = FALSIFIED by the user's own picks** (Anvika has zero bend). Impossibility is a **dial** (zero-bend → invented world), not a requirement. The constant is the craft band: "looks like a creative team made it."
6. **Generalizations from the local-biz pressure test:** (a) the impossible must be **transparently impossible** (a gag), never plausible enhancement — hotels especially (the buyer's #1 fear is lying photos); (b) for services, "real product pixels" generalizes to **the real thing the buyer walks away with** (visa stamp, offer letter, room) — bind to the artifact.
7. **Direction lock:** literalize the PROMISE, never the brand (the Keynote literalized "we're a big deal" — with a buyer-outcome intake check, the Keynote class becomes ungeneratable).
8. **Half-second read** (user, on my balloon idea): no decode chains. A cold scroller gets it instantly or it's dead. Comprehension = buyer physics = floor.
9. **Judges need pixels** (user): "I can't judge them without seeing the visual." Text-judged takes starve the only judges that matter. Renders cost pennies; **render the set, kill on pixels.** ("Render once" economy was wrong for calibration.)

4. **Desire-code, never problem-code** (the "acidity ad" law) — enact the promise's desired state in the category's energy; relief-staging reads as medicine. Wrong-world enactment is fatal even when the pun is instant.

7. **Design voice ≠ the ceiling** — three genuinely distinct voices, all "average, decent typography." Voice is floor.
8. **First-draw ideas and Sonnet-volume-30 do not contain the leap.** 0/30 at the idea stage.

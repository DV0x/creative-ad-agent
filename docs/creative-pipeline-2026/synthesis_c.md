## 1. UGC Brief Generation

### Why Briefs Are a New Output Type

The pipeline currently generates only AI-rendered static images. Research B establishes that UGC outperforms branded content by 3-5x on CTR, delivers 50% lower CPC, and converts at 3-6% versus 1-3% for polished ads. Research A confirms that founder selfie content and customer testimonials are among the top-5 performing creative types under Andromeda. The critique identifies this as a structural blind spot: the pipeline's strategic intelligence (research extraction, hook generation, emotional angle selection) has no channel into the content types that perform best.

A UGC brief translates the pipeline's strategic output into a structured document that a human creator can execute. The brief is the artifact -- not the video itself. The pipeline controls the strategy; the human controls the performance.

### UGC Brief Template: Founder Selfie Format

This is the most accessible UGC format. It requires no external creator, no production budget -- only a founder, a phone, and a window.

```
## FOUNDER SELFIE BRIEF

### Campaign Context
- Brand: [Name]
- Product/Offer: [Specific product or promotion]
- Funnel Stage: [TOF / MOF / BOF]
- Target ICP Segment: [From research brief -- specific persona]
- Psychological Driver: [Pain / Aspiration / Social Proof / Urgency / Education / Transformation]

### The Hook (First 3 Seconds)
- Opening line (spoken): "[Exact scripted line -- conversational tone]"
- Text overlay (burned in): "[5-8 word hook displayed on screen]"
- Visual action: [What the founder does physically -- e.g., holds product, looks at camera, walks into frame]
- Emotional register: [Specific emotion to convey -- frustration, excitement, pride, vulnerability]

### Script Structure (15-30 seconds total)
1. HOOK (0-3s): [Opening line + visual action]
2. PROBLEM/CONTEXT (3-8s): [1-2 sentences establishing why this matters]
3. BRIDGE (8-15s): [How the product connects to the problem -- mechanism, story, or proof]
4. PROOF (15-22s): [Specific claim, number, testimonial reference, or demonstration]
5. CTA (22-30s): [Single clear next step aligned to funnel stage]

### Talking Points (Not a Script -- Hit These Beats Naturally)
- Beat 1: [The core pain point in customer language, sourced from VoC research]
- Beat 2: [The owned differentiation -- what ONLY this brand can claim]
- Beat 3: [Specific proof point most relevant to this ICP segment]
- Beat 4: [Transformation or outcome in customer language]

### Shot Direction
- Framing: Vertical (9:16), head-and-shoulders, natural background
- Lighting: Natural window light, no ring light
- Eye line: Direct to camera lens
- Environment: [Specific -- home office, kitchen, warehouse, etc.]
- Product integration: [When and how to show product -- e.g., "hold product at Beat 3, show label at proof point"]
- Energy: [Conversational, not performative. Speak like telling a friend.]

### What NOT to Do
- Do not open with brand name or product name
- Do not use ring light (signals "ad" to audience)
- Do not script word-for-word -- hit the beats, speak naturally
- Do not include multiple CTAs
- Do not mention competitors by name

### Technical Specs
- Aspect ratio: 9:16 (1080x1920)
- Duration: 15-30 seconds (under 15 preferred for TOF)
- Captions: Burned-in, bold black-on-white, positioned in center frame
- Audio: Direct voice, no background music (or very subtle)
```

### How Research Data Feeds Into Briefs

The brief template draws from specific pipeline outputs:

| Brief Field | Pipeline Source | Research Section |
|-------------|---------------|-----------------|

| Opening line (hook) | Hook bank -- top-performing hooks per driver | Research C, Section 1 |

Generic research produces generic briefs. Both positions in the debate agree on this prerequisite.

## 2. Format Diversity

### Current State vs. Recommended Mix

| Format | Current Pipeline | Recommended (Research A) | Gap |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------------|-----|
| Video (Reels/Feed) | 0% | 50% | Pipeline cannot generate video |
| Static Image | 100% | 30% | Over-indexed on single format |
| Carousel | 0% | 20% | No carousel support |

The pipeline serves 30% of the recommended format allocation (Research A, Section 5; confirmed in Critique, Part 3.2). Format diversity is itself a ranking signal under Andromeda -- different formats generate different engagement patterns and different Entity IDs (Research A, Section 5).

### What the Pipeline Can Realistically Generate Per Format

#### Static Images (Current + Expanded)

**Current capability:** AI-rendered images in clay diorama style.
**Expanded capability:** 4-6 additional visual styles (see Advocate position), each producing compositionally distinct Entity IDs.

**Realistic output per session:** 6-12 static images across 4-6 styles.

**Styles mapped to categories and funnel stages:**

| Style | Best Category | Best Funnel Stage | Entity ID Risk |
|-------|--------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Product-on-gradient | E-commerce, beauty, high-SKU | All | Low (product-focused, distinct from lifestyle) |
| Typography-dominant | SaaS, tech, services | TOF/MOF | Very low (no rendered scene) |
| Infographic/data-visual | Health, fintech, science-backed | MOF/BOF | Very low (educational layout) |
| Editorial cutout | Fashion, beauty, premium D2C | All | Low (isolation treatment is compositionally distinct) |
| Lifestyle-render | Wellness, home goods, fitness | TOF | Medium (environmental scene) |
| Surrealist/conceptual | Awareness campaigns, crowded categories | TOF | Very low (novelty-driven) |

#### Carousel Storyboards (New Output Type)

The pipeline generates a card-by-card narrative blueprint. Each card specifies hook, copy, and visual direction. The marketer or designer executes the cards.

**Carousel Storyboard Template:**

```
## CAROUSEL STORYBOARD

### Campaign Context
- Brand: [Name]
- Funnel Stage: [TOF / MOF / BOF]
- Carousel Type: [Story progression / Educational / Social proof sequence / Before-after / Product showcase]
- Card Count: [3-7 cards -- first card is strongest, mobile users see ~1.75 cards initially]

### Card Sequence

#### Card 1: THE HOOK (Must stop the scroll)
- Visual direction: [What the image shows -- this is the scroll-stopper]
- Text overlay: "[5-10 word hook -- question, bold claim, or pattern interrupt]"
- Purpose: Create curiosity or tension that compels swipe

#### Card 2: THE PROBLEM / CONTEXT
- Visual direction: [Illustrate the pain point or situation]
- Text overlay: "[Expand on the hook -- deepen the tension]"
- Purpose: Validate the viewer's experience, build emotional investment

#### Card 3: THE MECHANISM / BRIDGE
- Visual direction: [Show HOW the product solves the problem]
- Text overlay: "[Explain the mechanism or differentiator]"
- Purpose: Education -- give the viewer a reason to believe

#### Card 4: THE PROOF
- Visual direction: [Testimonial, data point, before/after, or social proof]
- Text overlay: "[Specific proof -- number, quote, or result]"
- Purpose: Remove doubt, build trust

#### Card 5: THE CTA
- Visual direction: [Product beauty shot or offer visual]
- Text overlay: "[Clear CTA aligned to funnel stage]"
- Purpose: Convert attention into action

### Visual Consistency Rules
- Color palette: [Consistent across all cards]
- Typography: [Same font family, size hierarchy]
- Layout pattern: [Consistent positioning of text vs. image elements]
- Aspect ratio: 1:1 (1080x1080) for feed carousel

### Carousel Types and When to Use

| Type | Structure | Best For |
|------|-----------|----------|
| Story Progression | Hook -> Problem -> Solution -> Proof -> CTA | TOF prospecting |
| Educational | Hook -> Step 1 -> Step 2 -> Step 3 -> CTA | MOF education |
| Social Proof Sequence | Review 1 -> Review 2 -> Review 3 -> Product -> CTA | BOF conversion |
| Before/After | Before state -> Transformation -> After state -> How -> CTA | Beauty, fitness, wellness |
| Product Showcase | Hero product -> Feature 1 -> Feature 2 -> Lifestyle -> CTA | E-commerce, high-SKU |
```

**Realistic output per session:** 2-3 carousel storyboards (different types mapped to different funnel stages).

## 3. Creative Testing Framework

### Hook x Style Matrix Generation

Under Andromeda, the pipeline should produce creative as integrated concepts, not isolated elements. The 3-3-3 framework (Research C, Section 6.3) provides the testing structure: 3 funnel levels x 3 angles x 3 formats = 27 combinations. The pipeline adapts this to its output capabilities.

**Matrix Structure:**

```
HOOK x STYLE TESTING MATRIX

Hooks (from hook bank):
H1: [Pain-driver hook -- e.g., "Tired of X?"]
H2: [Aspiration-driver hook -- e.g., "Imagine waking up to Y"]
H3: [Social-proof-driver hook -- e.g., "50K customers switched to Z"]

Styles (from visual style library):
S1: Product-on-gradient
S2: Typography-dominant
S3: Clay diorama
S4: Editorial cutout

Output Matrix (H x S):
| | S1 | S2 | S3 | S4 |
|--|----|----|----|----|
| H1 | H1-S1 | H1-S2 | H1-S3 | H1-S4 |
| H2 | H2-S1 | H2-S2 | H2-S3 | H2-S4 |
| H3 | H3-S1 | H3-S2 | H3-S3 | H3-S4 |

= 12 distinct creatives, each a unique Hook x Style combination
= 12 potential Entity IDs (maximizing auction diversity)
```

Each cell represents a distinct Entity ID candidate. The same hook rendered in a different visual style creates a compositionally distinct creative that Andromeda classifies separately. This is the direct mechanical consequence of Entity ID clustering (Research A, Section 3).

### Funnel-Stage Creative Sets

The pipeline should generate creative sets tagged by funnel stage, not isolated assets.

**TOF Set (Awareness -- Reach New Audiences)**
- 3-4 static images: Different psychological drivers (pain, aspiration, curiosity)
- 1-2 video storyboards: Hook-first Reels, pattern interruption
- 1 carousel storyboard: Educational or story-progression type
- 1-2 organic post concepts: Educational or hot-take format
- Copy framework: AIDA or storytelling
- CTA tone: Soft -- "Learn More," "See How," "Discover"
- Visual styles: Surrealist/conceptual, typography-dominant, lifestyle-render (scroll-stopping, novel)

**MOF Set (Education -- Build Consideration)**
- 2-3 static images: Mechanism explanation, ingredient/feature breakdowns
- 1-2 video storyboards: Founder video, product demo, FAQ format
- 1 carousel storyboard: Educational or social-proof sequence
- 1 UGC creator brief: Testimonial or expert review format
- Copy framework: PAS or FAB
- CTA tone: Educational -- "See Results," "Read Reviews," "Watch Demo"
- Visual styles: Infographic/data-visual, editorial cutout, product-on-gradient (information-dense, trust-building)

**BOF Set (Conversion -- Drive Purchase)**
- 2-3 static images: Offer-driven, urgency, price/value
- 1 carousel storyboard: Social proof sequence or product showcase
- 1 founder selfie brief: Personal recommendation with offer
- Copy framework: Direct offer + urgency
- CTA tone: Direct -- "Shop Now," "Claim Offer," "Get 40% Off"
- Visual styles: Product-on-gradient, typography-dominant (clear, direct, product-focused)

### Refresh Cadence: The Weekly Operating System

Research consensus on refresh cadence:
- 5-10 new ads launched weekly (Research A, Section 10; Research C, Section 6.5)
- Run untouched for 7 days before evaluating (Research A, Section 10)
- Creative fatigue after 4 exposures to same ad -- conversion drops ~45% (Research A, Section 4)
- Top-performing ads see significant drops within 2-4 weeks (Research A, Section 4)
- High-spend accounts need new variants every 3-4 days (Research B, Section 6.2)

**Pipeline Cadence Model:**

| Week | Action | Pipeline Output |
|------|--------|----------------|
| Week 1 | Initial creative generation | Full set: 6-12 statics, 2-3 carousels, 2-4 video storyboards, 2-3 briefs, 3-5 organic posts |
| Week 2 | Evaluate Week 1, launch refresh | 5-10 new variations on winning hooks x new styles |
| Week 3 | Retire underperformers, iterate on winners | New hook angles using same winning visual styles |
| Week 4 | Full creative refresh | New hook bank generation, new style combinations |

**Rotation logic:** When a hook performs well, rotate it across new visual styles. When a visual style performs well, rotate new hooks through it. The Hook x Style matrix makes this systematic rather than ad hoc.

### A/B Test Structure

For each test cycle, the pipeline generates matched pairs:

- **Same hook, different style:** Tests which visual treatment resonates (Entity ID diversity test)
- **Same style, different hook:** Tests which message resonates (messaging test)
- **Same hook+style, different funnel copy:** Tests which CTA and copy length converts (conversion test)

Each pair runs for 7 days minimum with 100+ conversions before declaring a winner (Research C, Section 8.2.1). Winners graduate to the Scale campaign; losers are retired.

## 4. Andromeda Optimization

### Entity ID Diversity Checklist

Before deploying any creative set, validate against this checklist. Each item that fails indicates Entity IDs will cluster, reducing auction diversity.

**Pre-Launch Entity ID Audit:**

- [ ] **Visual style count:** Are there 4+ visually distinct styles in the creative set? (1-2 styles = 1-2 Entity IDs regardless of hook count)
- [ ] **Psychological driver diversity:** Are there 3+ different emotional drivers represented? (Pain, aspiration, social proof, urgency, education, transformation)
- [ ] **Format diversity:** Are there 2+ formats in the set? (Static + carousel, or static + video storyboard)
- [ ] **Storytelling structure variation:** Are there 3+ different narrative structures? (Testimonial, demo, comparison, founder story, educational)
- [ ] **Logo swap test (per hook):** Could a competitor use this hook by changing the brand name? If yes, revise to include owned positioning.
- [ ] **Tone variation:** Are there 2+ different tones? (Urgent, playful, authoritative, relatable)
- [ ] **Copy length variation:** Is there a mix of short-form (<125 chars) and medium-form (125-150 chars) primary text?

**Entity ID Danger Signals:**
- All creatives use the same background color/gradient family
- All creatives use the same composition layout (e.g., product centered, text above)
- All creatives address the same pain point in the same emotional register
- All creatives use the same visual style (e.g., all clay diorama)
- Hooks differ only in headline text while sharing the same visual

### Creative Fatigue Monitoring Signals

Implement these monitoring thresholds to trigger creative refresh before performance degrades:

| Signal | Metric | Threshold | Action |
|--------|--------|-----------|--------|
| Hook rate decline | 3-sec video views / impressions | >20% drop from baseline | Refresh hooks, test new opening lines |
| CPMr spike | Cost per 1,000 unique reach | Exceeds $20 or rises >30% week-over-week | New creative needed -- not bid adjustment |
| Frequency ceiling | Average impressions per user | >4 exposures per user | Rotate to new creative set |
| CTR decay | Click-through rate | >15% decline from peak | Replace underperforming creatives |
| Creative age | Days since launch | >14 days for TOF, >21 days for BOF | Mandatory refresh regardless of metrics |
| Conversion rate decline | Purchases / link clicks | >20% decline from first-week baseline | Full creative and offer refresh |

**The critical insight (Research A, Section 4):** Rising CPMr is the primary early warning signal. When CPMr rises above $20, the advertiser is paying to show the same ads to the same people. The fix is creative refresh, not bid adjustment. The pipeline should flag this as a re-run trigger.

### Performance Feedback Loop Design

The pipeline currently has no feedback mechanism. Here is the minimum viable feedback loop:

```
FEEDBACK LOOP: Creative Performance -> Pipeline Iteration

1. TAG OUTPUTS
   - Every hook tagged with: psychological_driver, funnel_stage, hook_type, owned_position_reference
   - Every visual tagged with: style_name, composition_type, category_match
   - Every brief tagged with: format, creator_type, emotional_arc

2. CAPTURE RESULTS (Manual Input -- v1)
   - After 7-day run, marketer inputs per-creative:
     - Hook rate (3-sec views / impressions)
     - CTR (link clicks / impressions)
     - Cost per result
     - Conversion rate
     - Creative age at retirement

3. PATTERN EXTRACTION
   - Which psychological_driver produced the highest hook rates?
   - Which style_name produced the lowest CPM?
   - Which funnel_stage x hook_type combinations produced the best cost-per-result?
   - Which hooks passed/failed in practice vs. logo-swap prediction?

4. ITERATE
   - Next pipeline run: Weight toward winning drivers, styles, and types
   - Deprioritize (don't eliminate) underperforming combinations
   - Generate new variations within winning directions
   - Test new combinations in losing directions (may need different visual treatment)
```

V1 is manual input. V2 (future) integrates with Meta Ads API or analytics platforms to pull performance data automatically.

### Mobile-First Specifications

Research B establishes that 94-98% of Meta activity is mobile. 90% of inventory is vertical (9:16). The pipeline must enforce these constraints:

**Aspect Ratio Coverage:**

| Ratio | Dimensions | Required For | Priority |
|-------|-----------|-------------|----------|
| 9:16 | 1080x1920 | Reels, Stories | Mandatory -- 90% of inventory |
| 4:5 | 1080x1350 | Feed (optimized) | Mandatory -- outperforms 1:1 by 15% |
| 1:1 | 1080x1080 | Carousel, Feed (standard) | Required for carousel |

**Composition Constraints for Art Style Prompts:**

- **Safe zones:** Leave 180 pixels at top and bottom free of text/logos (Meta UI overlay)
- **Thumb-safe CTA placement:** Place CTA text in center or top-40% of image (not bottom corners)
- **Thumbnail legibility:** All text must be readable at 300px width (mobile feed thumbnail size)
- **Minimum font size:** 12pt at final rendered size
- **High contrast requirement:** Text must pass 90%+ legibility at thumbnail scale (white on dark or dark on light, no mid-tone on mid-tone)
- **Maximum text overlay:** 2-3 short phrases per static image, positioned in top-40% or center
- **Product visibility:** Product must occupy 30-40% of visual space for product-focused styles

**Per-Style Mobile Constraints:**

| Style | Mobile-Specific Rule |
|-------|---------------------|
| Product-on-gradient | Product centered in middle 60% of frame, gradient extends to edges |
| Typography-dominant | Text stacked vertically, max 3 lines, fills 50-70% of height |
| Infographic/data-visual | Max 4 data points per image, each in its own visual block |
| Editorial cutout | Product cutout fills 40-60% of frame, clean edges |
| Lifestyle-render | Atmospheric scene with product clearly visible at thumbnail |
| Surrealist/conceptual | High-contrast focal element in center, abstract elements at edges |

## 5. Implementation Roadmap

### Reconciling the Debate: What to Build and When

The debate between advocate (AI styles first) and skeptic (briefs first) reveals that both investments are necessary and the question is sequencing. The reconciliation: they are largely independent work streams that can run in parallel, with one exception -- brief quality depends on the improved research layer.

**Dependency Map:**

```
[Research Layer Improvements] -----> [Brief Generation Quality]
         |
         +-------------------------> [Hook Quality (all formats)]

[Visual Style Expansion] ----------> [Entity ID Diversity (statics)]
         |
         (no dependency on research for initial styles)

[Brief + Storyboard Templates] ----> [Format Diversity (video, carousel)]
         |
         (depends on research layer for strategic content)
```

### Phase 1: Parallel Foundation (Weeks 1-2)

**Stream A: Visual Style Expansion (Highest Immediate Impact)**

Add 4-6 new art style workflows. Each requires a workflow file and prompt engineering extending existing infrastructure. The critique (Part 7, Tier 1, Item 1) identifies this as the single highest-leverage change.

Priority order for styles:
1. **Product-on-gradient** -- Lowest risk, highest versatility, dominant D2C pattern (Research B, Section 2.3). Works for every category. No uncanny valley risk. Immediately multiplies Entity IDs.
2. **Typography-dominant** -- Zero uncanny valley risk. Serves SaaS/tech/services where product is intangible. High mobile legibility. Compositionally maximally distinct from all other styles.
3. **Infographic/data-visual** -- Serves MOF/BOF education hooks. Authority-building. Distinct compositional pattern. Best for science-backed brands, health, fintech.
4. **Editorial cutout** -- Product isolation with artistic treatment. Growing trend (Research B, Section 9.1). Serves fashion, beauty, premium D2C.
5. **Lifestyle-render** -- Aspirational environmental scene. Medium uncanny valley risk (avoid humans). Best for wellness, home goods, aspirational brands.
6. **Surrealist/conceptual** -- Attention-disrupting, TOF optimized. Scroll-stopping through novelty. Best for crowded categories.

**Effort:** Each style is a workflow markdown file + prompt engineering. Extends existing infrastructure (SKILL.md routing). Estimated 1-2 days per style.

**Stream B: Strategic Research Layer (Foundation for Everything Else)**

Both debate positions agree this must happen. These changes improve every output mode.

Changes to research agent template:
1. Add "Owned Positioning" section -- single most impactful research change (Research D, Part H)
2. Add "Competitive Context" field -- even LLM-knowledge-based competitive intelligence improves specificity
3. Add feature-to-emotion translation layer -- every feature mapped to emotional benefit
4. Add buying objection extraction per ICP segment -- each objection becomes a hook angle
5. Add VoC customer language section -- trigger events, review language, hesitation language (Research D, Part C)

Changes to hook skill:
1. Enforce logo-swap test as hard validation gate -- hooks that fail get revised, not shipped
2. Require hooks to trace to owned positioning
3. Add hooks for objection-handling (not just need-creation)

**Effort:** Template additions to research.md and SKILL.md. No new architecture.

### Phase 2: Brief Generation (Weeks 2-4)

With the improved research layer from Phase 1 providing richer strategic inputs:

1. **Founder Selfie Brief** -- Most accessible format. No production infrastructure needed. Template driven by research brief + hook bank.
2. **UGC Creator Brief** -- Full script with shot list. For brands with creator access.
3. **Carousel Storyboard** -- Card-by-card narrative. Semi-direct execution using design templates.
4. **Organic Post Concept** -- Ideas optimized for boosting. Leverages organic engagement signals.

**Effort:** Text template generation using existing research and hook data. The pipeline already produces structured text. This is a new arrangement of existing capabilities.

### Phase 3: Format Expansion (Weeks 4-6)

1. **Video Storyboard Outlines** -- Scene-by-scene production briefs enabling the video portion of 50/30/20 mix.
2. **Category-Aware Style Routing** -- Art style SKILL.md detects brand category from research brief and biases default style selection accordingly.
3. **Mobile Composition Constraints** -- Add safe-zone enforcement, thumb-zone awareness, and thumbnail legibility rules to all art style prompt workflows.

### Phase 4: Feedback and Iteration (Weeks 6+)

1. **Performance tagging system** -- Every output tagged with driver, style, funnel stage, hook type.
2. **Manual feedback input** -- Marketer inputs 7-day performance data per creative.
3. **Pattern extraction** -- Identify winning driver x style x funnel combinations.
4. **Iteration weighting** -- Next pipeline run biases toward winning combinations.
5. **Creative analytics dashboard** -- Track hook rate, CTR, cost-per-result, creative age by tag.

### Priority Justification

| Phase | Why This Order | Blocking Dependency |
|-------|---------------|-------------------|
| 1A: Visual Styles | Fixes the binding constraint (Entity ID clustering). All other improvements are partially wasted while output collapses to 1-2 IDs. | None -- extends existing architecture |
| 1B: Research Layer | Foundation for hook quality, brief quality, and all output differentiation. | None -- template changes only |
| 2: Brief Generation | Unlocks highest-performing content types (UGC, founder). Requires improved research layer. | Depends on 1B for strategic quality |
| 3: Format Expansion | Covers video and carousel portions of recommended mix. Requires brief infrastructure. | Depends on Phase 2 templates |
| 4: Feedback Loop | Enables data-driven iteration. Requires enough output diversity to generate meaningful comparisons. | Depends on Phases 1-3 for tagged output |

### What This Achieves

After full implementation, a single pipeline session produces:

- **6-12 static images** across 4-6 visually distinct styles (6-12 potential Entity IDs)
- **2-3 carousel storyboards** with per-card direction (2-3 additional Entity IDs)
- **2-4 video storyboard outlines** as production briefs (enabling video format)
- **2-3 UGC/founder briefs** directing highest-performing content types
- **3-5 organic post concepts** for boosting
- **All outputs tagged** with psychological driver, funnel stage, visual style, and hook type
- **Hook x Style matrix** enabling systematic A/B testing
- **Funnel-stage sets** (TOF/MOF/BOF) with format-appropriate creative in each

This moves the pipeline from "6 hooks + 6 images in one style" to "15-30 strategically diverse outputs across multiple formats, styles, and funnel stages" -- aligned with the 5-10 new ads/week cadence that the research universally recommends.

## Summary

The pipeline's output strategy must evolve in three dimensions simultaneously:

1. **Visual diversity** -- From 1-2 Entity IDs to 6-12 through new art styles. This is the binding constraint fix that unblocks all other improvements.

2. **Format diversity** -- From 100% static to static + carousel storyboards + video storyboards + UGC briefs + organic post concepts. Covers the 50/30/20 recommended mix through a combination of direct output (statics) and strategic direction (briefs, storyboards).

3. **Testing structure** -- From ad-hoc creative to systematic Hook x Style matrices, funnel-stage sets, and refresh cadence. The pipeline outputs creative ready for structured A/B testing, not isolated assets.

The research layer improvements (owned positioning, VoC, competitive context, logo-swap validation) are the prerequisite that makes all three dimensions strategically differentiated rather than generically competent.

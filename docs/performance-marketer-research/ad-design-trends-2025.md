## 1. Mixed Media Ad Trends

**Confidence: HIGH** -- Multiple authoritative sources converge on these trends.

### The "Tactile Rebellion" (2026 Dominant Movement)

The single biggest aesthetic movement in design for 2026 is what Creative Bloq and multiple sources call the **"tactile rebellion"** -- a deliberate rejection of AI's hyper-polished aesthetic in favor of work that feels unmistakably made by human hands. This is not a minor counter-trend; it is the dominant direction.

**Key characteristics:**
- **Hand-built sets, stitched textures, analog surfaces** -- designers are putting their hands back on the work literally
- **Physical collage, ink, fabric, clay** -- materials that mimic touch, sound, and motion
- **Natural light over studio lighting** -- imperfections valued over perfection
- **Visible imperfections of overlapping layers** offering depth and narrative storytelling
- **Mixed media combining photos, doodles, stamps, and brush textures**

### Scissorworks / Hand-Done Collage

Philip VanDusen identifies "Scissorworks" as a major 2026 trend -- hand-done collage combining found artwork, imperfect cut edges, and archaic imagery creating juxtapositions. This is a direct response to AI's polished imagery, a renaissance of analog expression that leans into imperfection and narrative depth.

**Visual description for image generation:**
- Torn paper edges with visible fiber texture
- Layered photographs over painted or textured backgrounds
- Hand-cut shapes with slightly irregular edges
- Mix of found vintage imagery with contemporary photography
- Visible tape, pins, or adhesive elements holding composition together
- Intentional overlap with parts of images obscured by other layers

### Mixed-Media Layering

Digital Synopsis lists mixed-media design as a core 2026 trend: "layered combinations of photography, illustration, typography, and texture creating dimensional richness rejecting flatness." This goes beyond simple photo + text -- it is about creating depth through diverse visual material types:

- Photography composited with hand-drawn illustration elements
- Text that interacts with imagery (behind objects, through cutouts)
- Scanned paper textures layered under or over digital photography
- Brush strokes, ink splatters, or paint texture overlaying clean product shots
- Film grain or halftone dots applied selectively (not globally) for texture variation

### The "Low Ink" / Xerography Aesthetic

It's Nice That highlights a punk-inspired trend of embracing the imperfect qualities of office photocopiers -- grain, debris marks, low-coverage effects. This deliberately unpolished, greyscale approach references punk zine culture and independent publishing.

**Visual description:**
- High-contrast black and white with blown-out highlights
- Visible scanner/copier artifacts (streaks, dust marks, toner spots)
- Text that looks hand-typed or stamped rather than digitally set
- Layered photocopied images with degraded quality used as aesthetic choice

## 2. Layout Patterns & Composition

**Confidence: HIGH** for the patterns listed; MEDIUM for specific conversion data.

### Dominant Layout Formats for Meta Ads

**4:5 Vertical Format** is now the default. Instagram's 2025 grid update moved to 4:5 aspect ratio for profile thumbnails. The 4:5 vertical crop outperforms 1:1 square in Feed placements by up to 15%. Design everything at **1080px x 1350px** as the starting point.

### Trending Composition Patterns

#### Split-Screen / Dual Composition
- One side significantly larger, containing image; other side with bold text
- Before/after arrangements showing transformation (very common in DTC)
- Two contrasting color environments telling parallel visual stories (Pepsi example: pastel beach vs. desert landscape)
- Problem vs. Solution side-by-side layouts

#### Asymmetric Layouts
- Designers breaking free from rigid grids and symmetry
- Elements of varying size, weight, or position -- intentionally off-kilter yet visually engaging
- Weighted compositions where the product or key message occupies 60-70% of the frame

#### Bento Grid / Modular Layouts
- Compartmentalized sections providing structured yet flexible organization
- Multiple information zones within a single ad frame
- Strong for carousel-style thinking applied to static images

#### Centered Product with Negative Space
- Billboard-style layout creating aspirational feel
- Product as sole visual element, positioned centrally
- Almost text-free execution signaling brand confidence
- Extensive whitespace with minimal visual elements (Indeed, Nike approaches)

#### Tilted / Non-Standard Angles
- Products and elements placed at angles rather than straight-on
- "Straight angles are obvious. We see dozens of them everyday" -- tilted compositions create visual tension
- 10-15 degree rotations on product shots or text blocks create dynamism

#### Visual Index / Specimen Layout
- Assets arranged as flat inventories resembling trinket collections or entomology displays
- Items numbered, laid out with no real sense of scale or proportion
- Nostalgic, specimen-like layouts influenced by Gen Z's curation culture
- Products or ingredients laid out flat on colored backgrounds

### Carousel Performance

Carousels are currently performing very well on Meta and are considered a "hot format." Story-driven sequencing through carousel slides drives both engagement and conversion.

## 3. Typography Trends in Ads

**Confidence: HIGH** -- Extremely well-documented across multiple design trend reports.

### Hot Typography Treatments

#### Condensed Sans-Serifs (Still Dominant)
- Bold, humanist nuances with compact, impactful design
- Projects strength and clarity
- Ideal for punchy, short headlines in ad creative
- Searches for "bold font" rose 65.7% heading into 2026

#### Variable Fonts
- Single font file allowing multiple styles and weights to coexist
- Flexible weight, width, and style adjustments on the fly
- Increasingly used for responsive ad creative across placements

#### Oversized / Hero Characters
- Single letterforms displayed at large scale, dominating layouts as sculptural objects
- Letters used as design elements, not just text
- Initial letters of brand names or product names blown up to fill the frame

#### Liquid & Experimental Typography
- "Letterforms stretch, soften, and flow" suggesting movement in static compositions
- Wavy, distorted, bubble-like fonts
- Type Collage: blending multiple fonts, sizes, and styles for instant visual energy

#### Collaged, Mismatched Typography
- Experimental lettersets prioritizing expression over legibility
- Characters sourced from scavenged materials, handwriting, or unconventional methods
- Individual letters deliberately "off-kilter" and unperfected
- Ransom-note style that feels punk/DIY

#### Brute Force Typography
- Heavy stacked type, spreadsheet-style grids, deadpan compositions
- Mainly black-and-white with sparse color accents
- Intentionally haphazard appearance conveying authenticity
- Think: fashion editorial meets brutalist web design

#### Serif Revival
- Serifs experiencing a major comeback, especially curvy serif fonts with 1970s energy
- Psychedelic, warm, personality-driven
- Works well for beauty, wellness, and lifestyle brands wanting warmth

#### Ink Trap Fonts
- Amplified functional details introducing rhythm and character
- "Typography that feels engineered yet expressive"
- Popular in tech and SaaS ad creative

### Typography as Design Element (Not Just Copy)

The overarching shift: typography is moving from functional text delivery to being the primary visual element. In many high-performing ads, the headline IS the design, with the product secondary. Text treatments include:

- Text behind object (AI-powered depth effect where text appears to pass behind the product)
- Knockout text / transparent inside (text shape cut out revealing background image)
- Bold outlines / stroke text creating comic-book style impact
- Reversed or mirrored text as "thumb stopper" creating curiosity

### Practical Ad Typography Rules

- **Headline: 2-3 words maximum**, significantly larger font
- **Benefits section: 3 callouts maximum** with readable font sizes
- **High contrast between text and background** -- guarantee legibility in under 1.2 seconds
- **Remove logos from ad creative** since Meta profile logos appear adjacent

## 4. Color & Texture Trends

**Confidence: HIGH** for trend direction; MEDIUM for specific palette recommendations.

### Color Macro-Trends

#### Saturation Revival
After years of muted, "millennial" palettes, bold saturated colors are back:
- **Deep jewel tones, neon, multi-layered gradients**
- Unexpected color pairings (vibrant blues clashing with fiery oranges)
- Rich pinks, greens, corals replacing the muted sage/blush/taupe era
- Searches for colorful assets exceeded 8,000 in a recent two-month period with 67% conversion

#### Acid Fade Gradients
- High-saturation gradients transformed into neon, prismatic, heat-map blends
- Psychedelic liquidity and smooth transitions
- Bold, youthful optimism -- effective for Gen-Z targeting
- Not your 2019 gradients: these are MORE saturated, more prismatic, almost hallucinatory

#### Blue-Green Dominance
- Blue-greens predicted as THE color direction of 2026
- Blending natural ocean mystery with clean technology sleekness
- Works across tech, wellness, and lifestyle verticals

#### Pantone Cloud Dancer (Bone White)
- 2026 Color of the Year: soft, warm white signaling clarity and creative reset
- Functions as breathing-room neutral base layer
- Paired with bold accent colors or texture for impact

### Texture Treatments

#### Film Grain / Analog Noise
- Film grain applied intentionally (not globally) to make flat digital art feel tangible
- Print-style halftone dots maintaining tactile feel
- VHS overlays and 1980s-inspired warm tungsten lighting effects
- Film grain emulation guides seeing significant engagement (indicates practitioner interest)

#### Grainy Gradients
- Modern gradients integrating grainy/noise layers for a dreamy aesthetic
- "Soft Gradients 2.0" -- subtle, airy blends feeling like light passing through glass
- Textured, imperfect designs reflecting broader movement where authenticity is prioritized over perfection

#### Neomorphism / Soft Shadows
- Soft shadows and highlights creating realistic, three-dimensional appearance
- Modern visual aesthetic for product and UI-focused ads
- Works particularly well for app marketing and tech products

#### Bronze Age / Metallic Textures
- Bronze tones combined with mineral textures
- Simultaneously ancient and futuristic
- Signals craftsmanship and artisanal quality
- Ideal for premium spirits, coffee, fragrances, luxury goods

#### Obsidian / Deep Black
- Glossy black backgrounds with precise typography
- Dramatic contrast between matte and gloss finishes
- Luxury positioning through intentional darkness
- Effective for perfume, tech hardware, boutique fashion

### What Works in Ad Color Specifically

- **White backgrounds** tend to perform better for product-focused ads
- Colors influence up to **90% of an initial impression**
- **Soft purples, pinks, and greens** are aesthetically pleasing options that test well
- **High-contrast palettes** (near-black with white, or saturated brand hue + clean white) guarantee legible text
- **Red backgrounds with white typography** for maximum contrast and urgency (HP approach)

## 5. Specific Visual Techniques

**Confidence: HIGH for trend existence; MEDIUM for conversion impact.**

### Aura / Glow Effects Around Products

The "aura" phenomenon is massive in 2025-2026:
- "Aura" mentioned over **2.3 million times online** in 2025, generating 31.9 billion impressions
- Pinterest dubbed "aura beauty" one of the biggest trends of 2025
- Applied to products as neon halos, soft colored backlighting, luminous edge effects
- Strong diffused backlight illuminating products (especially liquids/translucent items)
- Creates radiant, luminous quality -- product appears to emit light

**Image generation prompt elements:**
- Colored light emanating from behind product
- Soft neon edge glow (not harsh)
- Gradient halo fading from product outward
- Ambient colored light reflecting on surrounding surface

### Ripped Paper / Torn Edge Effects

Part of the broader "Scissorworks" and collage revival:
- Torn paper overlays adding texture and depth
- Ripped edges creating literal windows into product imagery
- Brown kraft paper, wrinkled materials with organic edges
- Layering torn papers in front of and behind photos for dimensionality
- Significant template marketplace demand (Canva, Etsy, Figma all offering templates)

**Image generation prompt elements:**
- Product image visible through torn paper opening
- Irregular, fibrous paper edges (not clean cuts)
- Multiple paper layers at different depths
- Vintage or kraft paper texture surrounding modern product photography

### Duotone Photography

Enduring trend popularized by Spotify:
- Two contrasting colors creating dramatic visual effect
- Portrait photography and product shots produce strongest results
- Y2K aesthetic variant: neon pastels paired with retro fonts
- Works across branding, social media, and advertising

**Image generation prompt elements:**
- Product or lifestyle photo mapped to two-color palette
- High contrast maintained through tonal mapping
- One warm + one cool color for maximum impact
- Applied to backgrounds while product retains natural color (selective duotone)

### Product Floating with Shadows

Active technique in 2025 ad design:
- Products suspended in space creating magic, motion, and intrigue
- Custom shadows crucial for selling the effect (opacity and blur matching lighting conditions)
- Shadow direction and intensity aligned with main light source
- Subtle motion blur or atmospheric effects enhancing sense of levitation
- "Creates wonder about why the product is floating in air" -- prompts engagement through mystery

**Image generation prompt elements:**
- Product suspended against clean or gradient background
- Soft drop shadow directly below, slightly diffused
- Slight motion blur on product edges suggesting gentle movement
- Clean, even lighting (not dramatic shadows on the product itself)

### Geometric Frames / Masks on Photos

Strong 2025 technique:
- Images masked into geometric or abstract shapes
- "Photo Obscura" trend: fragmentation and transparency for selective reveals
- Non-traditional shapes (organic blobs, hexagons, circles) acting as photo frames
- Layering effects using cropped photos alongside textures
- Deconstructed modular, puzzle-like compositions

**Image generation prompt elements:**
- Product or lifestyle photo visible through geometric cutout shapes
- Clean geometric border (circle, hexagon, arch) framing product
- Multiple geometric windows showing different product angles
- Abstract blob shapes containing photography against colored background

### Layered Transparent Elements

Philip VanDusen's "Translucence" trend for 2026:
- Multi-dimensional worlds through overlapping transparent layers
- Mimicking vellum or frosted plastic
- Building visual depth without full 3D representation
- Modular visual space that invites viewer participation

Also related: **Glass Block** effect:
- Vertical ribbing and refracted imagery
- Type distorted through translucent barriers
- Like privacy glass brought into digital space
- Modern, tactile feel without requiring full 3D

**Image generation prompt elements:**
- Semi-transparent colored overlays partially covering product
- Frosted glass panel between viewer and product
- Multiple depth layers with varying opacity
- Refractive distortion through glass-like elements

### Film Grain / Analog Aesthetics

Full-on revival in 2026:
- Grainy textures, warm tungsten lighting, VHS overlays
- 1980s-inspired sets evoking rediscovered home videos
- Halftone printing effects for tactile quality
- Deliberately degraded image quality as aesthetic choice
- Search interest in 80s themes increased 83.7%

**Image generation prompt elements:**
- Visible film grain across entire image (fine, organic noise)
- Warm color temperature (tungsten/golden shift)
- Slightly washed-out blacks (lifted shadows)
- Subtle color fringing or chromatic aberration at edges
- Light leak effects (warm orange/yellow streaks)

### Bold Color Blocking with Photo Cutouts

Predicted to dominate in 2025-2026:
- Combining solid, contrasting blocks of color in single composition
- Rich, saturated tones with unexpected pairings
- Product cutout placed against bold solid color sections
- Structured color blocks creating visual hierarchy that directs the eye

**Image generation prompt elements:**
- Product with transparent/removed background placed on bold color block
- 2-3 strong contrasting color sections dividing the frame
- Product at intersection of color blocks
- Clean edges between color sections (not blended)

### 3D Elements Mixed with Flat Design

The hybrid approach is accelerating:
- "Dimensional depth blended with illustrative texture"
- Hyper-realistic 3D product renders integrated with flat graphic elements
- 3D product floating above 2D illustrated or graphic background
- Stylized (not photorealistic) 3D rendering gaining ground

**Image generation prompt elements:**
- Photorealistic product rendering against flat-colored or illustrated background
- Subtle 3D shadow grounding product in space
- Flat graphic elements (icons, shapes, text) coexisting with 3D product
- Slight perspective on product while background remains flat

## 6. Designed vs AI-Generated Quality Gap

**Confidence: HIGH** -- Well-documented by Nielsen Norman Group, IAB, Smartly, and multiple sources.

### The Quantified Trust Gap

- **48% of consumers** trust ads co-created by a person with AI support
- **Only 13%** trust ads created entirely by AI
- Ad executives believe 82% of Gen Z/Millennials feel positive about AI ads, but **only 45% actually do** -- a 37-point perception gap that WIDENED from 32 points in 2024
- **86% of marketers** have seen AI outputs that resemble competitor content (the "sameness" problem)
- **Over 70% of marketers** have encountered AI-related ad incidents (hallucinations, bias, off-brand content)

### What Makes an Ad Look "AI-Generated" (The Tells)

#### Visual Tells
1. **Glossy over-smoothness** -- AI has difficulty creating accurate textures with fine detail, producing a characteristic sheen
2. **Uncanny valley in human elements** -- fingers, toes, teeth, hair strands remain problematic
3. **Too-perfect symmetry** -- AI defaults to pleasing symmetry that reads as artificial
4. **Homogeneous lighting** -- lack of the subtle inconsistencies real-world lighting creates
5. **Texture repetition** -- AI textures repeat patterns in ways that organic textures never do
6. **Semantic incoherence** -- objects that look plausible individually but don't make logical sense together (e.g., buttons that don't align, impossible product angles)

#### Compositional Tells
1. **Narrative shaped by technology** -- "audiences perceive when the narrative is shaped around what the technology CAN do rather than what the story SHOULD be"
2. **Rapid fragmented editing** (in video) -- using 1-second clip montages to mask distortions
3. **Distant camera shots** -- avoiding close-ups that would reveal flaws
4. **Soulless production quality** -- technically coherent but emotionally vacant
5. **Everything too clean** -- no dust, no wear, no imperfection, no history

#### The McDonald's / Coca-Cola Lesson
- McDonald's Netherlands AI ad was technically coherent but labeled "AI slop" for violating emotional expectations
- Coca-Cola improved technically between iterations but retained "artificial quality" even after eliminating problematic human close-ups
- Both cases: viewers detected something was off not from specific visual errors but from the OVERALL feeling of the creative

### What Professional Designers Do That AI Misses

1. **Intentional imperfection** -- human designers add imperfections that create warmth and authenticity (slightly uneven kerning, hand-adjusted color, organic layout decisions)
2. **Narrative coherence** -- every visual element serves the story; AI adds elements because they look good, not because they mean something
3. **Cultural specificity** -- references to specific subcultures, eras, or aesthetics that AI blends into generic "aesthetic"
4. **Emotional pacing** -- knowing when to show less, when to leave space, when to break a pattern
5. **Material intelligence** -- understanding how real paper tears, how real ink bleeds, how real light falls
6. **Contextual hierarchy** -- the viewer's eye is guided through a specific journey; AI tends to distribute visual weight too evenly
7. **Brand memory** -- consistency with a brand's visual history and evolution, not just its current logo and colors

### The Emerging Design Counter-Strategy

Designers are deliberately leaning INTO what AI cannot do:
- **Tactile, handmade elements** that reference physical craft
- **Cultural and subcultural specificity** (punk, zine, specific eras)
- **Deliberate imperfection** as a trust signal
- **Process visibility** -- showing the hand of the maker
- **Material honesty** -- if it's a collage, show the paper; if it's painted, show the brushstrokes

This is creating a **bifurcation in ad aesthetics**: AI-generated "clean" creative on one end, and deliberately human "crafted" creative on the other, with the middle ground (generic "designed-looking" creative) being squeezed out.

## 8. High-Performing Static Ad Formats

**Confidence: HIGH** -- Based on Obvi's documented $40M framework, Motion data, and multiple practitioner reports.

### The Obvi Framework (8 Formats That Scale)

These 8 static ad formats drove a DTC brand to $40M+ revenue:

1. **Lifestyle Banner** -- Product in real-world context
2. **UGC Banner** -- User-generated content styled as ad
3. **Render Banner** -- 3D product renders on clean backgrounds
4. **Review Ad** -- Customer testimonial as primary content
5. **Community Ad** -- Social proof and engagement signals
6. **Us vs. Them** -- Competitive comparison layout
7. **Problem vs. Solution** -- Before/after or challenge/resolution
8. **News/Media** -- Editorial-style format with press mentions

### Key Static Ad Data Points

- Static image ads still account for **40% of all global display impressions**
- Static images drive approximately **60-70% of conversions on Meta** -- should NOT be abandoned for video
- **56% of digital marketers** still rely on static creatives as a core part of their media mix
- Static ads require **~15 minutes to create vs. one week** for video, enabling rapid iteration
- In beauty vertical, brands iterate creatives **every 7-10 days**, testing 3-5 visual variations per product

### What Information Hierarchy Works

Based on Motion's analysis:
1. **Headline: 2-3 words maximum** -- significantly larger font size
2. **Product: visually dominant** -- centered or weighted placement
3. **Benefits: 3 callouts max** -- readable but secondary to headline
4. **CTA: clear but not dominant** -- the design itself should compel action
5. **Logo: REMOVE from ad creative** -- Meta profile logo provides brand identity; logo on ad creates "blindness in the feed"

### Creative Diversity > Creative Volume

Since Meta's Andromeda algorithm update, creative diversity matters more than volume:
- Test trust-building angles across DIFFERENT formats (UGC unboxing, lifestyle + social proof, creator testimonials)
- When you find a winner, repurpose that MESSAGING across new VISUAL formats
- Balance: static vs. video, lo-fi vs. polished, emotional vs. rational benefits

## 9. Scroll-Stopping Visual Hooks

**Confidence: HIGH** for the techniques; MEDIUM for specific performance claims.

### The Physics of Attention

- Users decide within **0.5 seconds** whether to engage with a post
- The first **2-3 seconds** of video determine everything
- **92% of people** scroll without sound -- visuals and text do the heavy lifting
- Recognition must happen in under **1.2 seconds** (lock logo, color, and type to a consistent frame)

### Visual Pattern Interruption Techniques

1. **Unexpected imagery** -- fitness brand opening with someone in office clothes working out; skincare ad beginning with cream applied to a tomato
2. **Reversed/mirrored text** as curiosity driver
3. **Photo collage opening** -- static photo collage of creator with product (works well for beauty/lifestyle)
4. **Product in impossible context** -- floating, suspended, in unexpected scale relationship

### Trending Visual Hook Formats (2025)

Based on GoP rimer's analysis:

1. **ChatGPT Q&A Hook** -- Interface mimicry using ChatGPT's visual design to introduce product benefits
2. **Typing Effect** -- Animated text appearing character-by-character with keystroke sounds
3. **Green Screen** -- Creator filmed against dynamic composited backgrounds
4. **Fake Podcast** -- Ad formatted as podcast episode (584M global podcast listeners primed for this format)
5. **Incoming Call** -- Phone interface replicating incoming calls, exploiting instinctive behavior
6. **Slow Motion** -- Extended duration playback enhancing perception of texture, comfort, pleasure
7. **Mirror Selfie** -- Creator films through mirror reflection, appears personal/non-promotional

### Visual Pacing for Retention

- Switch camera angles or visual elements every **3-4 seconds**
- Add zooms, crop-ins, or motion graphics to keep frame fresh
- Layer value progressively: tease new information every few seconds
- "Oh, and there's one more thing..." structure encourages continued viewing

## 10. Implications for AI Image Generation Workflows

**Confidence: MEDIUM** -- These are derived recommendations, not directly observed.

### What Image Generation Should Prioritize

Based on this research, an AI image generation workflow for performance marketing ads should be able to produce:

#### Must-Have Capabilities

1. **Product on colored background with shadow** -- The single most common static ad format. Clean product cutout, solid or gradient background, realistic drop shadow.

2. **Product floating with aura glow** -- Product suspended with colored backlighting/halo effect, soft shadow below, slight motion suggestion.

3. **Bold color blocking** -- 2-3 contrasting solid color sections with product at focal point.

4. **Lifestyle context integration** -- Product naturally placed in real-world scenes without looking composited.

5. **Before/after or comparison layouts** -- Split-frame compositions showing transformation or competitive comparison.

6. **Text-behind-object composition** -- Bold headline text appearing to pass behind the product (depth effect).

7. **Film grain / analog texture overlay** -- Selectively applied grain, warm color shift, light leaks that make the image feel photographed rather than generated.

#### Differentiating Capabilities

8. **Collage / mixed-media composition** -- Torn paper edges, layered photographs, hand-drawn elements combined with product photography.

9. **Geometric frame / mask composition** -- Product visible through geometric cutout shapes (circles, hexagons, arches, abstract blobs).

10. **Duotone / selective color treatment** -- Two-color palette mapping on background or secondary elements while product retains natural color.

11. **Transparent / glass overlay effects** -- Frosted glass, translucent panel, refractive distortion elements creating depth layers.

12. **Specimen / flat-lay composition** -- Multiple products or ingredients arranged in curated, grid-like display with clean spacing.

### Critical Quality Markers to Avoid "AI Slop"

To avoid the AI-generated look, the workflow should:

1. **Add intentional imperfections** -- Slightly uneven spacing, organic texture variation, not-quite-perfect alignment
2. **Use culturally specific references** -- Specific era aesthetics, subculture visual language, not generic "aesthetic"
3. **Maintain material honesty** -- If there's paper, it should look like paper; if there's light, it should behave like light
4. **Create narrative coherence** -- Every element should serve a purpose, not just fill space
5. **Apply selective (not global) effects** -- Grain on the background but not the product; glow behind but not on the product
6. **Include subtle imperfection in text** -- Slight hand-adjusted kerning, imperfect baseline, weight variation within a word
7. **Ground products in physical reality** -- Consistent shadow direction, realistic reflections, appropriate scale relationships

### Aesthetic Modes to Support

## 11. Gaps & Uncertainties

### What This Research Could NOT Verify

1. **Specific conversion rate data by visual technique** -- While multiple sources claim certain techniques "outperform," hard conversion data comparing specific visual styles (e.g., floating product vs. flat-lay) is not publicly available. Most conversion claims come from agency marketing content (potential bias: HIGH).

2. **AI image quality trajectory** -- The gap between AI-generated and designed ads is closing rapidly. Experts warn telltale signs "are becoming obsolete within months." The quality markers listed above may have a shelf life of 6-12 months. **Confidence in longevity: LOW.**

3. **Vertical-specific performance data** -- Whether torn-paper aesthetics convert better in beauty vs. tech, or whether color blocking works better for CPG vs. fashion, is not documented in the available research. The trends are reported as universal when they likely have significant vertical variation.

4. **Counter-evidence on "authenticity" trend** -- The research overwhelmingly claims "authentic, raw content outperforms polished." However, Obvi (a $40M brand) is one of the few documented cases of successfully using AI-generated images. The "authenticity beats polish" narrative may be more aspirational than empirical. **Confidence: MEDIUM.**

5. **Geographic variation** -- Nearly all research is US/Western-focused. Performance marketing ad aesthetics in APAC, LATAM, or Middle Eastern markets may differ significantly. This research does not address non-Western ad design trends.

6. **The "designed vs. AI" distinction's future** -- If AI tools improve to the point where they CAN produce intentional imperfection, cultural specificity, and material honesty, the quality gap markers identified here become irrelevant. The question is whether that happens in 6 months, 18 months, or 3 years. **Confidence in the 12-month relevance of these markers: MEDIUM.**

7. **Carousel-specific design patterns** -- Despite carousels being highlighted as a "hot format," detailed research on what visual design patterns work specifically within carousel sequences is thin. Most trend reports focus on single-image or video creative.

### What Would Change These Conclusions

- If Meta's algorithm shifts to strongly favor video over static (currently static drives 60-70% of conversions, but this could flip)
- If AI image generation achieves reliable "intentional imperfection" (would collapse the human-craft premium)
- If consumer trust in AI-generated content rises significantly (currently only 13% trust fully AI content)
- If a new platform/format disrupts Meta's 4:5 vertical dominance
- If the "tactile rebellion" trend is a bubble that collapses back toward clean/minimal (possible if it becomes oversaturated)

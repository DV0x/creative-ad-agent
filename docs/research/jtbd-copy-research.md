# JTBD Copy Research — Creative Machines

Research date: 2026-04-26
Audience: D2C founders running their own paid social, and in-house perf marketers at 10–50 person D2C brands.
Product: AI ad-creative generator that produces 6 brand-researched ad concepts (hook + image) from a brand URL in ~5 minutes.
Goal: Replace the current hero (`Ship your next winning ad by 4pm.`) with copy grounded in the actual job the user hires the tool for.

---

## 1. The JTBD framework, briefly

Jobs-to-be-Done isn't a personality test. It's a forensic question: "What was happening in this person's day that made them stop doing the old thing and try this new thing?" Bob Moesta's Switch Interview pulls the timeline of a real purchase — first thought, passive looking, active looking, deciding event, first use, habit — and the friction points (anxieties, habits) that almost killed the switch. The output is not "a persona," it's a sentence: *"When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]."* (Source: Bob Moesta, *Demand-Side Sales*; jobstobedone.org).

For copy, the practical translation is a three-layer test on every headline (Sivo Insights, thrv.com, Stone Mantel):
- **Functional job** — the literal task the customer needs done. Measurable. ("Ship 20 fresh creative variants into Meta this week.")
- **Emotional job** — how they want to feel during/after. ("Not panicked on Sunday night. Not embarrassed when the CMO asks 'what are we testing?'")
- **Social job** — how they want to be seen by peers/boss/team. ("As the operator who actually knows what's working, not the one pasting Canva templates.")

A hero headline that hits the functional job alone reads like a feature list ("Generate 100 ads in 5 minutes"). One that hits all three reads like a relief ("Stop briefing freelancers on Sunday. Ship a week of brand-researched creative before standup."). The Brooks Lockett / GrowthX / Trajectory frameworks all converge on the same two formulas worth stealing:
- *"Make [time-consuming task] our job, not yours."*
- *"It's a new [capability] for your [job-to-be-done]."*

The Basecamp lesson is the warning: customers didn't say "project management" — they said "stop the chaos." The tool's category language and the customer's job language are usually different, and the customer's wins. (Source: Intercom Bob Moesta interview).

## 2. Jobs being hired

### D2C founder (solo or 1–3 person team, $50K–$500K/mo ad spend)

- **Functional**: "Get 5–10 new ad concepts I can launch into Meta this week without sitting in Canva for 6 hours, paying a freelancer $400 per asset, or briefing someone who needs 4 days." This founder is the media buyer, the brand voice, and the creative director. Volume requirement is real: most successful DTC brands run 50+ ads live (Motion, Constanthire), and creative refresh cycles are 7–10 days.
- **Emotional**: "Not feel like an imposter when I open Ads Manager. Not dread the weekend creative session. Not feel guilty that I'm shipping the same UGC angle for the 4th week in a row." Weekend Canva burnout is a recurring complaint in indie maker communities (IndieHackers; Constanthire: "62% of founders cited 'creative fatigue' as their biggest growth bottleneck").
- **Social**: "Look like a brand, not a side hustle. When my customer sees my ad next to Hexclad's or Caraway's, I don't want to look amateur." This is identity work — the founder wants to be seen (by customers, peers in DTC Twitter, future hires) as someone running a real operation.

### In-house performance marketer (10–50 person D2C brand)

- **Functional**: "Ship 20–50 new creative variants per week into Meta + TikTok, structured around testable hypotheses (hook × visual × angle), without waiting on the design team or burning the freelancer budget." Performance creatives "influence 50–200 creative assets every month, across multiple platforms" (Marketing Week, Intelligent People job specs). Barry Hott: "5–7 new concepts weekly... creative fatigue happens 80% faster than it did in 2020."
- **Emotional**: "Not be the bottleneck. Not have to defend low hook rates in the Monday standup because we shipped only 4 things last week. Feel in control of the testing roadmap, not subordinate to a freelancer's calendar." The fear is being judged on hook rate (target: 25%+ Meta, 30%+ TikTok per Sovran/Hawky benchmarks) and CAC, not on the volume of beautiful Figma boards.
- **Social**: "Be seen by the CMO/founder as the person who runs a creative system, not the person who chases freelancers. Be quoted on DTC Twitter/Motion case studies as the operator who 'increased top-of-funnel spend 60%' (Hexclad framing). Look like a strategist, not a producer."

A note on segmentation (constitution principle 13 — "is the category right?"): the two segments share the *functional* job (ship more variants faster) but diverge sharply on the *social* job. The founder wants to feel like a real brand. The in-house marketer wants to feel like a real strategist. Copy that addresses only volume/speed will land flat for both because it ignores the harder job.

**Patterns across the set:**
- Everyone leads with **volume** ("100,000 ads", "thousands of ads", "20+ variants") or **outcome** ("14x conversions", "win", "dominate"). Nobody leads with **input quality** — i.e., what the tool *knows* before it generates.

- Motion is the most-respected adjacent tool but solves the *analysis* job, not the *generation* job. They're a likely partner/peer, not a direct competitor.

## 5. Market gap (positioning opportunity)

There are two gaps stacked on top of each other.

**Gap #1: Nobody owns "researched, not generated."** Every AI ad tool sells *output volume*. None credibly owns *input quality*. The dominant complaint about AdCreative.ai, Pencil, and the rest in the verbatim research is exactly this: they produce variants but the variants are generic because the system doesn't know the brand. Creative Machines' actual moat — every hook is traceable to specific brand research (reviews, product pages, customer language) — directly answers the loudest objection in the category. The positioning sentence to occupy is something close to: *"AI ads that read like your brand wrote them, because we read your brand first."* Or more sharply: *"Your hooks. Pulled from your reviews. Not generated from a prompt."*

**Gap #2: Nobody speaks the perf marketer's KPI vocabulary in the hero.** "Hook rate," "variants," "creative testing," "fatigue" are the daily working language of both audience segments — and they appear in zero competitor headlines. A hero that uses the audience's actual KPI words signals "we built this for you" before the sub-headline even loads. This is the cheapest differentiator available because it costs nothing and competitors apparently can't bring themselves to do it (likely because they're chasing broader TAM).

**The combined positioning bet**: *Brand-researched creative concepts (not templates, not generic AI) ready to test on Meta and TikTok in five minutes.* This is defensible (constitution principle 8) because the research-extraction workflow is harder to copy than image generation — competitors would have to re-architect. It's desirable because it answers the loudest verbatim complaint in the category. It is fragile (principle 9) to one specific 12-month risk: GPT-5/Claude-class models that can do brand research + image gen in a single prompt with comparable fidelity. The defense against that is workflow lock-in (campaign history, brand asset library, review ingestion) — not the prompt itself.

## 6. Headline patterns that work

From Wynter, Brooks Lockett, Trajectory, GrowthX, Dayana Mayfield (saashero):

1. **Specificity beats cleverness.** "Drive more revenue" is dead on arrival. "Ship 6 brand-researched ad concepts in 5 minutes" lands. Wynter's clarity-rated panels consistently kill metaphorical heroes.
2. **Job-replacement formula works.** *"Make [time-consuming task] our job, not yours"* and *"Do [JTBD], not [time-consuming task]"* — both convert because they name the user's actual pain explicitly.
3. **Subhead = mechanism, not benefit.** The H1 names the job; the sub explains *how* it works (the differentiator). Most competitor heroes redundantly restate the H1's promise in the sub instead of explaining the method.
4. **Use the audience's vocabulary, not yours.** Basecamp lost 8 years using "project management" before realizing customers said "stop the chaos." The perf marketer says "hooks," "variants," "fatigue," "hook rate." Use those.
5. **Length: 6–10 words for H1, 15–25 for sub.** Longer H1s test worse for clarity (Wynter data, paraphrased).
6. **Avoid hope-words.** "Win," "dominate," "growth," "unleash" are background noise — every competitor uses them. They don't signal anything.
7. **Lead with the deliverable, not the process.** "AI generates ads" is process. "6 ad concepts in your brand's voice" is deliverable. Deliverable converts better because it answers "what do I get?" before "how does it work?"

## 7. Five candidate hero drafts

Each combination is built from a specific job in §2 and a specific verbatim phrase in §3. None invent claims. Each one trades off differently — read the "risk" line.

---

### Draft A — The "research-first" pitch (leads with the moat)

**Headline:** Ad concepts pulled from your brand's research — not from a prompt.
**Sub:** Paste your URL. We read your reviews, product pages and customer language, then deliver six on-brand hooks and visuals — ready to test in Meta or TikTok in five minutes.
**Supporting line:** Six hook angles. Six visuals. One pack. Twenty-five cents per creative.

- **Job hired:** Functional (ship variants fast) + emotional (it'll sound like *us*, not like AI slop) + social (I look like a strategist who has a system, not a hack-job).
- **Why it works:** Directly answers the #1 verbatim objection in the AdCreative/Pencil category ("the AI doesn't know my brand"). Owns Gap #1. Names the deliverable concretely (six). Uses "test" (perf marketer vocabulary). Avoids hope-words.
- **Risk / objection:** "Pulled from research" sounds like work — could read as slow. The "five minutes" beat needs to be loud enough in the sub to defuse that.

---

### Draft B — The "stop the Sunday brief" pitch (leads with the founder's emotional job)

**Headline:** Stop briefing freelancers on Sunday night.
**Sub:** Creative Machines reads your brand and ships six tested ad-concept directions — copy and image — before your morning standup. No Canva. No Fiverr. No two-week turnaround.
**Supporting line:** Built for D2C operators shipping 20+ creatives a week.

- **Job hired:** Emotional (relief from weekend dread) + social (I'm not the founder duct-taping ads in Canva at midnight).
- **Why it works:** Pulls directly from the IndieHackers / Constanthire verbatim ("Canva templates are generic", "creative fatigue", "weekend builds"). Names the enemy (Canva, Fiverr, freelancer brief) — gives the founder someone to be relieved from. The "20+ a week" line signals "we know your volume requirement."
- **Risk / objection:** Skews founder-centric; the in-house perf marketer might read "freelancers" and feel this isn't built for their team workflow. Also strong negation in H1 ("Stop") — some test panels prefer positive-framed H1s.

---

### Draft C — The "hook rate vocabulary" pitch (leads with KPI language)

**Headline:** Six new hook angles. In your brand's voice. Every five minutes.
**Sub:** Creative Machines researches your brand, then generates six ad concepts in six tested copywriting frameworks — stat, story, FOMO, curiosity, callout, contrast — each with matched art direction. Test more, test faster, fatigue slower.
**Supporting line:** Hook rate is the #1 predictor of creative performance. Stop running the same one for the fourth week.

- **Job hired:** Functional (ship more diverse hooks) + social (I run a real testing system).
- **Why it works:** "Hook angles" is verbatim perf marketer vocabulary that no competitor uses. Names the six frameworks — concrete, novel, and signals method. Closes with the strongest verbatim claim in the research ("hook rate is the #1 predictor", Sovran). Owns Gap #2.
- **Risk / objection:** Assumes audience knows "hook" terminology. Stronger for the in-house perf marketer than the early-stage founder. Founder might not know what a hook framework is.

---

### Draft D — The "complete creative pack" pitch (leads with the deliverable problem)

**Headline:** A complete creative pack. Not just copy. Not just an image.
**Sub:** Paste your brand URL. Get six ad concepts — researched from your reviews and product pages, written in your voice, paired with on-brief visuals. Ready for Ads Manager in five minutes.
**Supporting line:** Replaces the brief, the freelancer, and the Canva afternoon. From $0.10 per creative.

- **Job hired:** Functional (the literal job: I need a *complete* thing I can launch, not raw material to assemble).
- **Why it works:** Directly addresses the founder's existing wording problem (the current hero doesn't communicate "complete creative"). Forces a contrast against Predis/Creatify (just video) and AdCreative (just templates). The "Replaces the brief, the freelancer, and the Canva afternoon" line is the substitution sentence the founder will mentally check against his current workflow.
- **Risk / objection:** Slightly defensive H1 (negation pattern). Less differentiated on the "research" moat than Draft A — the brand-research bit is buried in the sub.

---

### Draft E — The "operator system" pitch (leads with the social job for both segments)

**Headline:** Run a real creative testing system, not a freelancer queue.
**Sub:** Six brand-researched ad concepts per run, in six hook frameworks, with matched visuals. Built for D2C founders and in-house teams shipping 20–50 variants a week into Meta and TikTok.
**Supporting line:** Reads your reviews. Writes in your voice. Renders in five minutes. From $0.10 per creative.

- **Job hired:** Social (I want to be seen as the operator with a system) + functional (volume + diversity at the right cadence).
- **Why it works:** Names both segments explicitly — solves the segmentation tension in §2. "Real creative testing system" is the language Barry Hott / Motion / admetrics use to describe what mature DTC orgs aspire to. Positions Creative Machines as the *infrastructure* of that system, not just another generator.
- **Risk / objection:** "Freelancer queue" might offend agency-adjacent users — but the founder has explicitly de-prioritized that audience, so this is feature, not bug. Also slightly long; sub could compress.

---

### Recommended top pick: **Draft A**, with Draft C's KPI line as a secondary "why us" beat below the fold.

Draft A is the only candidate that leads with the actual moat (research-first, traceable hooks) — which is also the loudest verbatim complaint in the entire category. It owns Gap #1 (which no competitor occupies) and uses concrete deliverable language (six hooks, six visuals). Draft C is the strongest "audience tell" copy (uses perf marketer KPI vocabulary) but is tactically risky as a standalone H1 because it requires the reader to already speak the language. Pair them: Draft A as the hero, Draft C's hook-rate line as the reinforcement section.

If the founder wants to A/B test, the cleanest two-cell test is **A vs. B** — moat-led vs. emotion-led. Both are defensible, both pull from verbatim research, and the result will tell you which segment is showing up to the landing page in higher proportion.

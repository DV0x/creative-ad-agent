# q2-concept-origination

Creative strategists at performance marketing agencies usually turn **customer language + competitor ads + awareness-stage mapping** into a structured set of **concepts**, then generate multiple **variations** from each concept for testing. In practice, that means mining reviews and communities for “voice of customer,” studying what competitors are already running in Meta Ad Library/swipe files, then organizing findings into angles, personas, and Eugene Schwartz–style awareness stages before writing ads.[1][2][3][14]

- **Customer review mining / voice-of-customer mining**
  - Valchanova describes review mining as a way to understand “what customers care about and what words they use,” and says the workflow is to identify the pain point, find reviews of existing solutions, record the language in a VOC data file, and reuse that language in landing pages or ads.[14]
  - In the same guide, she says you can use Amazon for physical products and specialized review sites like Clutch, Yelp, Tripadvisor, and Booking.com depending on the offer type.[14]
  - Admetrics says high-performing creative teams “leverage insights from historical campaign data and customer feedback to prioritize concepts.”[1]

- **Reddit / community mining**
  - Moz’s Reddit-mining process is explicitly about finding “trends, patterns, and sticky ideas,” then polishing and “up-level[ing] the ideas” into content.[2]
  - The same Moz article recommends looking at posts from the past month, recategorizing results by upvotes, recency, and comments, and using that to surface recurring audience language and topics.[2]
  - Outbrain similarly says that for problem-centric messaging, “the best place to start is to find out what people with the problem you solve are talking about,” including forums and social groups, then create content that identifies the problem and begins to answer it.[15]

- **Competitor research via Meta Ad Library and swipe-file tools**
  - Direct practitioner descriptions of Foreplay/Atria were not present in the retrieved sources, so I could not verify named quotes about those specific tools.
  - What the retrieved sources do support is the broader competitor-analysis workflow: Admetrics recommends developing repeatable ad frameworks such as “problem/solution, demo-driven, or testimonial ads” and using prior campaign data and customer feedback to choose concepts.[1]
  - Salesforce defines marketing frameworks as structured approaches that help with market research, audience definition, value proposition, and channel choice; that is the same kind of structure agencies use when turning competitor ads into testable angles.[12]

- **Eugene Schwartz customer-awareness stages**
  - MotionApp lists the five stages as **Unaware, Problem Aware, Solution Aware, Product Aware, Most Aware** and frames them as a way to match ads to funnel stage.[3]
  - Outbrain gives the practical creative implication: for unaware audiences, show them the problem; for problem-aware audiences, educate on the problem; for solution-aware audiences, use testimonials, success stories, and reviews; for product-aware audiences, emphasize comparisons and product walkthroughs; and for most-aware audiences, use incentives and specific offers.[15]
  - The YouTube transcript quoted in the search results is even more direct: “if they’re not in the product awareness stage then don’t even talk about your product.”[7]

- **Angle/persona matrices**
  - The retrieved sources do not use the exact phrase “angle/persona matrix,” but they do describe the underlying method: map messaging territories to funnel stages, define the target audience, and tailor messages to specific customer groups.[1][12]
  - Zion & Zion says frameworks help map “what would help and what would hinder the customer experience,” then determine awareness at each touchpoint and identify opportunities for new ideas and advantages.[4]
  - That is the same logic agencies use when they cross customer segment, pain point, and awareness stage into a matrix of testable ad angles.

- **“Concepts vs variations”**
  - The clearest support in the retrieved sources is Admetrics’ recommendation to “develop frameworks” and test repeatable formats, then use customer feedback and historical data to prioritize concepts.[1]
  - The practical implication is a two-layer workflow: first create a **concept** such as “problem/solution,” “demo,” or “testimonial,” then generate **variations** of hook, offer, visual, and CTA within that concept for testing.[1]
  - The sources retrieved do not provide a named agency quote using the exact phrase “concepts vs variations,” so I can’t attribute that wording to a specific practitioner from the material provided.

- **What the actual workflow looks like across agencies**
  - Start with review mining and Reddit/community mining to extract customer objections, repeated phrases, and unmet desires.[2][14][15]
  - Classify the findings by awareness stage so the ad speaks to the right level of problem knowledge.[3][7][15]
  - Build a matrix of audience segment × pain point × proof type × offer type, then turn the strongest combinations into ad concepts.[1][4][12]
  - Check competitor ads for proven claims, formats, and visual structures, then create multiple variations inside each winning concept for iteration.[1][12]

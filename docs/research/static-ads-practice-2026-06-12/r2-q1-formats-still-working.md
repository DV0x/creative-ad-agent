# r2-q1-formats-still-working

Classic direct‑response static formats are **not dead market‑wide on Meta in 2025–2026**; practitioners are still scaling **us‑vs‑them charts, testimonial screenshots, tweet/Notes‑style statics, listicle-style “Top 5 reasons” graphics, etc.** But most operators frame the issue as **creative fatigue at the execution level** (an individual ad or brand angle burning out fast) rather than the *format* being saturated, and they pair these statics with UGC/reels as core formats.

Below is what current practitioners are *actually* saying, with dates and direct quotes.

---

## 1. Practitioners explicitly saying these formats still work

### 1.1 “Us vs Them”, listicle graphics, testimonials as go‑to Meta formats

**Stormy AI – Meta Advantage+ creative playbook (2025)**  
In a 2025 ROAS playbook for Meta Advantage+ campaigns, Stormy AI (a creative/automation platform) spells out which creative “concepts” you should be feeding Meta and explicitly includes classic DR static patterns:

- They tell brands that “This does not mean six versions of the same image with different headlines. It means six fundamentally different concepts:” and then list:
  - “**UGC (User‑Generated Content): Raw, authentic testimonial videos.**”
  - “**Product‑Focused: High-production shots highlighting features.**”
  - “**Educational/Listicle: "Top 5 reasons why..." graphics.**”
  - “**Comparison: "Us vs. Them" charts.**”[6] (article updated for 2025)

They present **“Top 5 reasons why…” listicle graphics** and **“Us vs. Them” comparison charts** as **recommended formats**, not as burned‑out relics, within a 2025 Meta‑Advantage+ context.[6]

They also explicitly link these to combating fatigue through variety:

- “By using Stormy AI to streamline creator sourcing and outreach, brands can maintain a constant stream of fresh UGC content. **This prevents "creative fatigue," a common issue where the AI stops showing your ads because the engagement rates have plummeted.**”[6]

Interpretation:  
For Stormy, fatigue is about **running the same creative too long**, not about the **format** (comparison chart, listicle) being saturated platform‑wide. The solution is more *concept variety* (including these classic statics), not abandoning them.

## 2. Practitioners calling specific classic patterns *problematic* or constrained

### 2.1 Before/after creatives: increasingly disallowed and risky in 2026

**Forge Digital Marketing – “How to Advertise Supplements on Meta (2026 Edition)”**  
This 2026 guide from an agency that runs Meta for supplements explicitly warns against one of your classic DR formats:

- “Avoid:  
  – **Dramatic before‑and‑after images for weight loss, muscle gain, or skin conditions.**  
  – Zoomed‑in ‘problem area’ shots that could be read as body shaming…  
  – Visuals that clearly imply a medical transformation…”[5]

And later:

- “**Don’t: Use dramatic transformations or weight-loss imagery**  
  – Avoid before‑and‑after photos, extreme physique changes, and visuals centered on ‘problem’ body parts.”[5]

This isn’t framed as “the format is fatigued,” it’s framed as **policy and compliance risk**:

- “Meta’s automated systems review the image or video as aggressively as the text, so your creative must be policy‑safe even on mute.”[5]

**Implication for your question:**

- **“Before/after” as a transformation trope is actively discouraged in 2026 in regulated categories** (supplements, weight loss, skin), not because of performance fatigue but because of **ad policy**—you simply get rejected or restricted.  
- The *replacement formats* they recommend are:
  - “Lifestyle imagery: people moving, cooking, working, and living—where the product is part of a healthy routine, not a magic fix.”[5]  
  - “Clean product shots … simple benefit callouts”[5]  
  - “Educational graphics” / “infographics that explain how ingredients support body functions … keep charts and diagrams simple”[5]  

For DR marketers in health categories, this is effectively a **forced creative migration away from classic before/after statics** to lifestyle, infographics, and soft claims.

## 3. Explicit discussion of *creative fatigue* vs “format is dead”

### 3.1 Omneky 2026 analysis: fatigue is rapid and systemic, but creative‑level

**Grow with Rohan – “How AI Is Changing Paid Advertising in 2026” (Jan 2026, summarizing Omneky Q1 2026 data)**  
The article cites Omneky’s cross‑account analysis on Meta creative fatigue:

- “According to **Omneky’s Q1 2026 analysis, creative fatigue degrades ad performance by an average of 28% after just 3–4 weeks of continuous serving.**”[2]

And further:

- “The industry benchmark for top-performing accounts in 2026 is **50+ active creative variants. Most advertisers run only 3–5.**”[2]

This is important for distinguishing **format‑level vs execution‑level fatigue**:

- Omneky’s data is about **individual creatives burning out in 3–4 weeks** due to frequency and engagement decay.[2]  
- There is **no claim that “us vs them images don’t work anymore” or “testimonial screenshots no longer convert.”**  
- Instead the operative prescription is high **creative velocity** and **variant count**, regardless of format.

The same article uses that insight to redefine what matters:

- “The advertisers winning in 2026 are not fighting the machine. **They are feeding it better data and more creative than their competitors.**”[2]

So: **fatigue is real and fast**, but in the **unit‑level** sense; the classic static frameworks still sit in the creative mix.

There’s no statement that **specific DR static formats have stopped working market‑wide**; the “solution” is **governance and creative refresh**, not abandoning “us vs them” or social‑proof statics.

## 5. Direct answers to your specific format questions

Below I’ll map practitioner commentary to each of the formats you mentioned, and clearly distinguish **format‑level “dead” vs. execution‑level fatigue.**

### 5.1 Us‑vs‑Them comparison charts

Evidence:

- Stormy AI calls “Comparison: ‘Us vs. Them’ charts” one of the **core creative concepts** to feed Advantage+ in 2025.[6]  
- Motion’s Kajabi library relies on “Us Vs Them” for **13%** of active creatives while shipping ~17 new creatives/week.[12]  
- Instagram media buyer’s tier list puts “Us vs Them” in **top image formats** (with “Tweet style images”).[9]

Assessment:

- **Format‑level:** No practitioner source is saying “us‑vs‑them charts are saturated or no longer work.” Instead they are explicitly recommended and actively used at scale.[6][9][12]  
- **Execution‑level:** High creative churn (e.g., 17 new creatives/week at Kajabi[12]) and Omneky’s 28% fatigue after 3–4 weeks[2] indicate that *individual* us‑vs‑them ads will burn out quickly and must be refreshed.

### 5.2 Testimonial / review screenshots

Evidence:

- Stormy’s list includes “UGC (User‑Generated Content): Raw, authentic testimonial videos.”[6]  
- The IG tier list includes “Testimonials” as S‑tier creative.[9]  
- ZenWeb: “UGC handles credibility, especially for products that need social proof.”[10]

Most of the current chatter is about **UGC testimonial videos**, but the same logic is applied to testimonial statics and screenshots; nobody is saying “review screenshot ads died.”

Assessment:

- **Format‑level:** Social proof / testimonial as a pattern is still treated as core and “S tier.”[6][9][10]  
- **Execution‑level:** As above, each specific testimonial screenshot will fatigue; practitioners fight this with volume and rotation, not by abandoning the pattern.

### 5.3 Notes‑app / tweet‑style static ads

Evidence:

- The IG tier list callouts “Tweets Style Images” explicitly as a **top image format** alongside “Us vs Them” and “Trending Memes.”[9]

While nobody is writing a 2,000‑word piece only about “Notes app ads,” the **tweet‑style/lo‑fi text static** idea is being grouped in as **top‑tier**.

Assessment:

- **Format‑level:** No evidence that tweet/Notes statics are considered “burned out”; one practitioner calls them a **top image format to use heavily.**[9]  
- **Execution‑level:** As with other statics, fatigue is governed by frequency & time‑in‑market (Omneky’s 3–4 week finding).[2]

### 5.4 Before/after statics

Evidence:

- Forge Digital Marketing 2026: “Avoid: Dramatic before‑and‑after images for weight loss, muscle gain, or skin conditions.”[5]  
- “Don’t: Use dramatic transformations or weight-loss imagery … avoid before‑and‑after photos…”[5]

Assessment:

- **Format‑level:** In **health/supplement/weight‑loss** verticals, before‑after imagery is effectively **off the table in 2026** due to Meta policy risk, not just saturation. It’s not “fatigued,” it’s **dangerous**.  
- **Execution‑level:** In less regulated verticals, there’s no public practitioner claim that “before/after” is dead; the main constraint is **compliance**, not engagement. But most sophisticated 2026 practitioners in sensitive niches are **replacing** it with:
  - Lifestyle ads[5]  
  - Ingredient/benefit infographics[5]  
  - Soft claims with structure–function language[5]

### 5.5 Listicles (in‑feed DR style)

Evidence:

- Stormy AI: “Educational/Listicle: ‘Top 5 reasons why…’ graphics” as a core creative concept.[6]  
- Replo: “Yes, listicles remain highly effective for engagement and SEO… They work especially well for mobile readers and social media sharing.”[3]

Assessment:

- **Format‑level:** Listicle‑style “Top X reasons” framing is **explicitly defended as effective** and recommended for Meta creative and landing pages.[3][6] No practitioner says listicle‑style ads are saturated.  
- **Execution‑level:** As with any static DR concept, a specific listicle graphic will fatigue at the ad level within weeks (per Omneky).[2]

## 6. How performance‑marketers are *operationalizing* this in 2025–2026

Putting all the practitioner evidence together, the operating reality looks like:

- **The old DR static tropes (us‑vs‑them, listicles, testimonials, tweet/Notes‑style statics) are still working and recommended**, especially as part of an Advantage+ creative mix.[6][9][10][12]  
- **Before/after is the main pattern that’s genuinely “out of bounds”** in regulated niches (supplements, weight‑loss, skin) for policy reasons.[5]  
- Practitioners define “fatigue” as **ad‑level decay over 3–4 weeks**, not as a format failing across all accounts.[2][7]  
- Winning teams are responding by:
  - Running **50+ active creative variants** in top accounts[2]  
  - Shipping **double‑digit new creatives per week** (Kajabi example: “~17 new creatives per week”[12])  
  - Ensuring **concept diversity**: UGC, comparison charts, listicle graphics, product shots, offer‑driven statics.[6][10]  
  - Using static DR formats primarily for **direct‑response and clarity**, while Reels and UGC carry scale and engagement.[10]

If you want practitioner‑aligned execution in 2025–2026, the evidence points to:

- **Keep using us‑vs‑them, testimonial screenshots, tweet/Notes‑style text statics, and listicle‑style graphics.**  
- Consider **retiring or heavily softening before/after** in any sensitive category due to policy risk.  
- Treat **fatigue as an execution‑level problem** solved by **creative velocity and concept variety**, not as a reason to abandon these classic DR formats entirely.

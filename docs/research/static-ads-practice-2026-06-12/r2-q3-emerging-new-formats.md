  - “Your ad should look and feel like high‑quality organic content.”[9]  
  This is directionally supportive of fake‑UI statics (since they mimic organic UI), but Vovia does **not** mention chat screenshots, Reddit threads, or search bars specifically.

Given your constraints (no fabrication, no gap‑filling):

- I **cannot** point to a 2025–2026 practitioner quote that says something like “iMessage‑style statics cut our CPA by 30% on Meta in 2025.”
- The strongest *documented* 2025–2026 trend is toward **native, organic‑looking creative and UGC‑style video**, not a named sub‑genre of fake‑UI statics.[9][12]

So for now, consider **fake‑UI statics**:  
- Widely seen as a *tactic* in ad swipes, but **not** something I can back with 2025–2026 Meta‑specific performance quotes.

## 2. AI‑generated surreal / “bending‑reality” visuals

**Finding:** There is solid 2025–2026 practitioner chatter and tool marketing around **AI‑generated, surreal, “reality‑bending” visuals** for ads, but it is mostly framed as “scroll‑stopping” creative and not tightly broken out into “static vs video” or “Meta vs other channels.”

### Evidence of the style emerging and being used in ads

- In April 2026, an Instagram creator post (captioned “Created entirely with AI using a surreal cinematic prompt. Reality bending itself for content again. The internet remains deeply unserious.”) explicitly shows **surreal, reality‑bending AI images** positioned as content/ad creative.[3]  
  That’s direct practitioner language that this kind of AI “reality bending” imagery is being created and shared in 2026.

- A 2026 TikTok by @rpn for the AI tool **Verv** (positioned for product ads) pitches:  
  - “Verv (@verv.fm) is an AI ad and product visual creation tool that generates highly curated, editorial‑grade photos & videos. **Most AI ads look the same**.”[15]  
  The positioning here is that AI‑generated visuals are mainstream enough that “most AI ads look the same,” and tools are differentiating by making **more curated, editorial‑grade** outputs.

- Another 2026 AI‑creative TikTok (Higgsfield/Flux) positions AI imagery as a way to create cinematic, “scroll stopping” visuals for ads and e‑commerce:  
  - The host says that with Flux/Higgsfield you “get a refined cinematic professional image that's more vibrant and visually striking and ready to be transformed into a video… a scroll stopping visual that converts.”[8]  
  Again, not explicitly labeled “surreal,” but the selling point is visually intense, AI‑assisted imagery used directly as ad creative.

### Are practitioners calling these **winners** on Meta specifically?

- None of these sources give Meta‑specific performance deltas (e.g., ROAS uplift of surreal AI statics vs normal product photos).
- Evidence is more **qualitative**: language like “scroll stopping visual that converts” for AI‑generated cinematic imagery used in paid ads.[8]  
- The Apple “Welcome Home” / HomePod “reality bending” spot is referenced in a 2025 TikTok as a canonical example of **bending reality** creative, but that’s TV/brand video, not a static Meta ad.[7]

So:

- **Yes, 2025–2026 practitioners are clearly producing and advocating AI‑generated, visually surreal or “reality bending” images and videos for ads.**[3][8][15]  
- **No, I couldn’t find a 2025–2026 Meta practitioner saying “our best‑performing static on Meta is this surreal AI still and here are the numbers.”**

- Current evidence is **practitioner positioning of their tools and aesthetic preferences**, not hard Meta performance numbers.

Given your constraints, I can say:

## 4. Long‑text “founder letter” statics

- I could not find 2025–2026 Meta practitioners or agencies publicly praising **long‑text “founder letter” statics** as a new winning format with concrete metrics.
- Most 2025–2026 advice emphasizes **short, thumb‑stopping hooks**, particularly in video/Reels, rather than long static text blocks:  
  - Funnel.io’s 2024–2025 Instagram ads best‑practice guide (used through 2025) tells advertisers to focus on strong **hooks** in the first 1–3 seconds and on “pattern interrupt,” “bold claim,” “question” or “before/after” visuals.[12]  
  - They emphasize **“showing, not telling”** and recommend visual, not text‑dense, communication.[12]

Given that:

- I **couldn’t** find evidence that long‑form founder‑letter static images are a widely reported Meta winner in 2025–2026.
- What *is* being stressed is concise hooks and visual storytelling, which cuts against long‑text image ads.[12]

## 5. Meme formats

This is the one area where the general creative trend and practitioner language align most strongly with your hypothesis, but hard 2025–2026 Meta‑specific performance data is still thin.

What we can say from 2025–2026 sources:

- Contemporary Meta/Instagram creative advice stresses **“pattern interrupt”** and **trend‑surfing**, which is exactly where meme statics sit:  
  - Funnel.io’s Instagram ad best‑practices piece (live and referenced in 2025) says you should:  
    - Use **“Pattern interrupt: Something visually unexpected that breaks the feed’s rhythm.”**[12]  
    - “Find trends and make them your own,” suggesting you watch the Reels tab and Explore page to see “which formats, audio clips and editing styles are getting traction right now.”[12]  
  That is high‑level advice to use **trending formats and visuals**, which for many performance advertisers in 2025–2026 means memes and memetic layouts — but Funnel.io does not name “memes” explicitly.

- Vovia’s 2026 article emphasizes **native, creator‑like content** and says “Reels consistently outperform static images.”[9]  
  For statics, the implication is that anything that looks like organic, shareable content (memes, carousels, UGC screenshots) is more aligned with user behavior, but again, they don’t explicitly call out memes as a category.[9]

What’s missing:

- I couldn’t locate 2025–2026 Meta practitioners publicly saying “meme statics tripled our ROAS” with dated quotes and numbers.
- Memes are clearly encouraged under the umbrella of “pattern interrupt” and “trend‑based creative,” but they’re not broken out in the public case‑study content I have access to.[12]

So under your rules:

- I can confirm **best‑practice language that strongly supports meme‑like formats** as pattern interrupts and trend‑based content.[12]  
- I **cannot** cite a 2025–2026 Meta practitioner quote directly labeling “meme statics” as a top‑performing format with quantified results.

## 6. Broader context: Meta in 2025–2026 is pushing away from static formats

This context matters when interpreting why there are few static‑specific success stories.

- Fluency’s 2026 trends/benchmark report for Google Ads and Meta (published 2026) reports that:  
  - “the number of advertisers using Meta’s Single Image ads fell by over 8%, and those using static Carousel Ads fell by 7% from 2024 to 2025.”[5]  
  - At the same time, “39% of surveyed advertisers now use Meta Collection Ads… up nearly 15% year‑over‑year.”[5]  

- Vovia’s 2026 piece says outright:  
  - “Reels consistently outperform static images.”[9]  
  - They urge advertisers to “Invest heavily in User‑Generated Content (UGC) and native‑style, short‑form videos.”[9]

Given this:

- 2025–2026 Meta discussion is largely focused on **video/Reels, UGC, and feed‑driven formats** (Collections), not on inventing new static layouts.[5][9]  
- Where static imagery is discussed, it’s mostly in the context of **AI‑assisted production** (faster, more cinematic/product‑focused images) rather than entirely new conceptual static formats.[8][15]

This likely explains why you’re seeing lots of innovation in static *style* (fake‑UI, memes, editorial AI) in the wild but very little **formal write‑up**: the big public conversation is around *video*.

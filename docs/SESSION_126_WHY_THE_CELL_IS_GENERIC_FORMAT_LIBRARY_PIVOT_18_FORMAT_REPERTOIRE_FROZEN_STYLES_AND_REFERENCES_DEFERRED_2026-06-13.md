## Part 0 — The arc in one paragraph

S125 ended on the user's question: *"why are we producing generic DR ads?"* — to be taken fresh. This session answered it **mechanically** and the user agreed: an LLM is rewarded for the **most probable** output, which by definition is the **category average = the cliché**. Generic isn't a mistake the cell makes — it's the highest-scoring answer it has. Which is also why it can't tell good from bad: *generic and good sit at the same point for it.* Every past fix (style bank, tasteful center, world-spread, critic rubrics) was an attempt to **write taste down as rules** — and a rule can guard a *floor* (forbid known-bad) but can never recognize a *new good* thing, because the new good isn't on any list. The user's own past experiment proves it: a **bad-example/cliché blocklist just produced a different cliché not on the list** — because the *entire* high-probability zone is clichés; bad is infinite, good is specific. Conclusion: **the cell cannot be *made* tasteful — taste must be *imported* as a concrete artifact, and the model's job must flip from *invent* to *adapt*** ("the model is the hands, never the eyes"; this also permanently rules out the *leap* — the improbable-but-good output is forbidden by the model's nature). From there: SMBs (our ICP) have **no brand visual world to harvest**, so *we* supply the world via a **curated FORMAT LIBRARY** — proven DR formats + concrete craft references, adapted **content-first / palette-last**. We audited the production `art-style` skill (it's a *style menu*, the wrong spine; ~5 of 14 are real formats; zero concrete references), ran fresh static-only 2026 research, found the **entire NATIVE band of formats missing** (the cold-traffic winners that *also* need no brand visual world — the SMB unlock), and **froze an 18-format, 3-tier library**. Next session: **styles + the concrete-reference layer** (the hard part).

## Part 1 — THE DIAGNOSIS: why the cell produces generic concepts (load-bearing, agreed)

This is the intellectual result of the session. It finally explains, mechanically, both the genericness AND why every prior fix failed.

1. **The model samples the most-probable output, which IS the category average = the cliché.** Ask for a whey ad → you get the most expected whey ad → the one everyone has already made. "Whey on a kitchen shelf" is not an error; it is the highest-scoring answer. Predicting the expected thing is the model's entire training objective.
2. **Therefore it cannot tell good from bad.** Taste = recognizing the cliché. But to the model the cliché *is* the correct answer — it has no internal signal that whispers "everyone's seen this." Generic and good are the same point. **You can't ask the average to notice it's average.**
3. **There are only three places to fix this: inputs, generator, judge.** We spent the whole project on the first two — inputs (research/VOC/comp) and generator (style bank, tasteful center, world-spread). Every generator fix was the same move: *write taste down as rules.* **A rule guards a floor; it can never recognize a new good thing.** You cannot rule your way to taste.
4. **The judge was never built externally.** The "independent clean-context critic" is the *same model* → *same taste ceiling* → it blesses the generic alongside the generator. We kept making the cell grade its own homework.
5. **The bad-example-bank experiment is the proof (user-supplied).** Blocklisting known clichés just made the model hop to the next-most-probable thing — another cliché not on the list. The *entire high-probability region is clichés.* You can blocklist forever; there's always another mole. **"Avoid bad" can't work — bad is infinite, good is specific.**
6. **The corrective the user forced (critical):** a judge **cannot save a generic generator** — selection can't exceed the best candidate in the set. If every candidate is a kitchen shelf, the judge crowns the least-bad shelf or rejects everything forever (token/time waste). **So the generator must produce good candidates — the diagnosis cannot end at "add a judge."**
7. **The resolution:** you do **not** make the generator tasteful (impossible — it samples the average). You **change its job from INVENT to ADAPT** — hand it *one specific proven thing to transform*. The model is genuinely bad at "think of something good from nothing" and genuinely good at "adapt this specific good thing." **Taste lives in the references + the human who curates them — never in the model. The model is the hands, never the eyes.** (Industry confirms: Sanchez says ~80% of her concepts are *adapted* from existing ads, not invented.)
8. **The leap is permanently out of reach.** If the model is rewarded for *probable*, the **leap = the improbable-but-good output = forbidden by the model's nature.** Not a setting we haven't found — a law. The cell's ceiling is **"fresh and specific," not the leap.** This finally settles S124's open "bar question" via the model's own mechanics: the leap belongs to a human in the concept seat (or brand-campaign agencies), never the generator.

**One scar carried forward (so we don't relapse):** we *did* try positive references before — the **style bank** — and it went generic too. But for a *findable* reason: it was a **fixed, shared menu**, so the menu itself became the new average. The version that holds is references pulled **fresh, per brand, from that brand's own category** (or curated craft we install) — adapting **structure**, never a stored set everyone reuses.

## Part 2 — THE DIRECTION (LOCKED): assembly via a curated FORMAT LIBRARY

- **SMBs have no brand visual world to harvest.** Most of our ICP (small/medium D2C + local) has a logo and a palette, maybe a few product shots — no developed aesthetic. So **we supply the world** via a curated library. (This killed the within-session "harvest the brand's own world" idea for most users.)
- **The library = proven DR FORMATS + concrete craft REFERENCES.** Not abstract design-principles-in-text ("Swiss grid, flat color, +50 tracking") — that is *exactly* the S125 Attempt-#1 that the user called generic, because abstract principles return the average. **Concrete real proven ads carry the craft; adjectives don't.**
- **Adapt by CONTENT first, palette LAST.** What makes brand A's testimonial ≠ brand B's is the *real product photo, the verbatim hook, the specific offer* — not the hex code. Palette is the thinnest differentiator; **palette-only adaptation rebuilds the dead style bank.**
- **This is NOT the dead style bank — it does a different, achievable job.** The bank failed at "manufacture distinctiveness/taste from a menu" (impossible). A library is genuinely good at **"install a competent, on-brand, professional FLOOR — cheaply, across every brand."** And that floor is exactly what we now want, since the leap is off the table. Right tool for the floor; wrong tool for the leap.
- **Two disciplines keep it out of the grave:** (1) the library holds **structure + craft the model FILLS, not a look it riffs on** (library = the eyes, model = the hands; the bank died asking the hands to also be the eyes); (2) **content-first/palette-last + concrete-references-not-principles.**
- **Who curates taste (it mostly dissolves):** the **market** curates concepts (proven winners via comp); the **brand** pre-curated its own content (product/words/offer); **WE** install craft-taste *once* in the library; the **founder/human** is only the *final* eye that picks the winning set. Taste is **harvested from where it already exists**, not manufactured in the model.
- **Gate semantics (unchanged from S125):** critic = floor (kills/forces fixes, never approves); vision gate = fidelity (named diffs, 1 retry); **founder set-verdict = the per-run lock**; **real spend = the only judge that locks learning.**

## Part 3 — The native↔polished spectrum + "own the middle" (visual framing)

- A cold ad's job is to **NOT look like an ad.** The brain's ad-filter fires in ~200–500ms on the *grammar* of an ad (neat margins, brand colors, product dead-center) and scrolls past before reading a word. **Native** formats (real screenshot, Reddit thread, founder's face in a real room) slip *under* the filter → 1–3 extra seconds of attention. **Polished** designed posters trip it instantly → warm-only.
- **We were parked at the polished/flat-graphic end** — the format that is generic *by construction* (design-from-principles converges to the category mean) and that AI makes *doubly* generic (great at generic-pretty graphics, worst at real product/people).
- **"Own the middle"** (user: *"owning the middle feels right"*) = real product/label **photography is the whole image**, with design shrunk to clean callouts *on top* — a figure/ground flip from "product cutout pasted into a flat designed frame." **Caveat the user surfaced and we accepted:** "own the middle" fixes *texture*, **not concept** — a real photo of whey-on-a-shelf is still a generic *concept*. The concept problem is the taste problem (Part 1), solved by adapt-don't-invent, not by better texture.

## Part 6 — THE FROZEN FORMAT LIST (the deliverable)

Ranked by one principle: **cold-traffic performance × buildability for a thin-asset SMB.** **18 formats, 3 tiers. FROZEN this session.**

**TIER 1 — Cold acquisition engine** (native + copy-driven; win cold *and* need almost no brand visual world). **Build first.**

| Format | Job | Brand supplies | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder POV ★ | real face + first-person origin → fast trust | a founder photo + real voice | NEW |
| Notes-app / iMessage | personal note; the copy *is* the creative | just copy | NEW |
| Reddit / forum screenshot | third-party thread = organic proof | real customer language | NEW |
| Meme / tweet-style | culture-native; reads as content not ad | a sharp POV / line | NEW |
| Testimonial / review screenshot | peer proof; one specific quote | a real verbatim review (+ photo) | HAVE |

**TIER 2 — Cold-to-warm workhorses** (proof + information; need a real product photo + specific content). **Build second.**

| Format | Job | Brand supplies | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem→Solution (PAS) | pain-first hook → product as exit | the exact problem sentence + product | NEW |
| Us-vs-them | control the comparison | category-specific criteria + product | HAVE |
| Listicle / top reasons | numbered benefits, easy to scan | specific benefit lines | NEW |
| What's-inside / ingredients | transparency; "what's NOT in it" | real label photo + ingredient story | NEW *(hand-built as FP-2 in S125)* |
| Product demo / callouts | show the mechanism | product photo + features to name | NEW ← *this is "own the middle"* |
| Science / mechanism | named ingredient + dose/study | a real mechanism or citation | NEW |
| Advertorial / editorial | journalism-framed pre-education | curiosity headline + 3 facts | NEW |
| Stat / quote card | one bold number or sentence | one surprising stat | HAVE *(via typography)* |
| Lifestyle / product-in-use | real scene, identity | a real lifestyle photo *(asset-heavy)* | NEW |
| Infographic | teach → authority | real data | HAVE |

**TIER 3 — Warm / retargeting** (funnel completers; need a real offer). **Build last; easy.**

| Format | Job | Brand supplies | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offer / discount card (+ urgency) | concrete deal + deadline | a real promotion | NEW |
| Bundle / value-stack | perceived-value amplification | bundle + price math | NEW |
| Hero product shot | quality signal, one perfect product | strong product pixels | HAVE |

*Flagged:* **Before/after** exists inside split-comparison — but it is **policy-dead in health/supplements**; only safe for hair / home / SaaS-dashboard / fitness-non-injury.

**Tally:** 18 formats — **5 HAVE, ~13 NEW.** Two cleanups from the old skill: "typography-dominant" was a *treatment*, not a format (it renders the stat-card); product-on-gradient + clean-premium both collapse into hero-shot.

**The shape that matters:** **Tier 1 (the native band) is the whole new thing** — it is the cold engine *and* the only tier that doesn't need a brand visual world (built from copy + a screenshot + the founder's face), which is exactly what SMBs lack. **Founder POV** is the standout: every SMB founder has a face and a real room, and the research calls it the format that "builds brand trust faster than any other static structure."

**Completeness check (honest):** covers all **20** formats from the research, mapping to the standard practitioner "periodic table of static ads" — no major bucket missing. Caveats: a few fringe variants are *folded on purpose* (creator-still → lifestyle+testimonial; quiz/"which-are-you" → niche) and **AI-surreal/reality-bending was excluded by choice** (research flagged it unproven hype). The real completeness guarantee is **comp**, not this list — the repertoire is a *prior* that comp updates per brand from live category winners. **To wire later: ensure a comp-surfaced format not in the library can still be built.**

## WAY-IN LIST

1. The scar: "I can finally have protein daily without stomach issues" — the buyer wasn't ignorant; they quit because the last one wrecked them. The trigger isn't promise, it's recognition.
2. The formula itself: whey isolate + concentrate, no artificial sweeteners, no gums — the known bloating triggers, physically absent. The label is the proof, not a claim over it.
3. The competitive gap: zero competitors lead with digestive comfort on Meta in May 2026. OZiva buries "13% better digestibility" as a bullet. The lane is open and provable with independent reviews.
4. "This is the only protein I can have with just water" — the buyer's informal gut-tolerance test, in their own language. Survives plain water = survives your stomach.
5. Three-tub repeat: the reorder is the unstageable proof. A person whose gut rejects a protein does not reorder it. "On my 3rd tub" is the verdict.

- The "tried protein, got puffy" story — they've told it, their friends have heard it, it lives as a grudge not a theory.
- The category axiom: "protein makes you bloat" is received wisdom in their friend group, passed as warning.
- The gym-bro aesthetic: black tub, dark gym, scoop mid-air. They know it. They're mildly suspicious of it.
- "Just water" as the informal quality bar — can you stomach it plain? Indian fitness circles treat this as the real test.
- The ingredient-scan habit — some of them flip the pack; they've seen guar gum and put it back.
- The steel glass: the ubiquitous Indian kitchen vessel, not a shaker bottle. It means home, morning, daily routine.
- "On my 3rd tub" — repeat-order as credibility signal in a culture where switching costs feel high and loyalty is earned slowly.

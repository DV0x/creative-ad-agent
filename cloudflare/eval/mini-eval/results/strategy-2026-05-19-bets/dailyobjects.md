# The Bet — DailyObjects
*Prepared 2026-05-19*

---

## 1. The Memo

DailyObjects has a real product, a genuine design identity, and 21,000+ people who love it enough to leave a 4.7-star review. The problem is not the brand — it is how it is being shown to strangers.

Right now you are running catalogue ads and boosted design drops. Those formats work well on people who already know you: they see a product they've browsed, they return and buy. But a cold stranger — someone who has never heard of DailyObjects and is scrolling past hundreds of phone-case ads — gets no reason to stop, no reason to trust you over the sea of cheap Amazon cases or the other "design-led" brands in their feed. Product-on-background looks like everyone else. It is warm-audience creative misapplied to a cold audience, and it cannot convert the new customers you want.

You do not know which *message* breaks through to a cold Apple user, because you have never isolated and tested one. The blended ROAS you track mixes loyal returners with genuine cold acquisition — it hides what cold strangers actually cost and which creative actually moves them.

The test we are running answers one question: what is the reason a cold, design-conscious iPhone user picks DailyObjects over everything else in their feed? We are betting three distinct answers against each other — the Indian craft story, the scale-of-trust story, and the complete Apple lifestyle story — to find the one that actually converts. The winner becomes the creative foundation of your cold-acquisition programme.

One flag that must be addressed alongside the test: your external review profile (Trustpilot and similar) carries a pattern of quality and service complaints that a cold stranger will find if they search. It will suppress conversion regardless of how good the ad is. The fix is not to delay the test — it is to work the external review profile in parallel, which is fully in your hands.

---

## 2. Conversion Event

An online purchase on dailyobjects.com by a new customer — someone with no prior purchase or branded-search relationship with DailyObjects.

*Important operating note:* your current Meta reporting watches blended ROAS. To read this test correctly, you must isolate new-customer purchases — either by using Meta's native "new customers only" campaign objective, or by tracking first-purchase events separately in your analytics. Without that split, a warm-audience sale will inflate the result and you will misread which creative is actually converting cold strangers. Set this up before spend goes live.

---

## 3. The Blocker

**Cold strangers see DailyObjects creative that was built for a warm audience.** Catalogue ads and boosted design drops give a stranger no reason to stop and no reason to trust — they look like the rest of the design-case noise in the category. A cold Apple user has no way to distinguish you from the dozen other "aesthetic" brands in their feed, and the creative never asks them to.

This is entirely creative-addressable. The product, the price, and the funnel are all viable. The gap is that you have never given a cold stranger a *specific* reason to prefer you.

## 4. The Angles

### Test sizing

Budget: ₹5,00,000/month → ~₹1,25,000/week.
Conversion product for this test: phone cases and MagSafe accessories (lower ticket, higher purchase velocity — do not anchor this test on bags, where CPA will be materially higher and event volume insufficient).
Estimated cost-per-purchase for cases: ₹500–₹800, based on published Indian D2C e-commerce benchmarks — not your own confirmed number, which is not available. At ₹600, ₹1,25,000/week buys ~208 purchases; at ₹800, ~156. Three cells at either end yields ~52–69 events per cell per week — above the ~50-event-per-week learning-phase threshold. Four cells at ₹800 drops to ~39 per cell, below threshold and unreliable.

**Verdict: three cells.** If first-week CPP runs above ₹900, consolidate to two cells immediately and shift the saved budget to the stronger two.

---

### Angle A — "Made by Indian hands"
*(the craft-origin angle)*

**Hypothesis:** A cold Apple user will stop for DailyObjects when the ad leads with *who made it* — 1,000+ Indian artisans and makers, built locally, not imported-and-labelled. This bets that a meaningful slice of design-conscious buyers is looking for something with a real story behind it, and that no competitor in this category is telling that story.

**Why the evidence supports it:** The artisan/maker claim is live on the website and documented as a genuine part of how the brand was built. It is absent from every competitor cluster in the Meta category — rivals compete on price, protection, or generic "design-led" aesthetics. This is white space. It also sidesteps the durability criticism indirectly: the story of care and craftsmanship is the opposite of a mass-produced commodity, and it selects for buyers who value it.

**Visual lane:** Warm, textured, human. Not the clean studio flat-lay — lead with a maker's hands, material texture, or a workshop close-up alongside the finished product. Earthy warm tones. The contrast with every competitor's crisp white background *is* the stopper. Use existing artisan and process photography if available; if not, a tight product macro showing stitching, grain, or material detail is the nearest alternative. Copy is sparse — one line, artisan-forward, no price.

---

### Angle B — "21,000 iPhone users already switched"
*(the social-proof / trust anchor)*

**Hypothesis:** The barrier for most cold strangers is not message fit — they can see DailyObjects is design-led. The barrier is trust. Showing the scale of existing happy customers (21,000+ ratings, 4.7 stars) in a direct, confident frame removes the "is this brand legitimate?" friction and converts the curious-but-sceptical buyer.

**Why the evidence supports it:** The brand has genuinely earned this number — 21,000+ app ratings at 4.7+ is real, substantial social proof. Cold strangers who encounter a brand without prior exposure judge it by exactly this signal. Current Meta creative (catalogue/boosted drops) buries this proof. This angle makes it the headline. It also directly counterbalances the negative external reviews a doubting stranger might find: the volume of positive signal matters.

**Visual lane:** Product-hero on a clean background — this angle earns the right to use the studio look because the words are doing something the competitors' studio shots aren't. Lead with a confident typographic treatment: the rating and count front and centre, tight and bold, with the hero product below. DailyObjects' existing brand palette and type system. No lifestyle staging — the number is the statement. One product per frame, best-seller or newest iPhone model case.

---

### Angle C — "Your whole Apple life, one brand"
*(the ecosystem / lifestyle curation angle)*

**Hypothesis:** Apple loyalists do not want a case — they want everything to match. An ad showing the full DailyObjects stack (case, MagSafe wallet, Watch band, notebook, desk organiser) styled as a single curated setup creates a desire that no single-product ad can. It bets on the buyer who buys one piece and returns for the rest — and it positions DailyObjects as the brand that has thought about the whole Apple lifestyle, not just the case category.

**Why the evidence supports it:** The product range is real and unusually broad for this category — most competitors sell cases, full stop. No competitor ad cluster in the research is running an ecosystem angle. Apple users are documented to buy deeply into product ecosystems; the "everything matches" desire is real and documented in consumer behaviour. This angle also naturally lifts AOV if it converts — a buyer who comes in for the case and sees the Watch band and wallet in one frame is primed to browse the rest.

**Visual lane:** Styled flat-lay / desk-setup, top-down or slight angle. The full DailyObjects stack arranged in a single aspirational but achievable frame — not a luxury editorial, not a bare product shot. Natural light, a real desk surface (wood, stone, or linen), neutral background. Use the brand's existing lifestyle photography as the primary asset; this format already exists in the visual library. Copy: minimal, one line, curation-forward. No price in the frame.

---

### The positioning insight beneath all three

All three angles share one underlying claim that current creative never makes explicit: *DailyObjects is the brand that takes your Apple devices seriously.* Not just pretty cases — a considered, craft-backed, socially-proven, systematically curated approach to the things you carry every day. That is the space between the cheap-case mass market and the international protection brands that no one is fully occupying.

## 5. The Honest Flag

**Non-creative ceiling — external review profile.**
The Trustpilot and independent platform reviews carry a recurring pattern: colour fading, prints peeling after water, poor after-sales service. A cold stranger who searches the brand name after seeing an ad will find this. It will suppress conversion — no creative fixes it. The 21,000+ in-app reviews are genuine leverage, but they live inside the owned app; strangers search Google and Trustpilot first.

*What to do, in parallel with the test:* (1) Run a structured outreach to verified recent buyers directing them to leave a Trustpilot or Google review — this is the fastest move. (2) Identify whether the durability complaints point to a specific SKU cohort or a process issue that can be fixed at source. This is not a reason to delay the test; it is a reason not to ignore it while the test runs.

**Measurement precondition.**
Blended ROAS cannot read this test. New-customer purchases must be tracked separately — via Meta's new customer acquisition objective or a first-purchase event in your analytics — before launch. Without this, you cannot tell what cold-stranger acquisition is actually costing, and you cannot read which angle won.

**Timing note.**
May–June 2026 is a clean discovery window: no gifting peak (Raksha Bandhan is August; festive season is October–November), no iPhone launch cycle (typically September). Run the discovery test now without event noise. When results are in hand, the winning angle can be sharpened with a gifting hook for Raksha Bandhan and a device-refresh hook for the September iPhone launch — those are tactical bursts on top of a proven creative foundation, not part of this test.

# The Bet — Arjun Infra
*Prepared: 2026-05-19*

---

## 1. The Memo

Every buyer Arjun Infra has today arrived warm — through a referral, a friend's recommendation, a hoarding they drove past. The business has never run a structured Meta campaign against cold strangers. That matters, because a cold stranger from a Meta ad is a different sport: they have no relationship with the developer, they will Google the name, and what they find right now is very little. The strategy for this first campaign is not a message test — it is an audience-discovery test. The genuinely open question is which cold audience responds to a Meta ad at all. There are two real bets. First: Ongole-origin Telugu speakers settled in Hyderabad are the most promising cold audience on the entire platform — high emotional motivation (buying back home for parents, for an eventual return, as an investment), and no competitor in this market is targeting them deliberately. Second: local Ongole buyers are actively weighing "developer gated community" against "buy a plot and manage the construction yourself" — that is the real competitive battle, not Arjun Infra vs. another builder. An ad that speaks directly to that choice is white space in this market. The ₹60,000 budget supports two cells cleanly. There is one honest precondition: the developer has no verifiable independent reviews anywhere a cold buyer would look. That is a non-creative ceiling that will cost leads downstream. Soliciting Google Reviews from past buyers — ten to fifteen real ones — should start this week, running alongside the campaign, not after it.

---

## 2. Conversion Event

A qualified enquiry or booked site visit — submitted via a Meta native lead form (preferred over website traffic, for cost and reliability reasons given the current contact setup).

---

## 3. The Blocker

The single biggest reason a genuinely-qualified stranger could encounter this brand and not convert: **there is no third-party evidence online that Arjun Infra delivers what it promises.** No Google reviews, no JustDial presence, a self-stated 4.8 rating, a personal Gmail as the contact address. For a local buyer who can ask a neighbour, this is a low hurdle. For a diaspora buyer in Hyderabad or abroad — who *cannot* ask a neighbour, cannot drive past the site, and relies on what they find online — this gap is a genuine stopper. The brand has the completed projects, the real photography, and apparently satisfied buyers; it has not yet made any of that visible where a cold stranger will look.

---

## 4. The Angles

**Budget constraint, stated plainly.** ₹60,000/month = ₹15,000/week. The research file estimates tier-3 AP Meta lead-form CPL at the lower end of the ₹300–600 range (uncrowded local market, Telugu-language targeting, native lead form). At ₹300/lead: ~50 leads/week total across both cells, ~25/week per cell. This is below Meta's formal learning-phase threshold of 50 events/week per ad set, but across a six-to-eight week window it is sufficient to read a directional result. **This budget supports two cells — not three.** If early CPL comes in at ₹200 or below, a third cell becomes viable; confirm with first two weeks of data before expanding.

---

### Cell A — The Ongole Diaspora in Hyderabad

**Hypothesis:** Ongole-origin Telugu speakers currently living in Hyderabad are the highest-intent cold audience available on Meta for this campaign. They have a concrete, emotionally-loaded reason to buy property back home — for their parents, as a hedge against eventual return, as a real-asset investment in a market they understand — and no local developer is targeting them with a deliberate message. Reaching this audience in Telugu, in Hyderabad, will produce a lower cost-per-lead and higher lead quality than a generic local-Ongole boost.

**Why, anchored in evidence:** The founder explicitly named this group ("we know a lot of buyers are people from this area who are settled in Hyderabad or abroad — but we've never targeted them deliberately"). The research file confirms this is a structural feature of tier-3 coastal-AP real estate demand — diaspora buying back home. Competitors.md shows no competitor targeting this audience on Meta; the local ad space is uncrowded. The timing is also real: May–June is when NRIs and Hyderabad-settled migrants make summer visits back to Andhra Pradesh — "site visit while I'm back in town" is a live trigger right now. The geo-concentration (Hyderabad, not a national scatter) keeps CPL lower than a broad campaign and sharpens the self-selection.

**Visual lane:** Real completed-villa photography — warm, inside-the-home, daylight. Show a finished interior: a living room, a kitchen, a view from a bedroom window. Not an elevation render; not a drone shot of the site plan. The point is to show someone in Hyderabad exactly what their family's home would look like, finished and ready. Telugu-language headline. Tone: warm, grounded, understated — *"Ongole lo mee intikosam"* territory, not aspirational-luxury-developer copy. The visual distinctiveness from competitor norm (which runs renders and RERA badges) comes from the intimacy of the shot and the language trigger.

---

### Cell B — Local Ongole: Gated Community vs. Plot and Build

**Hypothesis:** The dominant competitive frame for a local Ongole buyer is not "which developer" — it is "gated community versus buy-a-plot-and-manage-my-own-construction." An ad that directly acknowledges this choice and reframes it — positioning Arjun Infra's gated community as the answer to the stress and time cost of self-build, rather than as a generic luxury property — will out-perform the standard competitor cluster of trust badges and amenity lists.

**Why, anchored in evidence:** Competitors.md states this plainly: "The sharpest competing choice a buyer faces is 'buy a plot and build' versus 'buy into a developer's gated community' — more than one developer versus another." Every local competitor is running approvals-and-amenities messaging. No one appears to be making the "you don't have to manage construction yourself" argument. This is genuine white space in the local ad environment. The product — a finished, move-in-ready gated villa — is perfectly positioned to make this argument honestly, and the brand has real completed-project photography to prove it.

**Visual lane:** Community-level real photography — the gated entrance, the internal streets, the landscaping, the finished exteriors of occupied homes. The "completeness" visual: this community exists, it is done, people live here. Not a single hero unit as in Cell A — the community itself is the product being shown. Telugu or bilingual caption. Tone: calm, confident, matter-of-fact — "this is finished and ready." Copy carries the plot-vs-gated argument; the visual simply shows what "finished" looks like. Distinct from Cell A (which shows interior intimacy) and distinct from the competitor norm (which shows renders and badge-lists).

---

**The positioning insight underneath both cells:** Arjun Infra is for people with an Ongole connection who want to come home to something finished and cared-for — not manage a construction project from wherever they're living now. Cell A makes this literal for the diaspora. Cell B makes it comparative for the local buyer. Same honest truth, two audiences.

---

## 5. The Honest Flag

**Precondition — third-party reviews:** The thin or absent independent review presence is a real non-creative ceiling. Ads will generate interest; some fraction of leads will Google "Arjun Infra reviews" before calling, and right now they find very little. This will suppress lead-to-site-visit conversion regardless of ad quality. The fix is simple and costs nothing: contact the buyers of every completed project and ask them to leave a Google review this week. Even ten to fifteen genuine reviews transforms the brand's verifiability for a cold stranger. This should happen in parallel with campaign launch, not after.

**Unit economics gap:** The founder did not provide a ticket price or an acceptable cost-per-enquiry. Assumption this strategy is written under: *any cost-per-lead below ₹1,000 is comfortably justifiable* for a high-ticket villa or apartment purchase, even at conservative Ongole price points. If the acceptable threshold is lower than this, confirm before launch and adjust geo-targeting or lead-form design accordingly.

**Lead form over website traffic:** Given the current website's contact setup (personal mobile, Gmail), Meta native lead forms are strongly preferred over a "traffic to site" campaign. They are 30–50% cheaper per lead and do not depend on the website's ability to convert a cold visitor. Set up a simple lead form: name, phone, project of interest, preferred time for a callback.

**Timing note:** The test window (late May through mid-July) coincides with summer travel — when Hyderabad-settled and NRI diaspora visit Andhra Pradesh. Cell A's hook can explicitly reference a site visit: *"visiting Ongole this summer? Come see Uma Panoruma."* This is not a separate campaign — it is a copy angle within Cell A's creative, not a structural change to the test.

# The Bet — DailyObjects
*Prepared: 2026-05-19*

---

## 1. The Memo

DailyObjects is running Meta regularly, but almost certainly spending most of
its budget on existing demand. Catalogue ads and boosted design drops are
warm-audience tools — they harvest people who already know the brand. That
produces decent blended ROAS and zero signal on cold acquisition, which is
exactly what you said you want. There is no clean new-customer CPA anywhere
in the account. Before this test, that number doesn't exist — and you cannot
optimise what you haven't measured.

The actual job here is to prove DailyObjects can convert a cold stranger: a
design-conscious Apple user who has never heard of you, is scrolling Instagram,
and has three competing answers to the question "why pay ₹1,200 for a case?"
— Amazon at ₹300, Spigen on brand recognition, or any of a dozen D2C brands
also calling themselves "design-led." Right now, your ads answer that question
with: *nice design*. That is not an answer. Every competitor says the same
thing, and the cold stranger's thumb keeps scrolling.

Your real differentiators are two things current ads don't say. First: this
brand is made by Indian craftspeople — real hands, real workshops, real skill.
Nobody in the category is showing this. The competitor scan is unambiguous: the
artisan-provenance lane in Indian D2C tech accessories is completely uncontested
on Meta right now. Second: DailyObjects is not a case brand — it is the brand
that covers the whole Apple life. iPhone case, Watch band, MagSafe dock, desk
setup — one brand, one aesthetic, solved. No commodity seller or single-category
competitor can say that honestly.

This test runs three angles at a cold audience to find which truth moves them.
One non-negotiable setup change: run this as a separate campaign with your
existing customer and site-visitor lists excluded. Without that, you are
measuring blended performance again and learning nothing new.

---

## 2. The Conversion Event

An online purchase on dailyobjects.com — new customers only. Phone-case SKUs
are the anchor for test sizing (higher volume, faster learning); the ecosystem
angle includes adjacent SKUs naturally.

---

## 3. The Blocker

A cold, qualified stranger — a design-conscious Apple user — sees DailyObjects
ads and gets design stimulus with no trust signal: attractive products, no
reason to believe the quality justifies the premium over Amazon or Spigen. The
current ad format (product catalogue, boosted drops) asks for the purchase
before earning it, and the brand's two genuine differentiators — Indian artisan
craft and Apple-ecosystem depth — are invisible in the creative.

---

## 4. The Angles

*Test sizing: Budget ₹5,00,000/month = ~₹1,25,000/week. Market benchmark CPA
for Indian D2C phone-case purchases: ₹700–1,200 (low-confidence range — the
brand's own confirmed number is absent; this is the industry estimate).
At ₹700 CPA: ~178 purchases/week → 3 ad sets clear the ~50-event learning
threshold. At ₹1,000+: only 2 ad sets clear it. Run 3 cells; if week-1 CPA
comes in above ₹1,000, collapse to the 2 highest-performing cells immediately.
Confirm actual CPA with week-1 data — that number matters more than this
estimate. Geographic targeting: concentrate on metro and tier-1 cities
(Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai) where the Apple-user

### Angle 1 — Made Here

**Hypothesis:** A cold Apple user will convert at a meaningfully higher rate
when the ad shows *how* DailyObjects products are made — Indian craftspeople,
real materials, real process — than when it shows only the finished product.
The craft origin justifies the premium, builds trust with a stranger, and

**Why this angle:** Competitors.md is explicit: no competitor in Indian D2C
tech accessories is showing the production reality on Meta — not the people,

quickly — competitors can't credibly copy a story that isn't theirs.

**Visual lane:** Short video (15–20 seconds), craft-forward. Lead with hands —

category breadth within the Apple ecosystem, and it is being hidden behind
single-product ads. This angle tests whether unlocking the breadth message
drives the click — and the purchase — that individual catalogue ads don't.

replacing it — is what the current ads are missing. It is the most conservative

visual approach simply needed the trust layer added.

**Visual lane:** Product-on-aesthetic-background still — the category-dominant

clean negative space. But integrate social proof directly into the visual:

line as a text overlay. The format signals "native to this category"; the
proof overlay carries the message. No art-style break from DailyObjects' own
identity — work within the existing design language.

## 5. The Honest Flag

**Durability / Trustpilot (partial non-creative ceiling):** Independent review
platforms carry a recurring complaint pattern — colour fading after wash, prints
peeling on contact with water. This is product-line-specific (likely printed
cases) and is a real conversion leak if a cold buyer Googles the brand before
purchasing. Two actions required before or alongside this test:
*(a)* Weight the test creative and linked SKUs toward the brand's durability-safe
product lines — MagSafe accessories, leather/materials-forward pieces, Apple
Watch bands. Avoid leading with the printed-case SKUs that carry the most
complaints until that review record improves.
*(b)* Start building independent review coverage now — Trustpilot, Google
reviews — by prompting satisfied customers via post-purchase email. This takes
weeks, not days; it runs in parallel, not after.

**No confirmed new-customer CPA (test-sizing flag):** The test sizing above
rests on a market benchmark, not the brand's own data. Week-1 CPA is the
most important number this test will produce. If it comes in above ₹1,000:
drop to 2 cells immediately and kill the weakest early performer. Do not
run a four-week test at 3 cells on a CPA that only supports 2.

**Separate campaign, exclusions on:** Run this test as a standalone cold-
acquisition campaign with existing customer lists, site-visitor lists, and
app-user lists excluded. Without these exclusions the performance mixes warm
and cold audiences and produces a blended ROAS figure that hides, again, the
new-customer number you are actually trying to find.

**Timing note — no seasonal hook in current window:** Today is May 19, 2026.
No significant gifting or festive calendar event falls in the next 6–8 weeks.
Run these angles as evergreen cold-acquisition creative. Flag for future:
Raksha Bandhan (August 9) and the pre-festive window from late August onward
are the next high-intent gifting peaks — plan a separate, short tactical burst
for those windows once this test has a winning angle to build from. Do not
fold a seasonal hook into this discovery test; keep the two campaigns clean
and separate so results are readable.

# The Bet — Noise (Premium Tier: Luna Ring & ColorFit Pro)
*Prepared 2026-05-19*

---

## 1. The Memo

Noise has a real strategic problem, and it is not a creative problem — at least not in the ordinary sense. It leads the Indian smartwatch market by building a decade of brand equity on one idea: premium features at an accessible price. That equity is now a ceiling. When a cold Meta audience sees a Noise ad for a ₹8,000+ Luna Ring or a top-tier Pro watch, they carry a reference point — "Noise is the affordable one" — that creates friction the price cannot survive. This is not speculation; it explains directly why the premium CPA sits at ₹1,200–1,800 while affordable-watch CPA is ₹350–500 on the same platform with the same creative team.

The good news: every competitor in this category is trapped in a features-and-price-war aesthetic — gym UGC, "Was ₹X, Now ₹Y" overlays, spec carousels. That aesthetic is the affordable market talking to itself. It leaves the entire quiet, design-led, cinematic lane completely unoccupied — and that lane is exactly where the Luna Ring (Red Dot Award winner, CES 2025) and the Pro series actually belong.

The move is not to defend the Noise brand name. It is to lead with the object. An award-winning designed product earns its price before the brand name needs to do anything. The test is built around four distinct hypotheses about who that product resonates with: the professional buying status-appropriate tech; the health optimizer buying the Luna Ring for its data depth; the gifting buyer (Father's Day is four weeks out, wedding season is now); and the India-pride buyer who wants India's answer to Oura. The ₹30L/month budget runs all four cells comfortably. We will learn which of these is the real premium buyer — and, by implication, how much of the CPA gap is brand-association friction versus simply the wrong message reaching the wrong person.

---

## 2. Conversion Event

An online purchase of either the Luna Ring or a ColorFit Pro watch (premium tier) by a new cold-audience customer, completed on gonoise.com or a connected marketplace checkout.

*Note: Luna Ring and Pro watches are distinct products at different price points with likely different primary audiences. Once this test identifies which angle converts, the recommended next step is to split Luna Ring and Pro into separate campaigns and run angle variants within each. For this round, both products are in-scope.*

---

## 3. The Blocker

**Brand-equity mismatch for the premium price point.** Noise's decade of market leadership was built on affordable positioning — and cold Meta audiences who know the brand at all know it as the accessible-watch brand. That mental anchor suppresses willingness to complete a higher-consideration premium purchase. The creative gap is not production quality (Noise has a professional library) but rather that current ads lead with the Noise brand name before establishing why this particular product deserves a premium price. The product's own credentials — a Red Dot Award, CES recognition, the physical quality of the object — are underused as a permission structure for the price.

---

## 4. The Angles

**Budget sizing:**
Monthly budget ₹30,00,000 → ₹7,50,000/week. Meta learning phase requires ~50 conversion events per ad set per week. At ₹1,200 CPA: ₹60,000/week per ad set → ceiling of ~12 cells. At ₹1,800 CPA: ₹90,000/week per ad set → ceiling of ~8 cells. **This budget comfortably supports 4–5 angles even at the high CPA end.** Four angles are prescribed below — do not underwork this budget by running fewer; the learning opportunity is real.

---

### Angle 1 — The Designed Object (Status / Professional)

**Hypothesis:** A cold urban professional sees the Luna Ring or Pro watch as design-credentialed, status-appropriate tech — something that reflects where they are in life, not a budget compromise — and the Red Dot Award and CES recognition are the permission structure that makes the price feel earned.

**Why:** The category has no one speaking to professional aspiration without price-war framing. Noise has genuine, external design credentials — Red Dot Award, CES 2025 — that most cold buyers have never been shown. Leading with those credentials bypasses the "Noise = affordable" anchor by establishing product quality before the brand name needs to carry any weight. Research confirms the Pro-6 is actively positioned as "Pro for Pros" with AI features and a professional framing — but the evidence suggests this positioning is not yet reaching cold audiences in an emotionally resonant way.

**Visual lane:** Quiet and cinematic. The product on wrist or finger in an aspirational-but-understated professional context — a clean desk, the edge of a boardroom table, a still cup of coffee. No price overlays, no spec callouts. The award badge appears once, small, as a footnote to the object's quality. Dark neutral palette — near-black background or muted grey — that signals premium without shouting. Treatments stay within Noise's existing brand identity system; no new type or colour language invented.

---

### Angle 2 — Know Your Body (Health Data / Luna Ring)

**Hypothesis:** A health-aware buyer is not buying the Luna Ring as a fashion object or a status signal — they are buying continuous, passive, accurate insight into their own body: sleep quality, recovery score, HRV. The ring form factor (worn 24/7, no screen distraction) is the point. If the ad leads with the depth of data and the ring's invisible-yet-always-on nature, it competes on a dimension where no competitor can follow.

**Why:** Luna Ring's product differentiation from smartwatches is precisely the ring form factor and its continuous passive monitoring. Research confirms genuine health-tracking features. The health-data buyer is a distinct audience from the status buyer — they are not buying because it looks good; they are buying because they want to understand their body better than a wrist-worn screen allows. This is a testable hypothesis that, if it converts, also tells us that Luna Ring's primary buyer is a health optimizer — which reshapes how all future Luna Ring creative is briefed.

**Visual lane:** Clinical precision — clean, almost minimal. The ring isolated against a white or very dark background, alongside a clean render of the health-data dashboard or a single striking metric (a sleep score, an HRV curve). The aesthetic borrows from premium health-tech, not consumer electronics retail. Calm, still, deliberate. No motion, no person visible — just the object and the data it produces.

---

### Angle 3 — The Right Gift (Gifting / Occasion)

**Hypothesis:** A buyer who is not yet considering a premium wearable for themselves is already in the market for a meaningful, high-consideration gift — and a beautifully packaged, award-winning Luna Ring or Pro watch is a better answer to "what do I give someone who has everything?" than anything else they are considering.

**Why:** Two live gift occasions sit inside the test window: **Father's Day (~4 weeks out, mid-June)** and **Indian wedding season (currently peak — May through June)**. The gifting frame sidesteps the brand-equity problem entirely: the buyer is not asking "is this brand premium enough for me?" but "is this the right gift?" — a different, more solvable question. Research confirms premium margins on these products; gifting converts at higher AOVs and the occasion provides the urgency trigger Meta rewards. This is a time-sensitive angle — weight early spend toward it and reduce after mid-June.

**Visual lane:** Warm and tactile. The product in its packaging — the box partially open, the ring or watch nestled, soft directional light, sense of weight and craft. Gifting context without kitsch: no balloons, no extreme brightness — the feel of something considered rather than something celebratory. Product box and unboxing texture are central. Aspect: portrait video or still optimised for Stories and Reels feed.

---

### Angle 4 — India Built This (National Pride / Global Credential)

**Hypothesis:** An India-proud buyer — particularly a buyer who has been told "if you want premium, buy imported" — sees the Luna Ring as evidence that India is capable of producing a world-class, globally recognized designed object, and converts partly out of that conviction.

**Why:** The competitors file confirms Noise is competing with Oura-type global smart ring entrants in the premium segment. The India angle is genuinely ownable — no competitor can claim it — and Noise's Red Dot Award and CES 2025 presence are the proof points that make the pride credible rather than defensive. The research shows the affordable segment is being commoditised by unknown brands; this angle explicitly moves the conversation away from price and toward origin-quality. It is the least certain of the four angles — whether national-pride functions as a primary purchase driver for ₹X,000 wearables is a genuine unknown, which is exactly why it earns a test slot.

**Visual lane:** Confident, architectural. The product isolated on a strong, minimal background — deep navy or white — as if on a design-awards stage. No busy lifestyle context. The wordmark and the award credential anchor the bottom of the frame. One line of text only: something in the register of *"Designed in India. Recognised by the world."* The treatment feels like an international brand campaign, not a local one — that juxtaposition is the point.

---

## 5. The Honest Flag

**Brand equity is the non-creative ceiling — no ad fully removes it in one test.** The ₹1,200–1,800 CPA gap between affordable and premium is real, and a portion of it is structural: it reflects how many cold buyers hold the "Noise = affordable" mental model. The best creative test can tell us *how large* that friction is and which angle minimises it — but it cannot erase a brand association built over a decade. A sustained reduction in premium CPA will require not just winning creative but repeated exposure to the premium identity over time. If this test's winner holds CPA in the low-four-figures, that is a success; if it stays at ₹1,600+, the diagnosis points to brand repositioning as a medium-term project, not a creative-refresh problem.

**Smartwatch market contraction is real — Luna Ring is the more defensible bet.** Smartwatch shipments fell ~17.6% in 2025 and are expected to continue declining. Smart rings are a different category with different growth dynamics. If the test returns similar CPAs across both products, the strategic recommendation is to weight subsequent spend toward Luna Ring — it lives in a less contracted, less price-warred product category, and its product differentiation is harder to copy at ₹199 by a challenger brand.

**Timing precondition for Angle 3:** The gifting angle is time-sensitive. Father's Day (mid-June) is approximately four weeks from launch — weight it immediately and plan to reduce after 20 June. Wedding season runs through June; the angle stays live until then. Do not let the gifting cell run past July without refreshing for the next occasion.

**No precondition on readiness — this brand is ready to test.** The funnel converts; the CPA is high but real; the creative library is professional; the product credentials are genuine. There is no funnel-leak that would make this test un-runnable. Run it.

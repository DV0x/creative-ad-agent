# The Bet — Verbis Education
*Performance marketing strategy · Meta (Facebook/Instagram) · June 2026*

---

## Part 1 — The Memo

Verbis has been operating since 2004 and has earned something almost no competitor can claim: an independent, verifiable reputation. 490 Google reviews at 4.9★, corroborated at 4.9★ on Justdial (486 reviews) and 4.8★ on Collegedunia (365 reviews) — that is a real proof base, not a brand claim. The problem is that a cold student scrolling Instagram has no way to know it. Every study-abroad consultant in India runs the same Meta ads: scholarships, work rights, expert guidance, free counselling call. Verbis has been invisible inside that noise.

The lane every major competitor has left empty is *honest assessment*. Students in 2025–26 are frightened — US F-1 visa rejection is running at 61%, Canada study permits at 64%, UK issuances down 32% year-on-year. They have read the warnings: consultants oversell unattainable universities, push tie-up colleges, and vanish after payment. Not one live ad from IDP, Leverage Edu, or KC Overseas positions on "we'll tell you honestly if you're not a fit." Verbis's own customers — by name, in verified public reviews — say that is exactly what this brand does.

The bet is to put that honest-assessment positioning into paid ads, backed by the proof base Verbis has already earned, and test it against three distinct buyer types the founder does not yet know cold-convert from Meta: the aspiring student, the working professional, and the parent. This is an audience-discovery test — the message stays consistent (honest guidance, proven by 490 independent reviews), but the pull and the specific fear differ by buyer. The test teaches us who responds to this brand on cold traffic.

Launch by 1 July. Run 4–6 weeks. Read the result, pick the winner, and scale into the August–October September-intake peak — which is the category's highest-volume season and is beginning to wake up right now.

---

## Part 2 — Conversion Event

A cold student, professional, or parent fills the eligibility form at verbisedu.com/apply and books a free counselling call with a Verbis advisor.

---

## Part 3 — The Want and the Friction

**The want.** Indian students — and the parents and professionals who share the journey — want an honest answer about where they can realistically get in, before they commit time and significant money to a process that could end in a visa refusal and a damaged record. Across 490 independent reviews, category-level buyer research, and the founder's own account, this want is weighted and consistent. It is also the one lane no competitor is running in paid Meta ads right now.

**The friction.** A cold stranger on Instagram has no reason to believe Verbis is different from the sea of identical consultants. The category norm is overpromising; the default assumption is that every consultant is the same. The ad must do trust work in the scroll moment — specifically, it must make the honest-assessment claim *credible* by pairing it with the social proof Verbis has earned. The claim without the proof is noise. The proof without the claim is a generic star-rating. Together, they clear the blocker.

---

## Part 4 — The Angles

This is an **audience-discovery test**: three distinct buyer hypotheses, each with an honest-assessment + social-proof message shaped to that buyer's specific fear. The founder does not know which buyer cold-converts from Meta; this test answers that question.

**Test sizing note (confirm before spend).** No monthly budget was provided at intake; the sizing below is conditional. Meta's learning phase requires approximately 50 conversion events per week per ad set to return readable signal. Using the researched Indian education CPL band of ₹300–₹600 per booked call (a planning estimate, not the founder's own data — confirm actual CPL with the team before launch), each ad set needs roughly ₹60,000–₹1,20,000 per month to read clearly. **Recommended cells by budget:**
- ₹1,00,000/month or below → run Angle 1 only.
- ₹1,50,000–₹2,00,000/month → run Angles 1 + 2.
- ₹2,00,000/month and above → run all three.

If CPL runs toward the low end of the range (well-optimised creative, Bengaluru-and-metro geo rather than fully national), all three cells become viable at ₹1,50,000+. Concentrating spend on Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Pune, and Delhi-NCR — rather than running nationally at launch — lowers CPL, tightens each cell's signal, and matches the cities where aspirational engineering and postgrad applicants cluster.

---

### Angle 1 — The Aspiring Student  *(primary)*

**Buyer** · Final-year undergraduate or recent graduate (18–23) planning their first postgrad application abroad (UK, Canada, US, Australia). Solution-aware: knows consultants exist, uncertain which one to trust. The desire being amplified: to know exactly which universities they are genuinely eligible for — to have a specific, honest plan rather than months of anxious guessing and a wasted application cycle.

**Promise** · The free call gives this student a specific, honest picture of which universities and visa pathways actually match their profile — not an optimistic shortlist built around what the consultant is incentivised to sell.

**Proof (only this)** · 4.9★ across 490 independent Google reviews (browser-verified, 2026-06-17); corroborated 4.9★ Justdial (486 reviews), 4.8★ Collegedunia (365 reviews; Estd 2004). Review topic-tags from Google quantify praise themes: *visa application (43 mentions), confidence building (59 mentions), OET/test preparation (110 mentions)*. Named advisor **Partha** praised by name for multi-stage guidance in multiple reviews — verbatim: *"Partha has been guiding/assisting us in all the stages of this process… shortlist the universities, application process…"* (Mahesh Ns, Google, ★★★★★, ~1 week ago). End-to-end arc confirmed by G. Bhavana: *"From IELTS coaching to university selection and visa approval, I received excellent guidance at every stage."* Founder's honest-assessment claim corroborated across multiple reviews. No verified visa success % is available; do not state one.

**Mandatories** · Show the real named advisor (Partha or Vinutha) — personal face is the proof of "individual attention," not a stock image of a happy student. Feature the 4.9★/490 review count visibly — this is the trust anchor that earns the click; do not bury it. Do not frame the ad around rejection or profile failure; the promise is *clarity and a realistic plan*, not *we'll tell you you can't go*. Do not state a specific visa success percentage (unverified). Stay within Verbis's existing verbal identity: honest, end-to-end, personal.

---

### Angle 2 — The Working Professional  *(secondary)*

**Buyer** · Working professional (25–32) considering a master's abroad for career advancement or a mid-career pivot. Problem-aware: knows they want a change, uncertain whether a foreign master's is the right vehicle and whether their profile can realistically get them there. The desire being amplified: an honest, professional-grade assessment of whether their specific background, target programs, and goals actually align — before they commit months of preparation and significant savings to a process that may not fit them.

**Promise** · The free call delivers a frank assessment of whether their profile is a genuine match for the programs and countries they're considering — not a sales conversation dressed as career advice.

**Proof (only this)** · Same verified review base: 4.9★ / 490 Google, 4.9★ / 486 Justdial, 4.8★ / 365 Collegedunia. Mahesh Ns review (master's planning, recent) directly corroborates this buyer's journey: *"We took help from Verbis Edu for masters education abroad. Partha has been guiding/assisting us in all the stages…"* The review corpus spans IELTS coaching, university shortlisting, application, and visa — the full arc a professional applicant needs covered. Named advisor Partha is the relationship anchor. No job placement partnerships are verified; do not imply post-graduation employment guarantees.

**Mandatories** · The self-selection trigger must specifically address working professionals or career-changers, not generic "students" — the buyer will not see themselves in a student-framed ad. Show named advisor Partha (a professional buyer trusts a specific expert more than a brand logo). Do not claim salary outcomes, job placement rates, or post-study work rights that are unverified for Verbis specifically. Do not reference test-prep coaching as the hook — this buyer's primary anxiety is profile and program fit, not IELTS scores. Feature 4.9★/490 reviews as trust anchor.

### Angle 3 — The Parent  *(conditional: run if budget supports three cells)*

**Buyer** · Parent (38–52) of a student planning to study abroad — typically the financial decision-maker or co-driver. Acutely anxious about financial risk (visa rejection = lakhs wasted, not just time lost) and about being led into applications that sound good but fail. The desire being amplified: to protect the family's investment by ensuring the plan is realistic, properly supported through visa, and not built on false hope.

**Promise** · Before any application fees or visa costs are committed, the family gets an honest account of what the student's profile actually supports — so money goes into applications that have a real chance, not aspirational ones that damage the record and waste the budget.

**Proof (only this)** · 4.9★ / 490 Google reviews; 4.9★ / 486 Justdial; 4.8★ / 365 Collegedunia — a volume of independent proof that a parent researching will find and trust. Review language uses the language of families, not just individual students ("we" framing in Mahesh Ns review). Visa approval guidance is a documented review topic (43 Google mentions, and G. Bhavana's review references visa approval). Founder's honesty claim ("we're honest about where they can actually get in, instead of overpromising") is the exact reassurance a financially-exposed parent wants. No specific visa success % is verified; do not state one.

**Mandatories** · The CTA must make the booking path clear for a parent: state explicitly that the parent can book and bring their student, or that the call is for the family. Do not frame around "your child might fail" — frame around protecting an investment and building a realistic plan. Feature 4.9★/490 reviews prominently; for a parent, institutional proof matters more than lifestyle aspiration. Do not claim scholarship amounts or financial guarantees Verbis has not verified. Named advisor visible in creative — trust for a parent is personal accountability, not brand authority. Stay within Verbis's existing verbal identity.

---

## Part 5 — The Honest Flag

**Landing page quality — confirm before any spend.** The /apply page is the point where the ad's trust arc either completes or collapses. It must surface: (1) the 4.9★/490 review count above the fold, (2) Partha's name and photo with a brief bio, (3) a short, low-friction eligibility form (ask only what is needed to qualify the lead — not a 15-field form). A polished ad pointed at a landing page that looks generic and carries no social proof is money burned. Audit and fix this before the campaign runs.

**Visa success rate — honesty-ceiling precondition.** The founder cites "really high visa success rate" as a differentiator. This is potentially the single most powerful claim in the category — competitors state nothing; Verbis could state a verified %. But "really high" is not a claimable figure. Until the founder pulls and documents the actual rate from team data, no ad may state a specific percentage. The angles above use the review corpus (which mentions visa approvals organically) in its place. The moment a verified rate is confirmed and documented, add it to Angle 1 and Angle 3 — it will sharpen both significantly.

**Advisor and team photos — required asset.** The personal-advisor proof is the creative engine of all three angles. The cell cannot execute the trust work without real photos of Partha (and ideally Vinutha). Founder should supply these before briefing the cell.

**Brand visual assets.** Verbis's visual identity is not recoverable from standard web fetch (JS-rendered SPA). The creative cell needs at minimum: logo file, primary colour hex(s), any existing brand photography. Without these, the cell builds from scratch — which is possible, but risks producing creative that clashes with the brand's existing look. Supply the asset kit alongside the brief.

**Budget — confirm and size accordingly.** See sizing note in Part 4. The two numbers needed: actual monthly ad budget, and actual average CPL from any prior Meta activity (if the team has it). These determine how many cells are live and whether geography should be narrowed to Bengaluru + metro tech hubs to improve signal quality.

**Timing.** Launch by 1 July to allow 4–6 weeks of signal before the August intake-research peak. Do not run the discovery test and a seasonal burst simultaneously — keep them in separate campaigns so the September-intake hooks do not corrupt the audience-discovery read.

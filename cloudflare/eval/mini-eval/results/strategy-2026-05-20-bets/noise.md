# The Bet — Noise (Premium Tier: Luna Ring & ColorFit Pro)
*Prepared 2026-05-19*

---

## 1. The Memo

Noise has a real strategic problem, and it is not a creative problem — at least not in the ordinary sense. It leads the Indian smartwatch market by building a decade of brand equity on one idea: premium features at an accessible price. That equity is now a ceiling. When a cold Meta audience sees a Noise ad for a ₹8,000+ Luna Ring or a top-tier Pro watch, they carry a reference point — "Noise is the affordable one" — that creates friction the price cannot survive. This is not speculation; it explains directly why the premium CPA sits at ₹1,200–1,800 while affordable-watch CPA is ₹350–500 on the same platform with the same creative team.

The good news: every competitor in this category is trapped in a features-and-price-war aesthetic — gym UGC, "Was ₹X, Now ₹Y" overlays, spec carousels. That aesthetic is the affordable market talking to itself. It leaves the entire quiet, design-led, cinematic lane completely unoccupied — and that lane is exactly where the Luna Ring (Red Dot Award winner, CES 2025) and the Pro series actually belong.

The move is not to defend the Noise brand name. It is to lead with the object. An award-winning designed product earns its price before the brand name needs to do anything. The test is built around four distinct hypotheses about who that product resonates with: the professional buying status-appropriate tech; the health optimizer buying the Luna Ring for its data depth; the gifting buyer (Father's Day is four weeks out, wedding season is now); and the India-pride buyer who wants India's answer to Oura. The ₹30L/month budget runs all four cells comfortably. We will learn which of these is the real premium buyer — and, by implication, how much of the CPA gap is brand-association friction versus simply the wrong message reaching the wrong person.

---

## 2. Conversion Event

An online purchase of either the Luna Ring or a ColorFit Pro watch (premium tier) by a new cold-audience customer, completed on gonoise.com or a connected marketplace checkout.

*Note: Luna Ring and Pro watches are distinct products at different price points with likely different primary audiences. Once this test identifies which angle converts, the recommended next step is to split Luna Ring and Pro into separate campaigns and run angle variants within each. For this round, both products are in-scope.*

---

## 3. The Blocker

**Brand-equity mismatch for the premium price point.** Noise's decade of market leadership was built on affordable positioning — and cold Meta audiences who know the brand at all know it as the accessible-watch brand. That mental anchor suppresses willingness to complete a higher-consideration premium purchase. The creative gap is not production quality (Noise has a professional library) but rather that current ads lead with the Noise brand name before establishing why this particular product deserves a premium price. The product's own credentials — a Red Dot Award, CES recognition, the physical quality of the object — are underused as a permission structure for the price.

---

## 4. The Angles

**Budget sizing:**
Monthly budget ₹30,00,000 → ₹7,50,000/week. Meta learning phase requires ~50 conversion events per ad set per week. At ₹1,200 CPA: ₹60,000/week per ad set → ceiling of ~12 cells. At ₹1,800 CPA: ₹90,000/week per ad set → ceiling of ~8 cells. **This budget comfortably supports 4–5 angles even at the high CPA end.** Four angles are prescribed below — do not underwork this budget by running fewer; the learning opportunity is real.

---

### Angle 1 — The Designed Object (Status / Professional)

**Hypothesis:** A cold urban professional sees the Luna Ring or Pro watch as design-credentialed, status-appropriate tech — something that reflects where they are in life, not a budget compromise — and the Red Dot Award and CES recognition are the permission structure that makes the price feel earned.

**Why:** The category has no one speaking to professional aspiration without price-war framing. Noise has genuine, external design credentials — Red Dot Award, CES 2025 — that most cold buyers have never been shown. Leading with those credentials bypasses the "Noise = affordable" anchor by establishing product quality before the brand name needs to carry any weight. Research confirms the Pro-6 is actively positioned as "Pro for Pros" with AI features and a professional framing — but the evidence suggests this positioning is not yet reaching cold audiences in an emotionally resonant way.

**Visual lane:** Quiet and cinematic. The product on wrist or finger in an aspirational-but-understated professional context — a clean desk, the edge of a boardroom table, a still cup of coffee. No price overlays, no spec callouts. The award badge appears once, small, as a footnote to the object's quality. Dark neutral palette — near-black background or muted grey — that signals premium without shouting. Treatments stay within Noise's existing brand identity system; no new type or colour language invented.

---

### Angle 2 — Know Your Body (Health Data / Luna Ring)

**Hypothesis:** A health-aware buyer is not buying the Luna Ring as a fashion object or a status signal — they are buying continuous, passive, accurate insight into their own body: sleep quality, recovery score, HRV. The ring form factor (worn 24/7, no screen distraction) is the point. If the ad leads with the depth of data and the ring's invisible-yet-always-on nature, it competes on a dimension where no competitor can follow.

**Why:** Luna Ring's product differentiation from smartwatches is precisely the ring form factor and its continuous passive monitoring. Research confirms genuine health-tracking features. The health-data buyer is a distinct audience from the status buyer — they are not buying because it looks good; they are buying because they want to understand their body better than a wrist-worn screen allows. This is a testable hypothesis that, if it converts, also tells us that Luna Ring's primary buyer is a health optimizer — which reshapes how all future Luna Ring creative is briefed.

**Visual lane:** Clinical precision — clean, almost minimal. The ring isolated against a white or very dark background, alongside a clean render of the health-data dashboard or a single striking metric (a sleep score, an HRV curve). The aesthetic borrows from premium health-tech, not consumer electronics retail. Calm, still, deliberate. No motion, no person visible — just the object and the data it produces.

---

### Angle 3 — The Right Gift (Gifting / Occasion)

**Hypothesis:** A buyer who is not yet considering a premium wearable for themselves is already in the market for a meaningful, high-consideration gift — and a beautifully packaged, award-winning Luna Ring or Pro watch is a better answer to "what do I give someone who has everything?" than anything else they are considering.

**Why:** Two live gift occasions sit inside the test window: **Father's Day (~4 weeks out, mid-June)** and **Indian wedding season (currently peak — May through June)**. The gifting frame sidesteps the brand-equity problem entirely: the buyer is not asking "is this brand premium enough for me?" but "is this the right gift?" — a different, more solvable question. Research confirms premium margins on these products; gifting converts at higher AOVs and the occasion provides the urgency trigger Meta rewards. This is a time-sensitive angle — weight early spend toward it and reduce after mid-June.

**Visual lane:** Warm and tactile. The product in its packaging — the box partially open, the ring or watch nestled, soft directional light, sense of weight and craft. Gifting context without kitsch: no balloons, no extreme brightness — the feel of something considered rather than something celebratory. Product box and unboxing texture are central. Aspect: portrait video or still optimised for Stories and Reels feed.

---

### Angle 4 — India Built This (National Pride / Global Credential)

**Hypothesis:** An India-proud buyer — particularly a buyer who has been told "if you want premium, buy imported" — sees the Luna Ring as evidence that India is capable of producing a world-class, globally recognized designed object, and converts partly out of that conviction.

**Why:** The competitors file confirms Noise is competing with Oura-type global smart ring entrants in the premium segment. The India angle is genuinely ownable — no competitor can claim it — and Noise's Red Dot Award and CES 2025 presence are the proof points that make the pride credible rather than defensive. The research shows the affordable segment is being commoditised by unknown brands; this angle explicitly moves the conversation away from price and toward origin-quality. It is the least certain of the four angles — whether national-pride functions as a primary purchase driver for ₹X,000 wearables is a genuine unknown, which is exactly why it earns a test slot.

**Visual lane:** Confident, architectural. The product isolated on a strong, minimal background — deep navy or white — as if on a design-awards stage. No busy lifestyle context. The wordmark and the award credential anchor the bottom of the frame. One line of text only: something in the register of *"Designed in India. Recognised by the world."* The treatment feels like an international brand campaign, not a local one — that juxtaposition is the point.

---

## 5. The Honest Flag

**Brand equity is the non-creative ceiling — no ad fully removes it in one test.** The ₹1,200–1,800 CPA gap between affordable and premium is real, and a portion of it is structural: it reflects how many cold buyers hold the "Noise = affordable" mental model. The best creative test can tell us *how large* that friction is and which angle minimises it — but it cannot erase a brand association built over a decade. A sustained reduction in premium CPA will require not just winning creative but repeated exposure to the premium identity over time. If this test's winner holds CPA in the low-four-figures, that is a success; if it stays at ₹1,600+, the diagnosis points to brand repositioning as a medium-term project, not a creative-refresh problem.

**Smartwatch market contraction is real — Luna Ring is the more defensible bet.** Smartwatch shipments fell ~17.6% in 2025 and are expected to continue declining. Smart rings are a different category with different growth dynamics. If the test returns similar CPAs across both products, the strategic recommendation is to weight subsequent spend toward Luna Ring — it lives in a less contracted, less price-warred product category, and its product differentiation is harder to copy at ₹199 by a challenger brand.

**Timing precondition for Angle 3:** The gifting angle is time-sensitive. Father's Day (mid-June) is approximately four weeks from launch — weight it immediately and plan to reduce after 20 June. Wedding season runs through June; the angle stays live until then. Do not let the gifting cell run past July without refreshing for the next occasion.

**No precondition on readiness — this brand is ready to test.** The funnel converts; the CPA is high but real; the creative library is professional; the product credentials are genuine. There is no funnel-leak that would make this test un-runnable. Run it.

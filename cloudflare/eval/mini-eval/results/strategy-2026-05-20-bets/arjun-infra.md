# The Bet — Arjun Infra
*Prepared: 2026-05-19*

---

## 1. The Memo

Every buyer Arjun Infra has today arrived warm — through a referral, a friend's recommendation, a hoarding they drove past. The business has never run a structured Meta campaign against cold strangers. That matters, because a cold stranger from a Meta ad is a different sport: they have no relationship with the developer, they will Google the name, and what they find right now is very little. The strategy for this first campaign is not a message test — it is an audience-discovery test. The genuinely open question is which cold audience responds to a Meta ad at all. There are two real bets. First: Ongole-origin Telugu speakers settled in Hyderabad are the most promising cold audience on the entire platform — high emotional motivation (buying back home for parents, for an eventual return, as an investment), and no competitor in this market is targeting them deliberately. Second: local Ongole buyers are actively weighing "developer gated community" against "buy a plot and manage the construction yourself" — that is the real competitive battle, not Arjun Infra vs. another builder. An ad that speaks directly to that choice is white space in this market. The ₹60,000 budget supports two cells cleanly. There is one honest precondition: the developer has no verifiable independent reviews anywhere a cold buyer would look. That is a non-creative ceiling that will cost leads downstream. Soliciting Google Reviews from past buyers — ten to fifteen real ones — should start this week, running alongside the campaign, not after it.

---

## 2. Conversion Event

A qualified enquiry or booked site visit — submitted via a Meta native lead form (preferred over website traffic, for cost and reliability reasons given the current contact setup).

---

## 3. The Blocker

The single biggest reason a genuinely-qualified stranger could encounter this brand and not convert: **there is no third-party evidence online that Arjun Infra delivers what it promises.** No Google reviews, no JustDial presence, a self-stated 4.8 rating, a personal Gmail as the contact address. For a local buyer who can ask a neighbour, this is a low hurdle. For a diaspora buyer in Hyderabad or abroad — who *cannot* ask a neighbour, cannot drive past the site, and relies on what they find online — this gap is a genuine stopper. The brand has the completed projects, the real photography, and apparently satisfied buyers; it has not yet made any of that visible where a cold stranger will look.

---

## 4. The Angles

**Budget constraint, stated plainly.** ₹60,000/month = ₹15,000/week. The research file estimates tier-3 AP Meta lead-form CPL at the lower end of the ₹300–600 range (uncrowded local market, Telugu-language targeting, native lead form). At ₹300/lead: ~50 leads/week total across both cells, ~25/week per cell. This is below Meta's formal learning-phase threshold of 50 events/week per ad set, but across a six-to-eight week window it is sufficient to read a directional result. **This budget supports two cells — not three.** If early CPL comes in at ₹200 or below, a third cell becomes viable; confirm with first two weeks of data before expanding.

---

### Cell A — The Ongole Diaspora in Hyderabad

**Hypothesis:** Ongole-origin Telugu speakers currently living in Hyderabad are the highest-intent cold audience available on Meta for this campaign. They have a concrete, emotionally-loaded reason to buy property back home — for their parents, as a hedge against eventual return, as a real-asset investment in a market they understand — and no local developer is targeting them with a deliberate message. Reaching this audience in Telugu, in Hyderabad, will produce a lower cost-per-lead and higher lead quality than a generic local-Ongole boost.

**Why, anchored in evidence:** The founder explicitly named this group ("we know a lot of buyers are people from this area who are settled in Hyderabad or abroad — but we've never targeted them deliberately"). The research file confirms this is a structural feature of tier-3 coastal-AP real estate demand — diaspora buying back home. Competitors.md shows no competitor targeting this audience on Meta; the local ad space is uncrowded. The timing is also real: May–June is when NRIs and Hyderabad-settled migrants make summer visits back to Andhra Pradesh — "site visit while I'm back in town" is a live trigger right now. The geo-concentration (Hyderabad, not a national scatter) keeps CPL lower than a broad campaign and sharpens the self-selection.

**Visual lane:** Real completed-villa photography — warm, inside-the-home, daylight. Show a finished interior: a living room, a kitchen, a view from a bedroom window. Not an elevation render; not a drone shot of the site plan. The point is to show someone in Hyderabad exactly what their family's home would look like, finished and ready. Telugu-language headline. Tone: warm, grounded, understated — *"Ongole lo mee intikosam"* territory, not aspirational-luxury-developer copy. The visual distinctiveness from competitor norm (which runs renders and RERA badges) comes from the intimacy of the shot and the language trigger.

---

### Cell B — Local Ongole: Gated Community vs. Plot and Build

**Hypothesis:** The dominant competitive frame for a local Ongole buyer is not "which developer" — it is "gated community versus buy-a-plot-and-manage-my-own-construction." An ad that directly acknowledges this choice and reframes it — positioning Arjun Infra's gated community as the answer to the stress and time cost of self-build, rather than as a generic luxury property — will out-perform the standard competitor cluster of trust badges and amenity lists.

**Why, anchored in evidence:** Competitors.md states this plainly: "The sharpest competing choice a buyer faces is 'buy a plot and build' versus 'buy into a developer's gated community' — more than one developer versus another." Every local competitor is running approvals-and-amenities messaging. No one appears to be making the "you don't have to manage construction yourself" argument. This is genuine white space in the local ad environment. The product — a finished, move-in-ready gated villa — is perfectly positioned to make this argument honestly, and the brand has real completed-project photography to prove it.

**Visual lane:** Community-level real photography — the gated entrance, the internal streets, the landscaping, the finished exteriors of occupied homes. The "completeness" visual: this community exists, it is done, people live here. Not a single hero unit as in Cell A — the community itself is the product being shown. Telugu or bilingual caption. Tone: calm, confident, matter-of-fact — "this is finished and ready." Copy carries the plot-vs-gated argument; the visual simply shows what "finished" looks like. Distinct from Cell A (which shows interior intimacy) and distinct from the competitor norm (which shows renders and badge-lists).

---

**The positioning insight underneath both cells:** Arjun Infra is for people with an Ongole connection who want to come home to something finished and cared-for — not manage a construction project from wherever they're living now. Cell A makes this literal for the diaspora. Cell B makes it comparative for the local buyer. Same honest truth, two audiences.

---

## 5. The Honest Flag

**Precondition — third-party reviews:** The thin or absent independent review presence is a real non-creative ceiling. Ads will generate interest; some fraction of leads will Google "Arjun Infra reviews" before calling, and right now they find very little. This will suppress lead-to-site-visit conversion regardless of ad quality. The fix is simple and costs nothing: contact the buyers of every completed project and ask them to leave a Google review this week. Even ten to fifteen genuine reviews transforms the brand's verifiability for a cold stranger. This should happen in parallel with campaign launch, not after.

**Unit economics gap:** The founder did not provide a ticket price or an acceptable cost-per-enquiry. Assumption this strategy is written under: *any cost-per-lead below ₹1,000 is comfortably justifiable* for a high-ticket villa or apartment purchase, even at conservative Ongole price points. If the acceptable threshold is lower than this, confirm before launch and adjust geo-targeting or lead-form design accordingly.

**Lead form over website traffic:** Given the current website's contact setup (personal mobile, Gmail), Meta native lead forms are strongly preferred over a "traffic to site" campaign. They are 30–50% cheaper per lead and do not depend on the website's ability to convert a cold visitor. Set up a simple lead form: name, phone, project of interest, preferred time for a callback.

**Timing note:** The test window (late May through mid-July) coincides with summer travel — when Hyderabad-settled and NRI diaspora visit Andhra Pradesh. Cell A's hook can explicitly reference a site visit: *"visiting Ongole this summer? Come see Uma Panoruma."* This is not a separate campaign — it is a copy angle within Cell A's creative, not a structural change to the test.

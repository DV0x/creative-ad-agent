# The Bet — DailyObjects
*Prepared: 2026-05-19*

---

## 1. The Memo

DailyObjects is running Meta regularly, but almost certainly spending most of
its budget on existing demand. Catalogue ads and boosted design drops are
warm-audience tools — they harvest people who already know the brand. That
produces decent blended ROAS and zero signal on cold acquisition, which is
exactly what you said you want. There is no clean new-customer CPA anywhere
in the account. Before this test, that number doesn't exist — and you cannot
optimise what you haven't measured.

The actual job here is to prove DailyObjects can convert a cold stranger: a
design-conscious Apple user who has never heard of you, is scrolling Instagram,
and has three competing answers to the question "why pay ₹1,200 for a case?"
— Amazon at ₹300, Spigen on brand recognition, or any of a dozen D2C brands
also calling themselves "design-led." Right now, your ads answer that question
with: *nice design*. That is not an answer. Every competitor says the same
thing, and the cold stranger's thumb keeps scrolling.

Your real differentiators are two things current ads don't say. First: this
brand is made by Indian craftspeople — real hands, real workshops, real skill.
Nobody in the category is showing this. The competitor scan is unambiguous: the
artisan-provenance lane in Indian D2C tech accessories is completely uncontested
on Meta right now. Second: DailyObjects is not a case brand — it is the brand
that covers the whole Apple life. iPhone case, Watch band, MagSafe dock, desk
setup — one brand, one aesthetic, solved. No commodity seller or single-category
competitor can say that honestly.

This test runs three angles at a cold audience to find which truth moves them.
One non-negotiable setup change: run this as a separate campaign with your
existing customer and site-visitor lists excluded. Without that, you are
measuring blended performance again and learning nothing new.

## 2. The Conversion Event

An online purchase on dailyobjects.com — new customers only. Phone-case SKUs
are the anchor for test sizing (higher volume, faster learning); the ecosystem
angle includes adjacent SKUs naturally.

---

## 3. The Blocker

A cold, qualified stranger — a design-conscious Apple user — sees DailyObjects
ads and gets design stimulus with no trust signal: attractive products, no
reason to believe the quality justifies the premium over Amazon or Spigen. The
current ad format (product catalogue, boosted drops) asks for the purchase
before earning it, and the brand's two genuine differentiators — Indian artisan
craft and Apple-ecosystem depth — are invisible in the creative.

---

## 4. The Angles

*Test sizing: Budget ₹5,00,000/month = ~₹1,25,000/week. Market benchmark CPA
for Indian D2C phone-case purchases: ₹700–1,200 (low-confidence range — the
brand's own confirmed number is absent; this is the industry estimate).
At ₹700 CPA: ~178 purchases/week → 3 ad sets clear the ~50-event learning
threshold. At ₹1,000+: only 2 ad sets clear it. Run 3 cells; if week-1 CPA
comes in above ₹1,000, collapse to the 2 highest-performing cells immediately.
Confirm actual CPA with week-1 data — that number matters more than this
estimate. Geographic targeting: concentrate on metro and tier-1 cities
(Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai) where the Apple-user
density is real and the CPA will be more efficient than a broad national buy.*

---

### Angle 1 — Made Here

**Hypothesis:** A cold Apple user will convert at a meaningfully higher rate
when the ad shows *how* DailyObjects products are made — Indian craftspeople,
real materials, real process — than when it shows only the finished product.
The craft origin justifies the premium, builds trust with a stranger, and
answers the "why not Amazon?" objection in a single frame.

**Why this angle:** Competitors.md is explicit: no competitor in Indian D2C
tech accessories is showing the production reality on Meta — not the people,
not the hands, not the workshops. This is confirmed white space in the
category's feed, directly aligned with the brand's own claimed identity
("crafted with care by 1000+ Indian artisans"). The self-description is real;
the ads don't say it. That gap is the opportunity. Of all three angles, this
one has the highest asymmetric upside and the lowest risk of being matched
quickly — competitors can't credibly copy a story that isn't theirs.

**Visual lane:** Short video (15–20 seconds), craft-forward. Lead with hands —
fingers working leather or material, close-up of stitching or edge finishing,
the workshop in the background. The finished product appears at the end as the
*outcome* of the human story, not the hero from frame one. Tone: warm, intimate,
unhurried — the opposite of the jumpcut UGC reels now crowding the category.
No voiceover; text overlay is enough. This is a contrast move against the
dominant visual language in the category feed, not a match of it.

---

### Angle 2 — Your Whole Apple Life, Sorted

**Hypothesis:** Cold buyers who are invested in the Apple ecosystem will
convert better when shown DailyObjects as the single brand that sweats the
details of their *whole* setup — iPhone case, Apple Watch band, MagSafe, desk
organiser — than when shown any single product. Ecosystem breadth is a real
differentiator no commodity seller or single-category brand can match; current
single-product catalogue ads erase it entirely.

**Why this angle:** The product range is genuine — iPhone cases, MagSafe
accessories, Apple Watch bands, bags, desk organisers. No cheap-case brand
covers this. Spigen doesn't do lifestyle. Amazon commodity sellers don't do
watch bands that match your case. The brand's true competitive advantage is
category breadth within the Apple ecosystem, and it is being hidden behind
single-product ads. This angle tests whether unlocking the breadth message
drives the click — and the purchase — that individual catalogue ads don't.

**Visual lane:** High-polish still, lifestyle desk setup — iPhone in
DailyObjects case, Apple Watch with DailyObjects band, MagSafe charger, laptop,
styled together on a clean desk in warm light. Products styled together, not
individually. Fits directly within DailyObjects' existing strong visual identity
(minimalist, design-forward) so no identity break. The ecosystem arrangement
is itself the message — the ad does not need to say "we cover everything";
the image shows it. Match the category's dominant product-on-aesthetic-background
visual language, but differentiate on what the image *contains*.

---

### Angle 3 — What 21,000 Users Already Know

**Hypothesis:** A cold skeptic — someone drawn by the design but held back by
"why should I trust this brand?" — converts when shown peer proof alongside
the design. Social proof changes the internal question from "is this worth
it?" to "am I the last to find out?" The brand has 21,000+ app users at 4.7
stars; this is real and it is currently unused in acquisition creative.

**Why this angle:** The brand's Meta ads today show designs without proof.
That is structurally the same as a brand a cold stranger has never heard of
asking for a ₹1,000+ purchase on faith. The 4.7 / 21,000+ figure is the
closest thing to independent validation the brand has that is unambiguously
positive — the Trustpilot situation is mixed, but the app rating is not. This
angle tests whether adding proof to the existing design message — rather than
replacing it — is what the current ads are missing. It is the most conservative
of the three cells and the one closest to current activity; it earns its slot
because the founder has no baseline and needs to know whether the existing
visual approach simply needed the trust layer added.

**Visual lane:** Product-on-aesthetic-background still — the category-dominant
format, familiar and native to the feed. Phone case on marble or desk surface,
clean negative space. But integrate social proof directly into the visual:
a clean callout ("4.7 stars · 21,000+ customers") or a single real review
line as a text overlay. The format signals "native to this category"; the
proof overlay carries the message. No art-style break from DailyObjects' own
identity — work within the existing design language.

## 5. The Honest Flag

**Durability / Trustpilot (partial non-creative ceiling):** Independent review
platforms carry a recurring complaint pattern — colour fading after wash, prints
peeling on contact with water. This is product-line-specific (likely printed
cases) and is a real conversion leak if a cold buyer Googles the brand before
purchasing. Two actions required before or alongside this test:
*(a)* Weight the test creative and linked SKUs toward the brand's durability-safe
product lines — MagSafe accessories, leather/materials-forward pieces, Apple
Watch bands. Avoid leading with the printed-case SKUs that carry the most
complaints until that review record improves.
*(b)* Start building independent review coverage now — Trustpilot, Google
reviews — by prompting satisfied customers via post-purchase email. This takes
weeks, not days; it runs in parallel, not after.

**No confirmed new-customer CPA (test-sizing flag):** The test sizing above
rests on a market benchmark, not the brand's own data. Week-1 CPA is the
most important number this test will produce. If it comes in above ₹1,000:
drop to 2 cells immediately and kill the weakest early performer. Do not
run a four-week test at 3 cells on a CPA that only supports 2.

**Separate campaign, exclusions on:** Run this test as a standalone cold-
acquisition campaign with existing customer lists, site-visitor lists, and
app-user lists excluded. Without these exclusions the performance mixes warm
and cold audiences and produces a blended ROAS figure that hides, again, the
new-customer number you are actually trying to find.

**Timing note — no seasonal hook in current window:** Today is May 19, 2026.
No significant gifting or festive calendar event falls in the next 6–8 weeks.
Run these angles as evergreen cold-acquisition creative. Flag for future:
Raksha Bandhan (August 9) and the pre-festive window from late August onward
are the next high-intent gifting peaks — plan a separate, short tactical burst
for those windows once this test has a winning angle to build from. Do not
fold a seasonal hook into this discovery test; keep the two campaigns clean
and separate so results are readable.

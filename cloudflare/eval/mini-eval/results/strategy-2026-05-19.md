# The Bet — Arjun Infra
*Prepared 2026-05-19*

---

## 1. The Memo

Every buyer you have ever won came through someone who already knew you — a referral, a walk-in, a local connection. You have never proven that a cold stranger, shown a Meta ad, will raise their hand and enquire. That is fine; most small developers start this way. But now you are asking Meta to bring those strangers, so the first test has one job: find out which stranger is actually there and ready to act.

Two audiences are plausible. Local Ongole families who are weighing a gated villa community against buying a plot and building their own house — the default path in tier-3 Andhra Pradesh. And the diaspora: people from Ongole and Prakasam district who are now settled in Hyderabad and have been meaning to buy property back home for years. You told me this buyer exists. You have never deliberately reached them. That is the biggest untested lever in your whole business right now — they already want what you are selling, they have the income to act, and they have a direct practical reason to use a developer rather than manage remote construction from 500 kilometres away.

Neither group has ever been spoken to specifically in your paid media. Your boosted posts — villa photos, festival greetings, project updates — are broadcast content: they assume someone interested enough will find you. A performance campaign works differently. It speaks to a specific person about their specific situation, and makes it trivially easy for them to raise their hand. That is what the test does. We run two cells: one speaks to the diaspora buyer in Hyderabad, directly and in Telugu, naming their situation. The other speaks to the local upgrader and shows them what gated-community life actually looks and feels like — not quality claims, not approval badges, but the real product. Both cells use Meta Instant Lead Forms, not your website contact page (more on that below). This is an audience-discovery test: we are not yet refining messages, we are finding out which audience is on Meta and ready to respond.

One action before you spend a rupee: ask three to five past buyers to leave a Google review. A serious lead who sees your ad will Google your name next. Right now they find almost nothing. A handful of real reviews changes what they find, and it is the single cheapest thing you can do to improve your conversion from enquiry to site visit. One afternoon, zero cost.

---

## 2. Conversion Event

A qualified lead submitted via Meta Instant Lead Form or a phone call — a real person who provides contact details and expresses project interest, with the intent of progressing to a site visit.

## 3. The Blocker

The brand has never addressed a specific identified audience with a direct message and a frictionless way to act on it. All current conversions happen through warm channels; cold-stranger conversion is entirely untested. The sharpest version of this gap is the diaspora buyer — confirmed real by the founder, known to buy, and never once deliberately reached through paid media. The first test exists to close that gap.

## 4. The Angles

### Cell 1 — Diaspora buyer in Hyderabad: *"Your family's home in Ongole — handled."*

**Hypothesis:** A buyer from Ongole now settled in Hyderabad, who has been deferring a property purchase back home, will convert at a materially higher rate than a local buyer when shown an ad that directly names their situation — and frames a developer-built, finished villa as the solution to managing that purchase from a distance.

**Why:** The founder confirmed this audience exists and has never deliberately targeted it. This is the largest single untested lever. On Meta, the targeting is Hyderabad geography plus Telugu-language preference; the ad copy does the self-selection — naming "Ongole-origin, based in Hyderabad" as the intended reader triggers recognition immediately, even within a broad geo. The product solves their practical problem directly: buying a finished developer villa eliminates the construction-management burden of buying a plot and building remotely. No competitor is making a specific offer to this audience; the local ad cluster is entirely focused on in-market trust signals aimed at local buyers.

**Visual lane:** Elevation renders and interior finishing photography — clean, aspirational, quality-forward. This buyer is purchasing at a distance and needs to see a professional, finished product, not warmth or family lifestyle imagery. Let the render or the interior shot lead; keep the frame uncluttered. Short bilingual Telugu/English copy that opens by naming the situation directly ("Based in Hyderabad? Planning to invest in Ongole?") — the identity trigger is in the words; the image establishes credibility. No heavy branding overlays. Quality of the product is the whole argument.

---

### Cell 2 — Local Ongole upgrader: *"What gated community living actually looks like."*

**Hypothesis:** A local Ongole family sitting on the habitual "buy a plot and build" versus "buy into a gated villa community" decision will convert at higher rates when the ad shows the lived reality of a completed gated community — the shared spaces, the amenities, the finished interiors, the feel of the place — rather than claims about quality or regulatory approvals.

**Why:** The primary competitive battle in tier-3 Andhra Pradesh real estate is not developer versus developer — it is the gated-community model against the deeply embedded local habit of buying a plot and building independently. Every competitor in this market advertises the same things: RERA approvals, trusted developer, amenity lists. Nobody is directly making the case for *why choosing a developer-built gated community is better than building your own.* Arjun Infra has real walkthroughs and completed-villa photography available — the exact assets to make that case compellingly and visually. This is the ad that uses them.

**Visual lane:** Real-photography led. Community life inside the gates — evening light in shared gardens, finished villa interiors that suggest daily use, clean common areas with people in them. Warm, lived-in, human. The ad should feel like a glimpse of a life, not a project brochure. Do not lead with renders or empty spaces; show the product inhabited. Telugu-language caption. This lane must be visually distinct from Cell 1: warmer palette, real-photo rather than render, family-and-life energy rather than aspirational quality.

---

## 5. The Honest Flag

**One. Get Google reviews before you start — this is not optional.** A serious lead who sees your ad will search your name before they commit to a site visit. Right now the brand has almost no independent third-party proof online — no meaningful Google reviews, no JustDial presence. The testimonials on your own website do not count to a stranger. This does not kill the test (a low-friction lead form will still capture curious enquiries), but it will limit how many of those leads convert to actual site visits downstream. Ask three to five satisfied past buyers for a Google review before the campaign goes live. This is one afternoon of outreach and it changes what a cold prospect finds when they verify you.

**Two. Use Meta Instant Lead Forms — not website clicks.** Your website's contact infrastructure is a personal mobile number and a Gmail address — honest about your scale, but not what closes a cold stranger's confidence at the moment of decision. Instant Lead Forms capture contact details inside Meta at lower friction and cost 30–50% less per lead than driving traffic off-platform. For a high-ticket, long-deliberation purchase where the goal is an enquiry (not a same-day purchase), the native form is the right mechanic. Do not run this campaign to website traffic.

**Three. A week-1 CPL gate — this budget carries two cells only if costs cooperate.** The cost-per-lead estimate used to size this test (₹200–350 for Instant Lead Forms in an uncrowded tier-3 market, in Telugu) is an industry-range estimate — not your own confirmed data, which you don't yet have. At ₹60,000/month (≈₹15,000/week), the budget supports two cells if CPL holds at or below ₹300. If week-1 data shows CPL running above that, consolidate immediately to Cell 1 (the diaspora buyer) — it is the higher-value untested hypothesis and the better use of a budget under pressure. Do not wait until end-of-month to make that call.

**Four. Agree a cost-per-lead ceiling before you start.** You did not give a target CPL or a maximum acceptable cost-per-enquiry. Given the ticket size of a villa or apartment sale, virtually any CPL under ₹1,000 is strong ROI if it converts downstream. But without an agreed ceiling you will have no way to judge the result — every number looks bad without context. Settle on a ceiling with your team before day one.

**Five. Timing.** Test window: approximately May 19 – late June 2026. No major festive trigger falls in this window, but the summer period mildly elevates hometown-property thinking among diaspora buyers who are visiting or planning visits — Cell 1 is well-timed. The monsoon (July–August) typically slows real estate enquiries; plan to pause or reduce spend then. The winning creative and audience from this test should power a deliberate burst in September–October 2026, timed to Dasara and Diwali — Andhra Pradesh's strongest real estate buying window of the year. That burst is a separate campaign; do not conflate it with the discovery test running now.

## 1. The Memo

DailyObjects has a real product, a genuine design identity, and 21,000+ people who love it enough to leave a 4.7-star review. The problem is not the brand — it is how it is being shown to strangers.

Right now you are running catalogue ads and boosted design drops. Those formats work well on people who already know you: they see a product they've browsed, they return and buy. But a cold stranger — someone who has never heard of DailyObjects and is scrolling past hundreds of phone-case ads — gets no reason to stop, no reason to trust you over the sea of cheap Amazon cases or the other "design-led" brands in their feed. Product-on-background looks like everyone else. It is warm-audience creative misapplied to a cold audience, and it cannot convert the new customers you want.

You do not know which *message* breaks through to a cold Apple user, because you have never isolated and tested one. The blended ROAS you track mixes loyal returners with genuine cold acquisition — it hides what cold strangers actually cost and which creative actually moves them.

The test we are running answers one question: what is the reason a cold, design-conscious iPhone user picks DailyObjects over everything else in their feed? We are betting three distinct answers against each other — the Indian craft story, the scale-of-trust story, and the complete Apple lifestyle story — to find the one that actually converts. The winner becomes the creative foundation of your cold-acquisition programme.

One flag that must be addressed alongside the test: your external review profile (Trustpilot and similar) carries a pattern of quality and service complaints that a cold stranger will find if they search. It will suppress conversion regardless of how good the ad is. The fix is not to delay the test — it is to work the external review profile in parallel, which is fully in your hands.

An online purchase on dailyobjects.com by a new customer — someone with no prior purchase or branded-search relationship with DailyObjects.

# The Bet — Noise · Luna Ring · May 2026

---

## 1. The Memo

Noise leads a category in structural decline. The affordable smartwatch — where it
built its 26% market share — is being undercut to the floor by brands that will
race to zero on price. The upmarket move to the Luna Ring and premium Pro watches
is not a vanity brand stretch; it is the only commercially rational exit from a
commoditising segment. The problem is that the current premium ads are almost
certainly doing what every competitor does: leading with features and specs, the
language that built the affordable business. That language does not close a
premium sale to a cold stranger. A cold buyer who sees Noise at a higher price
point has one of two reactions — they know the brand as the affordable-watch
brand and don't believe the premium, or they've never heard of Noise and have no
reason to pay up. What's missing in both cases is not awareness. What's missing is
a specific, resonant reason to believe that this product solves a real problem in a
way nothing on their wrist already does.

The Luna Ring is the right product to crack first. It is genuinely not a
smartwatch — no screen, no notifications, Red Dot Award-winning design, something
that looks like jewellery and behaves like a health lab. It does not need to win
a features-per-rupee argument. It needs a buyer to think: *that is for me and I
have been waiting for it.* The test below bets on which angle unlocks that
conviction. Five cells, five different reasons to believe — one audience, one
product, five competing hypotheses about what the real job-to-be-done is. Find the
winner here and the Pro watches become a phase-two test with an answer already
in hand.

One important note on scope: this test focuses the full budget on the Luna Ring.
Running Luna Ring and Pro watches simultaneously would mix two different products,
two different price points, and two different buyer psychologies into one test —
the result would be unreadable. Win the Luna Ring angle first; use it as the
hypothesis-starter for the Pro.

---

## 2. Conversion Event

An online purchase of the Luna Ring — initiated from a cold Meta impression,
no prior brand engagement assumed — on gonoise.com or a marketplace. The judging
metric is cost per purchase (CPA) against the founder's stated ceiling of
low-four-figures of rupees.

---

## 3. The Blocker

**The premium ad is running the affordable playbook.** In a saturated category
where every brand leads with features and spec claims, the Luna Ring needs a
different creative gear entirely — a specific, lived-in use-case that makes a cold
buyer feel the product was built for their actual life, not a feature list they
can comparison-shop against a ₹3,000 watch.

---

## 4. The Angles

**Budget sizing.** ₹30,00,000/month ÷ ~4.3 weeks ≈ ₹7,00,000/week. Five cells
at ₹1,40,000/week each. At the founder's own CPA range (₹1,200–1,800), that
funds 78–117 purchase events per cell per week — comfortably above the ~50
events/week Meta needs to exit its learning phase at both ends of the range.
Five cells is the right number: the budget powers them cleanly, and the five
hypotheses below are genuinely distinct bets that together form a real portfolio.

This is a **message test**: the audience is broadly defined as premium
health-and-tech buyers on Meta India, and each angle runs to the same broad
targeting. The primary question is *what to say*. A secondary read will fall out
naturally — each angle will self-select a slightly different sub-audience
(the recovery angle will skew athletic; the jewellery angle will likely skew
design-conscious and may skew female), and that audience data is valuable
secondary learning.

---

### Angle 1 — The Sleep Specialist

**Hypothesis.** Cold buyers for whom poor sleep is an active, felt problem will
convert when Luna Ring is positioned as the only health tracker that monitors
their sleep without the bulk, brightness, or discomfort of a wrist device — and
without the data being buried in a watch face they barely glance at.

**Why.** Sleep is the single most universal and emotionally resonant health
concern — it is not a niche. Luna Ring's continuous, ring-form sleep tracking is
a genuine product truth: a ring sits flat, never lights up in the dark, never
creates wear pressure at the wrist, and tracks through every sleep stage
passively. The research notes the brand is pushing into higher-value products;
sleep is the use case where the ring form is most obviously superior to any watch.
Competitors cluster on steps, heart rate, and lifestyle energy — nobody in the
Indian market is owning sleep intelligence as a distinct territory. This is the
most likely anchor cell of the five.

**Visual lane.** Warm, intimate, low-key residential photography. Ring on hand
in a night-time routine — a hand reaching for a glass of water on a bedside
table, a ring-wearing hand pulling a duvet, morning light on a face coming out
of sleep. No metrics screens visible; the feeling is "this person sleeps like
a deliberate act." Warm amber tones, soft focus in the background, the ring
prominent but not aggressively foregrounded. The mood should read *recovery,
not gadgetry.*

---

### Angle 2 — The Recovery Edge

**Hypothesis.** Fitness-forward buyers — people who train consistently and
already think in terms of effort and recovery — will convert when Luna Ring is
framed as the tool that closes the gap between training and knowing whether
you're actually recovering from it.

**Why.** India's fitness culture is in documented growth — gym memberships, home
workouts, running communities are a real and expanding segment. These buyers
already spend on gear; they are habituated to premium fitness purchases. The
angle positions Luna Ring not as a step-counter but as a recovery instrument —
the product that tells you if yesterday's session actually landed, and whether
today's is a good idea. This is a use case the ring form supports genuinely
(heart-rate variability, resting HR, SpO₂ tracked overnight without a wrist
strap interfering with sleep posture). The trigger is specific and near-term:
if you trained this week and don't know how recovered you are, you feel that.

**Visual lane.** Dynamic but controlled — real photography, active environments
(post-run cool-down stretch, a hand placing a water bottle after a workout).
Not the loud, aggressive energy of boAt's youth lifestyle aesthetic — closer to
a premium fitness brand: calm confidence, muted colour palette, natural light.
The ring should be clearly visible in athletic context. Text treatment (if any)
should be sparse and direct. Mood: *I have the data; I know my body.*

---

### Angle 3 — The Anti-Gadget

**Hypothesis.** Buyers who are aware they want health data but fatigued by the
constant interruption and screen-glare of smartwatches will convert when Luna
Ring is positioned as health tracking that has deliberately removed everything
that makes smartwatches annoying — not a stripped-down watch, but a different
philosophy entirely.

**Why.** This is the most contrarian bet in the set — and the most
un-copyable if it lands, because no watch-form competitor can make this
argument honestly. The Luna Ring's no-display design is usually presented as a
limitation or an "also" feature; reframing it as *the point* of the product
turns the ring form into the whole argument. The research shows competitors
cluster on brighter displays, more features, more notifications — the entire
category is racing in one direction. This angle runs the opposite direction.
The competitive gap here is structural: if this angle converts, it converts a
buyer who is permanently inaccessible to watch-form competitors. Worth one cell
to find out.

**Visual lane.** Minimalist, high-contrast, still. Ring on hand in a quiet
everyday moment — morning coffee, reading a book, hands at rest on a desk. No
busy lifestyle staging, no metrics dashboards, no tech energy. The visual *is*
the argument: deliberate stillness in a world of noise. Generous negative space;
the ring should be beautifully lit as an object. Where the other four cells
will carry energy and motion, this one earns its distinctness through calm.
Mood: *serene, deliberate, uncluttered* — the opposite of every other
wearables ad in feed.

### Angle 4 — Body Intelligence

**Hypothesis.** Self-improvement and health-data buyers — people oriented toward
optimising their own performance and who already consume health content — will
convert when Luna Ring is framed as the instrument that translates their body's
continuous signals into legible, actionable daily insight, positioned as AI-
assisted self-knowledge rather than another metric dashboard.

**Why.** The research notes the Luna Ring carries AI health features and that
the brand presented at CES 2025 under a "Pro for Pros" framing. The
self-optimisation buyer in India is real and growing — they are the person who
has tried multiple fitness apps, wears a watch but finds the data doesn't
change their behaviour, and is looking for something that actually connects
the dots. This angle differentiates on *depth of interpretation* rather than
number of sensors — a meaningful distinction from the features-per-rupee
battlefield. It is also the angle most likely to attract a buyer who has
already dismissed cheaper alternatives, because it requires the most from
the brand's actual product intelligence.

**Visual lane.** Clean, modern, with a subtle data-visualization aesthetic —
not clinical, not cold. Real photography of a ring-wearing hand in a neutral
environment, paired with light graphic treatment suggesting health metrics or
body-rhythm curves: soft, non-intrusive, clearly not a spec sheet. The feeling
should be *curious and confident* — someone who understands their own body as
a system. Slightly cooler colour palette than angles 1 and 2. Mood: *intelligent,
precise, forward-looking* — but human, not laboratory.

### Angle 5 — Worn, Not Carried

**Hypothesis.** Design-conscious and aesthetics-forward buyers — people for whom
what they wear is an expression of identity, not just utility — will convert
when Luna Ring is positioned as a piece of design that happens to track your
health, rather than a health tracker that happens to look acceptable.

**Why.** The Luna Ring won a Red Dot Award. That is a real, concrete, specific
credential — not a brand claim but an externally awarded fact — and in the
visual-first culture of Instagram and Meta feed, a genuinely beautiful object
at a premium price point can convert on desire before it converts on function.
This angle targets a buyer who may not have been looking for a smartwatch or
a health tracker at all; they are a buyer who responds to beautiful, considered
objects and who will rationalise the health-tracking capability as a bonus.
This cell also has a meaningful secondary benefit: it will likely skew toward
a demographic (design-conscious, potentially female, urban) that the other four
cells may underweight, giving the test audience-composition learning alongside
message learning.

**Visual lane.** Fashion and jewellery-adjacent styling — not tech photography.
Luna Ring lit as an object of craft: close-up on the ring's form and finish,
worn as part of a considered outfit, editorial framing. The environment should
feel aspirational but real — not a studio shoot, but a beautiful space. No
health metrics, no specs, no dashboard elements visible anywhere in the
creative. The copy (if any) should be minimal and taste-forward. Mood:
*I want this because it's beautiful* — treat the Red Dot Award credential as
a quiet endorsement, not a headline claim.

## 5. Honest Flags

**Flag 1 — Product page legitimacy (check before launch).** For a premium
purchase in a new category from a brand known for affordable products, the
Luna Ring product page needs to hold a cold buyer from a paid ad — who arrives
sceptical, does not know Noise as a premium brand, and needs to be convinced in
under thirty seconds. Two things to confirm before the test runs: (a) review
density — if the Luna Ring carries fewer than roughly 50–100 genuine reviews
on the product page and key marketplace listings, that is a trust ceiling the
ads will hit regardless of which angle wins; (b) category explanation — the
page needs to answer "why a ring, not a watch?" before the buyer scrolls to the
price. Neither of these is creative. Both are preconditions for a clean test.

**Flag 2 — Category saturation on Meta is real, and the CPA range may widen.**
The research explicitly notes that Noise, boAt, Fire-Boltt and a long tail of
challengers all advertise heavily on Meta — CPMs in this category are at the
upper end of the Indian market. The founder's own CPA range (₹1,200–1,800) is
the right benchmark and the budget maths above use it honestly. But the
saturation means that *undifferentiated* creative will face extra pressure — the
entire premise of this test (5 distinct angles, each genuinely unlike the
category norm) is the hedge against it. If any cell's creative ends up looking
like a features-per-rupee product ad, pull it early; it will not beat the
category average.

**Flag 3 — The Pro watches need their own test, not a budget split.** The
Pro watches are a real growth target but a different product with different buyer
psychology in a more contested tier (where Apple and Samsung also have presence
at the higher end). Running them alongside Luna Ring in this test dilutes both.
The recommended sequence: run this test to a winner (4–6 weeks), then launch
a Pro watch test using the winning Luna Ring angle as one hypothesis to carry
forward — the job-to-be-done framing that wins here is very likely portable.

**Timing note.** No major Indian festive or gifting window falls within the
immediate test period (May–July). This is a neutral launch window — no seasonal
tailwind, but no distortion either, making it a clean read. The next significant
seasonal moment is the Diwali/festive-sales window in October–November; if a
winning angle emerges from this test by July, there is time to build scaled
production creative around it before the festive peak.

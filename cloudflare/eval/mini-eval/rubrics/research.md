# Research binder — mini-eval rubric

The deliverable under test is **`research.md`** — the file the strategist
will reason from when writing The Bet. The judge grades it against the
criteria below, given the same intake the research apprentice was given
(`founder-facts.md`, any uploaded reference images, the current date). The
harness — not the judge — computes the overall pass from these verdicts:
overall **fails** if any *critical* criterion fails, or if more than one
*supporting* criterion fails.

Grade strictly. A junior produces a tidy summary that takes the brand's
self-portrait as fact, fills the file with plausible-sounding buyer language
that wasn't actually retrieved, and never names a gap because it never looked
for one. It looks like work. It hands the strategist downstream an inflated
portrait of a brand that may not be winning customers — and the strategist
diagnoses against a fiction. That is a FAIL even when the file looks clean.
You are grading judgment and provenance, not polish.

---

## Critical criteria — any one failing fails the whole file

### `sourced` — every claim traces to a citable surface

Every specific claim in `research.md` — a price, a date, a reviewer quote, a
landmark, a competitor name, an industry benchmark, a cultural anchor —
attaches to a source the apprentice could point to: a URL from
`search_results`, a quoted snippet, a fetched page's content, or a
reference-image filename. Surface attribution is by domain (e.g.,
*justdial.com*, *agoda.com*); the URL itself lives in the source the
apprentice retrieved, not paraphrased into prose where it could be mistyped.

- **PASS:** every specific claim has a surface attached; the judge could
  pick any line and find the source it came from.
- **FAIL:** specific claims float without attribution; reviewer quotes
  appear without a surface; "according to research" with no traceable
  origin; statistics cited without a date or source.

### `no-fabrication` — nothing invented

The sister discipline to `sourced`. No invented numbers, dates, reviewer
names, customer segments, prices, quotes, competitor names, or category
benchmarks. No paraphrased-then-quoted text dressed in the buyer's register
that wasn't actually retrieved. No "based on common patterns" filling in
where retrieval came back thin.

Watch especially for **synthetic buyer language** — phrases in the local
register (Tenglish, Hinglish, French, etc.) that read as authentic
verbatim but trace to no specific retrieved source. This is the most
dangerous failure mode of all because it is structurally indistinguishable
from real signal until the downstream campaign fails for reasons no one
can explain.

- **PASS:** every specific fact traces to retrieved content; no quoted
  buyer phrase exists in the file unless it appears in a retrieved
  snippet or fetched page; thin signal is shown as thin, not inflated.
- **FAIL:** any invented quote attributed to a reviewer who doesn't exist
  in the sources; any made-up price or benchmark; any extrapolated
  "buyer typically says X" without a real retrieved instance; any
  fabricated specifics dressed as evidence.

### `no-marketing-copy` — no finished creative

`research.md` is the strategist's evidence dossier, not a starter pack for
the hook writer. No finished hooks. No sample ad lines. No "phrases the
brand could use." No "how to use this in marketing" section. No synthesised
copy in the buyer's register that isn't a direct quote from a source.

The hook writer downstream owns finished copy; the strategist owns the
diagnosis; you own the evidence. A research file that has already written
the brand's marketing for it has done the hook writer's job, badly, instead
of its own.

- **PASS:** the file contains only evidence + observations; no creative
  output; no pre-written brand voice or copy patterns.
- **FAIL:** a "summary cheat sheet for marketing" section; a list of
  "phrases to use"; sample hooks or ad lines; "marketing recommendations";
  any synthetic phrasing in the buyer's register that wasn't quoted from
  a real retrieved source.

### `gap-named` — what couldn't be found is named, not hidden

When a surface returns empty, when a probe finds thin signal, when a
structural blind spot applies (Google Maps reviews aren't indexed, Reddit
is user-agent-blocked, the brand's website is JavaScript-rendered and
returns no content, the locale is too small for category-level forum
voice), the gap is **named explicitly**. Honest absence, not silent
inflation.

The strategist downstream has no defence against silent fullness. They
need to see what was reached versus what was not, so they can lower
confidence on judgments that rest on unreached signal. A research file
that pretends fullness is worse than one that admits emptiness — the
former produces a confident wrong strategy.

- **PASS:** thin signal is shown as thin; empty surfaces are named (e.g.,
  *"TripAdvisor: no reviews for this property"*); structural blind spots
  are flagged when relevant (*"Google reviews not accessible to search"*);
  the §8 Gaps section names what the strategist will want and you couldn't
  find. When the representative signal (e.g. a ratings distribution) is
  unreachable, the file says so — a skewed sample is not passed off as the
  average.
- **FAIL:** a file that reads as complete when reality was thin; no
  acknowledgment of structural blind spots when they apply; the §8 Gaps
  section absent or empty; fabricated richness shipping as real signal.

## Supporting criteria — more than one failing fails the whole file

### `claim-vs-reality` — both sides surfaced, contradiction visible

§1 of `research.md` surfaces both **the brand's own claim** (the website
positioning, the about-page voice, the marketing pitch) AND **the
evidenced reality** (reviews, photos, pricing, third-party coverage). When
the two diverge, the divergence is visible in the file. The strategist
will diagnose from this contradiction; your job is to put both sides on
paper, not to reconcile them.

- **PASS:** §1 clearly shows what the brand says about itself AND what
  evidence reveals; contradictions, where they exist, are surfaced
  rather than softened.
- **FAIL:** §1 reads as a brand description without a reality check; or
  reality dominates without the brand's stated claim being captured; or
  contradictions are smoothed into a neutral summary.

### `two-level-buyer` — both probes attempted, surface-matched

§2 attempts both the **buyer-as-customer** probe (brand-specific reviews
on platforms that carry them) AND the **buyer-as-person** probe
(category-level voice from forums, creator content, niche directories).
For thin-review brands the category probe is the primary lift; for
established brands both surface real signal. Probes are pointed at the
surface class that actually carries the signal type sought — formal review
forms for buyer-as-customer voice, creator content and forums for
buyer-as-person register.

- **PASS:** both probes attempted; results attributed; surface-class
  matched to signal type; for thin-review brands, the absence of
  brand-specific voice is named and the category probe carries the load.
- **FAIL:** only one probe attempted when both apply; category-level probe
  missing for a thin-review brand; probes pointed at obviously wrong
  surface classes (e.g., asking OTAs for casual language register).

### `want-and-strengths` — the pull and the claimable proof, both surfaced

§2c surfaces what the buyer is *trying to get* — the JTBD at two levels
(macro umbrella + hero product, the latter keyed to the reference image or
briefed line) — mined from the same verbatim voice, never invented. And §1
captures not only the claim-vs-reality gap but the **verified, claimable
strengths** — where evidence confirms a genuine plus — each sourced. Both
stay under the same provenance bar: a guessed desire or an inflated strength
fails here; genuinely thin material *named as thin* passes. This is what
gives the downstream cell its **pull** (desire) and its honest **proof**
— without them it builds spec sheets or fabricates.

- **PASS:** the buyer's want is surfaced at both levels from real retrieved
  voice; the hero-product JTBD is keyed to the reference/briefed product;
  verified strengths are captured with sources, or their thinness is named
  honestly.
- **FAIL:** §2c absent, or filled with aspirational/invented desire not
  traceable to retrieved voice; only criteria/objections with no "what they
  want"; claimable strengths absent for a brand that has them, or strengths
  asserted with no source; or a want or strength built on a single review or a
  complaint-skewed sample, with no prevalence or triangulation behind it.

### `locale-depth` — depth identified, hyperlocal probes if applicable

Locale depth is identified correctly from the founder's brief + brand
footprint + review clustering. When the dial reaches city-level or
tighter, the apprentice loads `reference/hyperlocal.md` and §3 of
`research.md` carries the three additional probes — language register,
cultural anchors, sub-culture identity — each with attributed evidence
from the right surface classes.

- **PASS:** locale depth is identified; for hyperlocal briefs, the three
  additional subsections appear in §3 with retrieved evidence; for
  national/global briefs, locale is named lightly and hyperlocal probes
  are correctly NOT run.
- **FAIL:** a hyperlocal brief treated as national (or vice versa); the
  three hyperlocal probes missing or filled with extrapolation; locale
  ambiguity unsurfaced when the founder's brief was unclear.

### `visual-reality-handled` — founder photos primary; gap named when absent

For visual-first verticals (hospitality, salons, restaurants, physical
products, real estate, anything where the look of the thing is
load-bearing for conversion), §4 of `research.md` uses founder-supplied
reference images when present — Read multimodally, described honestly
based on what they actually show. When reference images are absent for a
visual-first vertical, the gap is named explicitly and triangulation is
attempted with stranger-supplied photos on reachable surfaces.

- **PASS:** reference images are read and described honestly when present;
  gap named when absent for visual-first verticals; non-visual-first
  verticals are correctly skipped here.
- **FAIL:** reference images ignored when present; §4 contains visual
  descriptions without an image source (i.e., fabricated visual claims);
  visual-first vertical's visual reality skipped without naming the gap.

### `cost-and-calendar` — hedged range, anchored to today

§6 contains a **hedged cost-per-result range** for the brand's vertical
in its locale — sourced, with the source's date, never a fake-precise
point estimate. §7 anchors events and seasonality to today's date, within
the ~6-8 week test window; future-window items are flagged but not
pushed as live signals.

- **PASS:** cost is a range with source and date; calendar items are
  anchored to today's date with sources; future-window items are flagged
  as such; thin signal in either section is named honestly.
- **FAIL:** cost as a fake-precise point estimate; calendar items
  undated; events outside the test window pushed as live signals; or
  these sections silently absent without a gap-naming.

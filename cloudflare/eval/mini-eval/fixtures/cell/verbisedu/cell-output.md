## 3. Critic Verdict (inline, self-administered against `references/critic.md`)

### Kill tests per take

**TAKE 1 — "The Arc"**
- *Swap test:* G. Bhavana's quote is Verbis-specific. But the format — photo of a student desk with a review card overlaid — is fully replicable by IDP or Leverage with their own reviews. The concept survives; the execution is generic.
- *Canva test:* A founder can photograph their own desk, screenshot a Google review, and overlay the card in Canva in under ten minutes. This is the exact format of the prior hand-built v1 run (`verbis-testimonial-v1.png`). Cold traffic; the earned-simple exception does not apply. **FAIL.**

**TAKE 2 — "The Zero-Moment-of-Truth"** *(WINNER)*
- *Swap test:* "Partha has been guiding/assisting us in all the stages of this process." is a real verbatim Google review naming a specific person unique to Verbis. IDP cannot claim "Partha"; Leverage cannot claim a 4.9★ / 490-review Google listing for this specific business. The invite to verify ("search us on Google") is earned only by a brand with this proof base. The buyer's search behavior — looking up a consultant on Google Maps before booking — is their real zero-moment-of-truth; no competitor uses this as the creative device. **PASS.**
- *Category-look test:* Not a "laptop showing a dashboard," not "floating UI with gradient blobs," not "diverse team smiling at a screen." A phone screen showing a real third-party review platform is a fundamentally different object. **PASS.**
- *Obvious-first-idea test:* Making the buyer's own verification behavior the hero of the frame — showing the Google Maps listing as if the buyer just found it — is NOT the junior's first idea (which is "review card on a desk"). **PASS.**
- *Bundle coherence:* The picture IS the proof — the Google Maps listing directly demonstrates the claim (4.9★ / 490 independent reviews, Partha named). The picture and the hook are one thing. **PASS.**
- *Drawability:* Phone screen showing Google Maps listing (Verbis Edu title, 4.9★, 490 reviews, Mahesh Ns review card naming Partha), desk/notebook behind, navy CTA chip at bottom. A stranger can draw this. **PASS.**
- *Voice test:* The on-image copy is entirely verbatim from real sources (Google Maps UI + Mahesh Ns's real review text). No floating authoritative claims in nobody's voice. **PASS.**
- *Honesty test:* Every element is in the proof field. "Verbis Edu," "4.9," "490 reviews," "Education consultant · Mathikere, Bengaluru," "Mahesh Ns · 1 week ago · ★★★★★," review body — all verified (Google Maps, browser-verified 2026-06-17). No invented statistics, persons, or offers. **PASS.**
- *Staging test:* Staging is written with a reason — the student's zero-moment-of-truth (searching Google Maps before booking) is where this buyer actually encounters this proof. The staging adds to the argument: the ad shows the buyer the thing they were already going to do, and the result they'll get when they do it. **PASS.**
- *Canva test:* A founder cannot produce this in ten minutes in Canva: requires staging a phone showing a live Google Maps listing, photographing it in natural light with a desk behind, and composing the CTA as a separate design element. **PASS.**
- *Stage-fit flag:* The buyer is solution-aware; the format is right. The full Google Maps UI reads as evidence for an evaluating buyer — no mismatch. No flag.

**TAKE 3 — "The 4.9★ Poster"**
- *Swap test:* IDP could run "4.9★ · 50,000 students" in a larger, louder version of this exact format. The star-rating poster is the category's latent generic. The specific numbers are Verbis's, but the form is 100% transferable. **FAIL.**

**TAKE 4 — "The Honest Shortlist"**
- *Swap test:* A handwritten university shortlist could be any consultant's ad. "A list of universities you can actually get into" is exactly what every consultant claims to provide. The Mahesh Ns quote gives partial swap-protection, but the visual (handwritten notebook) adds nothing that only Verbis can claim. **FAIL.**

**TAKE 5 — "The Study Group Chai"**
- *Swap test:* The chai/group context adds social warmth but not swap-proof specificity. The G. Bhavana review carries the uniqueness; the group-chai staging is transferable. **FAIL.**

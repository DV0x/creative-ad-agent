# Brand Research — Ever/Body

**URL:** https://everbody.com
**Category:** Cosmetic dermatology / medical spa ("medspa")
**Market:** New York City (Manhattan), USA
**Researched:** 2026-05-19

## What the brand is

Ever/Body is a chain of cosmetic-dermatology clinics ("medspas") offering
non-surgical aesthetic treatments — injectables (Botox and wrinkle relaxers,
dermal fillers, Sculptra, Kybella), facials and peels (Hydrafacial, DiamondGlow,
Morpheus8 RF microneedling, VI Peel, BioRePeel), lasers (Clear+Brilliant,
Lumecca IPL), body contouring (Emsculpt NEO, truSculpt iD) and hair treatments
(laser hair removal, Keravive, plasma restoration).

Founded 2018 in New York City by Kate Twist. It launched with venture funding
and scaled fast: ~$17M at launch (2019), a $38M Series B led by Tiger Global
(2021), and a $55.5M Series C (2022). On 19 December 2025 Ever/Body was
**acquired by Advanced MedAesthetic Partners**, a multi-clinic medaesthetic
group (an aesthetics "roll-up" / management group). It currently operates around
10 clinics nationwide, roughly 3–4 of them in NYC; a Greenwich Village clinic
opened 18 April 2026.

## How the brand describes itself today

The current everbody.com positions the brand at the very top of the category.
Headline claims, verbatim:

- "The Gold Standard In Cosmetic Dermatology"
- "Raising The Bar In Aesthetics"
- "Reveal Your Most Confident Self"
- "Undeniable results from the industry's most experienced providers"
- "Performed By World-Class Clinical Experts" — with a "rigorous training program"
- "Curated Treatments, Grounded In Science" — "An Approach of Both Science & Artistry"
- "Partners On Your Beauty Journey"
- Treats "All Skin Types & Tones"

The tone is premium and authority-led: expertise, science, "world-class,"
"gold standard," "most experienced."

## How the brand originally described itself

At founding (2018), Ever/Body's stated mission was to *disrupt* cosmetic
dermatology by building "a service experience for all skin tones and textures,
ages, and genders — particularly for those historically underserved by the
industry: BIPOC and LGBTQ+ communities." Its named differentiators were
**price transparency up front** and a best-in-class provider-education program.
Trade press at the time framed it as a modern, approachable, inclusive
alternative to the intimidating and opaque traditional dermatologist's office.

## The offer and pricing

- No prices are listed on the website; cost is set at an in-person consultation.
- Reported entry price: Botox / wrinkle relaxers from ~$300.
- New-client promotion: "Take 20% Off Your First Visit."
- Patient financing offered through Cherry (instalment payments).

## Reviews and reputation

Ever/Body is well-reviewed overall — for example ~4.7 stars across 85 Yelp
reviews at the SoHo clinic, and 849 reviews on reviews.io, with active recent
reviews across its NYC locations into 2026.

What customers praise (recurring themes, across platforms):

- Knowledgeable, kind providers; friendly, personable front-desk staff.
- Clean, modern, welcoming clinics.
- **Explicitly low-pressure.** Reviewers volunteer, unprompted, that "no one
  will push you into anything or try to sell you anything you don't need," that
  there is "never pressure to purchase," and that consultations "did not feel
  salesly." (Worth noting: hard upselling is one of the most common consumer
  complaints about the medspa category in general.)

What customers complain about (the minority of reviews):

- **Price.** "The price is extremely high"; "they overcharge." This is the
  single most common negative theme.
- **Results occasionally not delivered.** One customer reported three truSculpt
  iD sessions (~$1,200) producing "zero results"; Ever/Body's leadership reached
  out and offered a complimentary follow-up after the complaint.
- **Location instability.** A customer who had pre-paid a 4-session package
  reported that their nearby clinic "closed with no notice," leaving sessions
  unused. This sits in tension with the brand's public expansion narrative.
- Glassdoor carries 56 employee reviews; staffing and operations under the new
  ownership are an open question, not a documented problem.

## The market

NYC cosmetic-dermatology / medspa is a large, crowded, sophisticated market.
Treatments such as Botox are a considered but routine purchase, repeated on a
roughly 3–4 month cycle — category economics are heavily repeat-driven. Demand
carries strong local intent: people search for and book treatments within a
neighbourhood. The category also has a reputation problem with consumers —
opaque pricing, hard upselling, and "overdone" results are widely-voiced fears.
Direct competitors are detailed in `competitors.md`.

## Visual reality

This is a visual-first vertical: the clinics, the providers, and the
results (natural-looking faces and skin) are the product. Ever/Body's brand
look is modern, clean, soft and minimal — a deliberately un-clinical,
contemporary aesthetic. Real clinic and provider photography is available, and
real (natural, not overdone) before/after results are central to how the brand
can be shown.

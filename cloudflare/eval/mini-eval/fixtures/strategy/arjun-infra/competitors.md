# Competitor Research — Ongole / Coastal-AP Residential Real Estate

The brand under research is Arjun Infra (see `research.md`), a residential
developer in Ongole, Andhra Pradesh.

## The competitive picture

- Arjun Infra competes primarily with **other local Ongole and coastal-Andhra
  developers** building plots, villas and apartments — many of them small,
  owner-run operations of similar scale.
- The broader alternative a buyer weighs is **open plots / independent house
  construction** rather than a developer-built gated community — a very common
  path in tier-2/3 Andhra Pradesh.
- Large branded national developers do not meaningfully operate in a market the
  size of Ongole; the real competition is local.

## Where competitors cluster

- Local developers advertise on **trust and approvals** — RERA / DTCP / HMDA-
  approved, "trusted," years in business, gated-community amenities.
- Listing portals (99acres, Square Yards, MagicBricks) carry projects, but they
  are a search channel, not a brand-building channel.
- The local-builder ad space on Meta in a tier-3 city is **relatively
  uncrowded** versus metro real estate — fewer advertisers bidding, which tends
  to keep ad costs lower.
- The sharpest competing *choice* a buyer faces is "buy a plot and build" versus
  "buy into a developer's gated community" — more than one developer versus
  another.

## Current visual zeitgeist (from the competitor-ads scan)

*Approximate read of what is dominating competing Meta ads in tier-2 / tier-3
Andhra residential real estate right now (May 2026). Hedged, qualitative.*

- The dominant ad form is **amenity-focused renders + RERA / HMDA / DTCP
  approval badges** — clubhouse, pool, gated-entrance shots; big "approved"
  overlays. Generic and template-feeling.
- Aerial / drone shots of plots and project layouts run heavily on
  plot-developer ads.
- Hoarding-style hard-sell overlays — "Book Now," "Limited Plots," festive
  templates ("Plots this Ugadi") — are common.
- What is **conspicuously under-served**: real lived-in community life — what a
  finished, *occupied* gated community actually feels like. Almost no
  competitor shows real, inhabited homes in real evening light; the category
  leans aspirational-renders, not lived-real.

### 2b. Buyer-as-person (category-level voice)

**What students worry about when choosing a study-abroad consultant:**

**Visa rejection and profile damage** — Students are acutely anxious about visa risk. US F-1 visa rejection for Indian applicants reached 61% in 2025; Canada study permit rejection was ~64% in 2025; UK visa issuances were down 32% in Q1 2026 vs Q1 2025 with refusals concentrated in South Asia (onwardo.com, 2026-06-06; monitor.icef.com, 2026-05-27). Students fear that a consultant's poor documentation or weak application will result in refusal and damage future chances (onwardo.com, 2026-06-06).

**Hidden costs and budget blow-ups** — Reels and community posts repeatedly warn: "Wrong visa or application strategy results into visa rejection or aapka lakhon ka loss" (Instagram, 2026-06-10). Students worry consultants underestimate living costs, add surprise processing fees, or push them into cheap but low-value colleges (Instagram, 2026-05-25; Facebook, 2026-05-24).

**Overpromising admissions, PR, or jobs** — Students are wary of consultants who guarantee admits to top schools or promise 100% visa success; guides explicitly warn against "false hope" language (onwardo.com, 2026-06-06; abroadeducationconsulting.com, 2026-05-21). They compare rankings and career outcomes extensively and distrust consultants who promise outcomes misaligned with their profile (edugoabroad.com, 2026-05-21).

**Uneven counselor quality** — Even large brands (IDP, Edwise, Leverage Edu) carry mixed reviews heavily dependent on the specific branch or counselor; students are aware that "the same brand" can deliver wildly different experiences by location (yocket.com, 2026-05-21).

**Documentation and ethical issues** — Visa guidance aimed at Indian applicants stresses that officers reject files for incomplete/inconsistent documents and fabricated materials; students worry consultants might rush files, mis-enter data, or tolerate fake work experience (onwardo.com, 2026-06-06).

**Communication and post-payment abandonment** — Students report consultants are very responsive before payment, then delay or disappear once fees are collected (yocket.com, 2026-05-21). They worry about lack of visa interview prep support and minimal help with pre-departure logistics despite "end-to-end" claims.

**Quoted examples of student concerns:**
- "Visa rejection is a major worry for international students" and the "cost of poor planning is higher" now (studies-overseas.com, 2026-06-01)
- "Usually it's not the tuition fee. First mistake students do is ignoring the cost of living" (Instagram, 2026-06-16)
- From guides on student choice: "One of the most common Mistake Indian Students Make… is selecting universities only because of rankings or brand reputation" without evaluating fit (abroadeducationconsulting.com, 2026-05-21)
- "If your visa is valid, your documents are complete [and] you give clear answers in the visa interview, immigration will not stop you" (Instagram, 2026-06-10) — reels reassuring students because many feel unsupported right up to departure

### 2c. What the buyer wants — the job (JTBD)

**Macro JTBD — the umbrella category**

Students hire study-abroad consultants to **navigate a high-stakes, complex process (university research, applications, documentation, visa, settlement) with expert guidance, while reducing stress and the risk of costly mistakes**. The underlying job is *legitimation and de-risking*: turning an aspirational goal into a concrete plan that survives visa scrutiny and leads to admission.

**Hero-product JTBD — the free counselling call**

Students book a free counselling call to **evaluate their own eligibility without commitment, understand what their realistic options are, and decide whether to pay for full services**. The call serves as a low-friction filter: students want clarity on their profile strength, the university/country fit, and whether the consultant is trustworthy (vs. overselling them) before they pay.

Evidence (from category voice): Reels and guides repeatedly frame free counselling as a starting point — "First, a free 30 minutes call… they walked into the counselling session with questions, confusion" (Instagram, 2026-05-23; yocket.com, 2026-05-21). The emphasis on early clarity reflects buyer anxiety about being "oversold" on unattainable targets.

### 2d. Decision criteria

Synthesized from student-facing guides and consultant comparison content:

- **Visa success and admit outcomes** — explicit focus on consultants' track record; students cite "got my visa in first attempt" and "received admits from multiple universities" as key praise drivers (yocket.com, 2026-05-21)
- **Cost transparency** — no hidden fees, clear breakdown of total budget (application, visa, living costs, block accounts), and clarity that the consultant is an intermediary, not a lender (fundabroad.com, 2026-06-13)
- **Personalized attention vs. assembly-line service** — students value "individualised attention" and "step-by-step support," especially first-generation applicants (yocket.com, 2026-05-21; eduler.in, 2026-05-19)
- **Realistic course and university fit** — students want to be matched based on their actual background and goals, not just rankings or consultant tie-ups (abroadeducationconsulting.com, 2026-05-21; onwardo.com, 2026-06-06)
- **Documentation quality and ethical practices** — students fear fabrication, fund parking, and sloppy paperwork; transparent, compliant handling is a trust driver (fundabroad.com, 2026-06-13; onwardo.com, 2026-06-06)
- **Ongoing communication and support** — students want timelines, status updates, visa interview prep, and pre-departure help (yocket.com, 2026-05-21)
- **Up-to-date expertise** — given rising visa rejections and policy changes (especially for UK, Canada, US, Australia), students prefer consultants who monitor and adapt to current rules (studies-overseas.com, 2026-06-01; monitor.icef.com, 2026-05-27)

### 2e. Alternatives landscape

**Other paid consultants** — IDP Education, Edwise International, Leverage Edu, KC Overseas Education, Yocket (consultation), upGrad Study Abroad, NewStrides, regional/niche players (Eduler, Thirdwave Overseas, Mancomp, YES Italy, etc.) (yocket.com, 2026-05-21; f6s.com, 2026-06-01). Students compare across these; differentiation is primarily on counselor quality, university tie-ups, and post-payment responsiveness.

**Direct university applications** — Students can apply directly to universities without consultants; this is a "do-it-yourself" path that saves consultant fees but requires self-navigation of visas and documentation (onwardo.com, 2026-06-06).

**Government resources and free platforms** — Government embassies, British Council, and some free community platforms (Yocket as a tool, not a paid consultant) offer some guidance; guides note these are less comprehensive than paid consultancy (yocket.com, 2026-05-21; studies-overseas.com, 2026-06-01).

**Loan + education consultancy bundles** — As costs rise, students increasingly look for consultants integrated with education loan providers (FundAbroad, etc.); this combination reduces friction (fundabroad.com, 2026-06-13).

**"Do nothing" / delay decision** — Given rising visa rejections and policy uncertainty, some students are withdrawing applications to avoid having a refusal on record; delaying is a rational alternative to applying now (monitor.icef.com, 2026-05-27).

### 2f. Category-level objections

**Visa rejection risk and stricter scrutiny** — Visa compliance has "gotten stricter" for Indian students; US F-1, Canada study permits, and UK visas all show elevated refusal rates in 2025–2026 (studies-overseas.com, 2026-06-01; onwardo.com, 2026-06-06; monitor.icef.com, 2026-05-27). Students worry that no consultant can guarantee a visa, only improve odds—and that mistakes cost thousands in refunds and lost time.

**Overpromising industry norm** — Student guides warn that consultants often "push only tie-up colleges," discourage applications to universities not on their list, and promise scholarship/PR/job outcomes that are not guaranteed (yocket.com, 2026-05-21; fundabroad.com, 2026-06-13). This category-wide credibility gap makes every consultant suspect until proven trustworthy.

**Consultant quality is uneven** — Even large, branded consultants deliver inconsistent experiences by branch and counselor (yocket.com, 2026-05-21). Students cannot easily predict quality upfront; this makes the free call critical as a quality test.

## 7. The calendar

**Test window:** Today (2026-06-17) through ~end of August 2026; overlaps with peak inquiry period for September intake.

**Seasonality for study-abroad counselling inquiries in India:**

**August–December — Peak for September/Fall intake**
- Students actively researching universities, shortlisting, and booking counselling calls
- September intake is "one of the major intakes in top study destinations like USA, UK, Canada, Australia" (Instagram, 2026-05-25)
- Application deadlines for Fall 2026 often close by November/December; students motivated to start early to meet timelines
- This is the **primary revenue season** for study-abroad consultants in India

**December–March — Secondary peak for January/Spring intake**
- Students who missed September deadlines or are applying for January/Spring intake become active
- January intake also common for Canada, Australia, some UK programs (Instagram, 2026-05-24)
- Slightly lower volume than September wave, but significant

**April–July — Softer period**
- Students in India are typically in exam season (board exams, college entrance tests); lower discretionary inquiry volume
- Current date (June 17) is in this softer window; inquiry volume likely rising as exams complete and students refocus on study-abroad plans
- September intake applications are ongoing, but peak research and decision phase has passed

**Actionable for test window:**
The campaign is launching in mid-June, which is the **lead-up to the August–December peak**. Students are beginning to research and will be increasingly active. Peak inquiry/booking activity comes in **August–October**. The strategist should expect volume to ramp sharply once the test runs; budget planning should account for this seasonality.

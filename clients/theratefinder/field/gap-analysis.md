## 3. The trap that looks like a gap 🪤

**"Bank said no?" is the obvious hook. It is also against Meta's rules.**

Meta's **Personal Attributes** policy bans copy that implies you know someone's financial situation. Their
own banned example: *"Are you struggling with debt?"* So "Bad credit?", "Declined?", "Bank said no?" get
**rejected** — which is exactly why 8 brokers whose entire websites scream *"approved when banks say no"*
buy **zero** Meta ads for it. The lane isn't empty because it's untapped. It's empty because it's mined.

And when those ads do run, borrowers hate them:
> *"Every ad on Facebook is 'we got your bank decline approved!' It **screams predatory**."* — r/PFC

### The way through: **THE SUBJECT SWAP**
> **Talk about the LENDER'S box, or OUR capability. Never about the borrower's condition.**

| ❌ Rejected *and* predatory | ✅ Legal *and* dignified |
|---|---|
| "Bad credit? We approve!" | "All income and credit types considered" *(Swivel runs this)* |
| "Are you drowning in debt?" | "If you own a home in Ontario with equity…" *(Miracle runs this)* |
| "Your bank turned you down?" | "Banks show you one option. We show you the market." *(Mortgage Alliance)* |

The borrower still self-selects — *they* know the ad is for them. We just never say it out loud.
**This is a hard law for every hook below, not a style preference.**

## 4. The three real gaps

### ⭐ GAP 1 — The commercial / investor borrower. **0 of 137 ads.**
Every ad in the entire field is residential: rate, refi, HELOC, first-timer, reverse. **Not one** speaks to
someone buying a fourplex, flipping a house, running a BRRRR, or financing a build.

TheRateFinder does **$200k–$150M**, **50+ commercial lenders**, fix & flip, BRRRR, construction, multi-unit.
Nobody is competing for this buyer on Meta. The deal sizes dwarf a residential refi, and the buyer is a
**repeat customer**, not a one-off.

**Honest risk:** the lane may be empty because commercial deals come from broker/realtor relationships, not
paid social. That's a real possibility — but small investors (flips, BRRRR, duplexes) *are* on Instagram in
force, the deal economics are big enough to absorb a high CPA, and **it is cheap to find out.** This is a
test, not a certainty. It's the highest-upside test on the board.

### ⭐ GAP 2 — Speed. Nobody sells time-to-close.
Only 10 of 137 ads mention time at all, and none make it the point. The **strongest speed claim in the
entire field** is buried inside a 360Lending customer testimonial: *"approved for $160,000 in **less than
2 weeks**."*

TheRateFinder's site claims **rush closings in 24–48 hours**. If that is true, it is **~7× faster than the
best proof point any competitor has published** — and speed is:
- **policy-safe** (it describes *our* process, never the borrower's condition),
- **legible** (a number a human instantly understands),
- **exactly what the deadline borrower buys** — the person whose financing condition expires Friday is not
  shopping on price.

> ⚠️ **Must verify with the client before we render a single pixel.** If "24–48 hours" is marketing gloss
> rather than an operational fact, this whole lane collapses and we do not touch it. Never fabricate.

### ⭐ GAP 3 — Show the receipt. Nobody does.
360Lending gets closest with a *customer quote* about a deal. **Nobody shows the deal itself.**
No one in the field puts up: *property type · amount · city · days to close · why the bank passed.*

TheRateFinder's website **already has six funded deals with amounts and locations.** That is the exact
analogue of the Verbis score-card — and on Verbis, the **proof creative was the best-performing ad of the
campaign (2.36× ROAS)** while every "clever" concept underperformed. Proof beats art in a trust purchase,
and a mortgage is the biggest trust purchase we've worked on.

## 5. The hooks (each one = a gap + the subject-swap law)

> All copy below is **provisional** — it is written against the *site's* claims. Every number must be
> confirmed by the client before it is rendered. Nothing here gets fabricated.

### Track A — SPEED (GAP 2) · *the deadline borrower*
- **A1 — "Closed in 48 hours."** The funded-deal receipt, but the hero is the **clock**, not the dollar
  amount. Construction: a real deal card — *$1.4M · commercial · Mississauga · **funded in 48 hours***.
- **A2 — "Your financing condition expires Friday."** Names the *deadline*, not the person's trouble
  (policy-safe: it's a date, not a diagnosis). The one moment when a borrower will pay any price.
- **A3 — "Banks measure approvals in weeks. We measure them in hours."** The comparison mechanic (True
  North's table, Miracle's 19.9%-vs-4.99%) — but the axis is **TIME**, which nobody else competes on.

### Track B — THE RECEIPT (GAP 3) · *proof, the thing that actually converted for Verbis*
- **B1 — The funded-deal wall.** Four or six real deal cards, grid-tiled: amount · property · city · days.
  No stock photos, no promises. Just receipts. *This is the proofwall, and I would bet on it.*
- **B2 — "The bank had one box. This deal didn't fit it. It funded anyway."** Tells the *deal's* story —
  the subject is the **file**, never the borrower. Legal, and it lands with everyone who's been declined.
- **B3 — 350 lenders, shown as a wall of lenders vs. one bank logo.** ⚠️ *Only if we can out-execute
  360Lending's "YOUR BANK vs 50+ LENDERS" — they own this and they're in Vaughan.* Our edge is **350 vs
  their 50** — a 7× number. Otherwise skip.

### Track C — THE COMMERCIAL LANE (GAP 1) · *the empty street*
- **C1 — "Your bank doesn't do fourplexes. 50 commercial lenders do."** Speaks to the investor nobody
  else is speaking to. Subject = the lenders, not the borrower.
- **C2 — "$200K to $150M."** The range *is* the ad. Instantly disqualifies the rate-shopper and qualifies
  the serious buyer — which is exactly what we need, because **Meta's Special Ad Category won't let us
  target them.** In a category where we can't aim, the creative *is* the targeting.
- **C3 — The BRRRR / fix-and-flip cycle.** Speak the investor's own vocabulary. Nobody else does — the
  entire field is talking to homeowners.

## 6. What I'd actually do

1. **Do not enter the rate lane.** We'd bring a 4.19% to a 2.49% fight.
2. **Lead with Track B (the receipt).** It's the proven winner-shape from Verbis, we already have the raw
   material on the client's own website, and nobody in the field is doing it.
3. **Test Track C (commercial) as the high-upside swing.** An empty lane, a repeat buyer, huge deal sizes,
   and a creative that self-selects — which matters enormously, because Special Ad Category means Meta
   won't let us target this person by any attribute. The ad has to do the aiming.
4. **Use Track A (speed) as the urgency layer** across both — *if and only if* the client confirms 24–48h
   is real.
5. **Obey the subject swap everywhere.** Never name the borrower's condition. Always name the lender's box,
   the deadline, or our capability.

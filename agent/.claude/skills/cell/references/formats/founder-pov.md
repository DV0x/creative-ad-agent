# Founder-POV — a DR format entry

A **format** is a proven job/structure, not a look. This document carries only what is *founder-POV-specific*: the job it does, the copy it needs, the material it requires, and what to forbid. It does **not** decide the look. The look is *derived* — by the shared method in [`../layer-stack.md`](../layer-stack.md): read the message's provenance → name one real maker → resolve every element from it (the look via [`../style-grammar.md`](../style-grammar.md), the lettering via [`../type-grammar.md`](../type-grammar.md)). **Machine, not skin:** the format is the machine (the founder's job and copy); the look comes from the maker, never from a fixed skin or a brand-color lookup.

Two founders' POV ads look nothing alike — not because the format varies, but because each is filled with that founder's real face, real voice, real origin, and the real artifact their message lives in.

---

## Philosophy — the job, and the cut that builds everything below

**The job: a real person closes the sale.** A stranger trusts a human who *built the thing* — who had the problem, who stakes their own name — more than they trust a brand. So the ad drops the brand voice entirely and lets the founder talk straight to the buyer.

This is the **cold-start** format. Its power does not depend on customer proof existing yet — which is exactly why it is the opposite of testimonial. Where testimonial **selects** a customer's verbatim words, founder-POV **authors** the founder's message in the founder's own voice. The honesty rule therefore moves: not "never paraphrase," but **"the story must be true and the voice must be the founder's real register."**

Everything founder-POV-specific falls out of one cut — what is **structurally necessary**, with everything else forbidden:

- **Necessary** (these four, nothing else): the **FOUNDER** (a real person, shown) · the **REASON** (the true origin — the problem or belief that forced this product into existence) · the **ANSWER** (the product, as the founder's response to that reason) · the **ASK** (a plain CTA).
- **Forbidden** (the same cut's other half): a stock-model "founder," corporate buzzword copy, a fabricated origin, a "story" that is secretly a feature list, studio-polished portraiture that erases the person.

> **The feel that governs the format:** it must read like *a person talking to you*, not a brand advertising at you. The moment it looks like a corporate ad, the trust evaporates — the entire asset is "a real human stands behind this."

---

## Match — when the cell reaches for this format

The **angle decides the format.** The founder never picks it; the cell matches it. Reach for founder-POV when both are true:

- **The brand's real strength is the founder** — their authenticity, mission, expertise, or origin is something a polished ad cannot manufacture, and a rival cannot copy.
- **The want is "can I trust this brand?"** — typical of unaware → problem-aware cold traffic meeting a new or small brand, *and* the specific case where **no review material exists yet** (so testimonial is off the table) but a real founder with a true reason does. This is the format's home lane.

**Awareness tunes the emphasis** (a dial, not a different format): cold → lead with the **relatable problem**, founder as fellow-sufferer ("I got tired of X too"); warm/hot → lead with the **mission / what they stand for**, and the CTA carries more weight.

### Material gate (hard — this gate is the format's honesty)

This format **requires a real founder willing to be shown and a true origin reason.** No real founder, or no true story → you **cannot** pick founder-POV. A fabricated founder or an invented origin is not weak creative; it is fraud — the one unforgivable sin of this format, the exact parallel to a faked review in testimonial. If there is no real founder story, route to a format whose gate you can meet — or, if none of the library molds fits, **build freestyle on the shared method** (`../layer-stack.md`). Never force a mold, and never invent the founder or the story to fill the format.

**Asset requirement (new — founder-POV needs *two* references, not one):** like every format it binds the real **product** image; it *also* binds the real **founder likeness** (their own photo). The founder's own face is theirs to use — legal *and* honest — unlike a borrowed stranger's face, which is a rights problem (`../layer-stack.md`, the weld's legal floor). With no real founder image, the render has no honest face to show; that is the gate, not a detail.

## The copy skeleton — named slots, each tracing to the job

A format is a **copy skeleton**: named slots, each filled from the brand's real content. Every slot traces to one of the four necessary elements — if a slot can't trace, it's cut.

| Slot | Required? | Source | The necessary element it serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Founder identity** | required | real name · role · face | **THE FOUNDER** — the human the eye meets |
| **Reason line** | required | true origin, authored in voice | **THE REASON** — the problem they couldn't accept |
| **Answer line** | required | the product as response, authored | **THE ANSWER** — what they did about it |
| **Skin-in-the-game chip** | 0–1, flex | real (uses it daily · guarantee · credential) | reinforces the trust on a *new axis* |
| **Product** | required | real product image | **THE ANSWER**, made concrete |
| **CTA** | required | written, plain | **THE ASK** |

**This is the inversion from testimonial — the copy is *authored*, not selected.** The reason and answer are written in the founder's real voice: **verbatim from a real founder line when one exists** (a real post, a line they've actually said), **authored from the true story when one doesn't.** The *story* is always true; only the phrasing is composed. This is the opposite pole from testimonial's most-verbatim discipline — and the reason this format is the mold's real test.

**The format bends to the material — never the reverse:**

- **The artifact follows what the founder will give.** Founder shown on camera → the strongest, most native version (a real photo). Founder won't show their face → the message shifts to a **handwritten note** or a **voice-as-caption over the product**; the story still carries. Drop slots that aren't real; **never pad.**
- **A skin-in-the-game chip must add a NEW axis** (the founder *uses* it / *guarantees* it), not restate the reason.
- **The reason must be a true, specific origin** — "I read the label and couldn't pronounce half of it" — not the generic, swappable "we care about quality." The specificity is what makes it un-runnable by a rival.

## What the format owns visually — and what it does NOT

The format owns only the **job-structure** — the two rules that come from "a real person closes the sale":

1. **The founder leads.** The eye meets a *person* first — a real face, a real hand, or (faceless) the founder's unmistakable personal artifact (their note, their voice).
2. **The product is the founder's *answer*.** Held, made, or shown *by the founder* — their thing, not a detached packshot. The moment the product floats alone, this stops being founder-POV and becomes a product ad.

**Everything else is NOT a format decision** — not the type, not the light, not the setting, not the chrome. Those are *derived* by the shared method:

- **Read the provenance → name the maker.** Where does the founder's message actually live? A talking-head clip → **a real selfie-video still with burned-in captions** (UGC-native). A personal note → **handwritten on real paper.** A "why I built this" post → **the platform's own post chrome.** The founder in their real workspace → **a documentary photo.** The maker decides the rest.
- **Resolve the look in [`../style-grammar.md`](../style-grammar.md)** — usually **photographic-real** (a real selfie or documentary photo) or **borrowed** (a real social post); **the lettering in [`../type-grammar.md`](../type-grammar.md)** — usually **system/native** (video captions, post UI) or **handwritten** (the note). One maker decides both.
- **Default to native.** A real founder selfie beats a studio "founder portrait" — it reads as a person, not a campaign. Polish is the exception, only for a brand whose whole positioning is polish (`../layer-stack.md`, step 3).
- **The honesty line:** a *real* founder shown as a real person is truthful and native; a stock-model "founder" or an AI-perfect face is the forbidden version.

## What this format hands the shot spec

After matching and filling, the cell carries forward the back-half contract (`../shot-spec.md`). The format pre-fills the **content**; the shared method fills the **maker/treatment**:

- **Claim** — the promise the founder's answer makes.
- **On-image copy (placed)** — reason line · answer line · founder name/role chip · CTA. (Authored in the founder's voice; verbatim from a real founder line where one exists.)
- **Objects** — the **founder** (real, shown) · the real **product** (bound to reference) · the **artifact** the message lives in (note paper / video frame / post chrome) · nothing else.
- **Action** — a caught human moment: the founder mid-sentence to camera, writing the note, holding the product in their space. *(Unlike testimonial's held-artifact stillness, founder-POV usually carries a real verb — another structural difference the mold must handle.)*
- **Composition** — founder leads, product is the answer (the two job-rules above); the rest from the maker.
- **Product binding** — the real product reference; what must stay true (colorway, wordmark, badges, the exact spec number — pixels outrank prose).
- **Founder binding** — the real founder-likeness reference; the render shows *that* person, not a generated lookalike.
- **Staging record** — the **maker** named from the provenance, carried verbatim from the derivation (not chosen here).
- **Must-show** — the founder (real person / their artifact) · the reason or answer line · the real product · CTA.
- **Forbid** — the job's other half (below), compiled into positive decisions.

Then the back half runs unchanged: **compile-verbatim → bind references → render once → we look.**

---

## No worked example — on purpose

A fully-resolved example (setting, light, props, and CTA all named) would act as a **template**: the next run reaches for *that surface* instead of deriving its own, and the swap test fails by construction — it is precisely how a prior render's specifics get cloned onto an unrelated brand. This entry teaches the **moves**, not a picture of one output. Derive every element from *this* brand's real provenance via [`../layer-stack.md`](../layer-stack.md); the finished look is the *output* of that derivation, never an input borrowed from here.

## Forbid / anti-patterns (the other half of the job-cut)

| Never | Why it isn't the job | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| a stock-model / hired "founder" | a paid face is the opposite of a *real* person | the real founder, or their real artifact |
| corporate buzzword copy ("passionate about quality") | sounds like a brand, not a human | the founder's actual register, specific |
| a fabricated origin story | inventing a reason is fraud (the material gate exists to stop this) | the true reason, or change format |
| a feature list dressed as a "story" | features aren't a *why* | the real problem or belief that forced it |
| a polished studio "founder portrait" | reads as *made*, not as a person talking | a real selfie / talking-head / note |
| an AI-perfect, too-smooth face | uncanny; reads as a fake person | real human texture (bind the real founder photo) |
| a founder face with no product/answer | a face that sells nothing | the founder *and* their answer (the product) |
| a generic "our journey" nostalgia montage | borrowed attention, no specific claim | one specific true reason, one founder |

## Checklist — before the render

- [ ] Founder is real; **material gate passed** (real founder + a true origin, zero fabrication)
- [ ] **Founder-likeness reference bound** (their real photo) — the same discipline as the product reference
- [ ] Copy is in the founder's **actual voice** (verbatim from a real line when one exists; authored-in-voice otherwise) — a *person*, not a brand
- [ ] The reason is a **true, specific** origin, not a generic "we care"
- [ ] **Maker named from the provenance** (talking-head still / note / post), not a studio portrait
- [ ] Product **bound to the real reference**; spec fact verified (24g, not 29g); listing overlays excluded (ribbons/badges that live on the website, not the physical pack)
- [ ] Founder leads; product is their **answer** (not a detached packshot)
- [ ] **Every element swept and named** — camera, setting, light, captions, CTA (nothing vague)
- [ ] Reads like **a person talking to you**, not a corporate ad
- [ ] **Native by default**; nothing stock or AI-perfect; nothing from the forbid list
- [ ] **Generated fresh** from the prompt + product + founder references (not edited off a prior render)

**The swap test (the cell's bar):** could a rival run this with a logo swap? With Shashank's real face + the true "couldn't pronounce the ingredients" origin tied to TWT's transparency DNA — no. With a generic "I'm passionate about health" founder line — yes, and it fails. **Genericness lives in the content (the specific true story), not the structure.** The skeleton is the proven floor; the real founder + true reason is what makes it un-swappable. You need both.

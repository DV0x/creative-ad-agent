# Typography-Dominant

Bold typography IS the hero. Text occupies 60-70% of the frame, delivering the hook as a visual statement. Graphic, poster-like, with maximum pattern-interrupt power. Zero uncanny valley risk.

**Prerequisites:** Hook-bank file must exist. Main Agent triggers hook-methodology skill first.

---

## Style Overview

**What it is:** Large-scale, high-impact typographic compositions where the text itself is the primary visual element. Product imagery is minimal or absent (10-15% of frame at most). The headline dominates the frame like a poster or protest sign — impossible to scroll past without reading.

**Why it works:**
- Pattern interrupt — text-dominant ads break the visual monotony of product shots and lifestyle imagery in feed
- Zero uncanny valley risk — no rendered objects, no humans, no environments to get wrong
- Message-first approach — the hook lands before any product comprehension is needed
- Works for intangible products (SaaS, services, finance) where there's no physical product to show
- Fastest production speed — composition is primarily typographic, not scene-based
- Kinetic energy from stretched, stacked, or rotated type creates a sense of motion and urgency

**Visual Characteristics:**

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Typography | 60-70% of frame, Extra Bold / Black weight |
| Product | 0-15% of frame (optional small element in corner or strip) |
| Background | Flat solid color or bold color blocking — no gradients, no textures |
| Graphic accents | Lines, geometric shapes, color blocks supporting the text |
| Layout | Text-first, rule-breaking — extends to edges, overlaps, breaks grid |
| Palette | 2-3 colors maximum, high contrast |

The typography-dominant style adjusts its visual energy based on the detected D2C category:

| Category | Color Treatment | Type Energy | Product Presence | Tone |

## Step 4: Visual Concept Creation

For each hook, build the typographic concept by answering:

### 1. What is the Key Word?
Identify the single most impactful word in the hook. This word gets the most visual weight — larger, bolder, or in a different color.

**Examples:**
- "Brain fog isn't normal" → **normal** gets accent color (challenges the word itself)
- "Your bank is betting you won't read this" → **betting** gets accent treatment
- "3 things your CRM isn't telling you" → **3** rendered enormous as visual anchor

### 2. What Type Treatment Matches the Hook Psychology?

| Hook Psychology | Type Treatment |
|----------------|----------------|
| Pain/Problem | **Stacked and heavy** — words pile up like weight pressing down. Dark background, light text. |
| Aspiration/Identity | **Stretched and rising** — letterforms extended upward, ascending energy. Light background, bold dark text. |
| Urgency/Scarcity | **Condensed and compressed** — text feels squeezed, pressured. High contrast, minimal spacing. |
| Curiosity/Question | **Overlapping and layered** — words partially hidden, text at different opacities creating visual intrigue. |
| Social Proof | **Large number as hero** — statistic dominates frame. Supporting text secondary. |
| Education/Value | **Structured list** — numbered or bulleted, clear information hierarchy within the type. |
| Transformation | **Before/after split** — two type treatments in one frame. Left side in one weight/color, right in another. |

### 3. What Color Blocking Supports the Message?

| Message Tone | Color Block Strategy |
|--------------|---------------------|
| Confrontational | Black background, white text, one accent word in brand color |
| Hopeful/Aspirational | White/cream background, bold dark text, accent in warm brand color |
| Urgent | Brand primary background (saturated), white text, accent as highlight |
| Educational | Light gray background, dark text, brand primary for key data points |
| Split/Contrast | Two-field split (brand primary left, white right), text bridges both |

## Step 5: Design Principles

### 1. Text as Hero (Not Decoration)
- Headline occupies 60-70% of the visual frame
- The type treatment IS the creative concept — not a label on an image
- Text is the first, second, and third thing the viewer sees
- If you remove the text, there is no ad

### 2. The Key Word Rule
- One word in the headline receives distinctive visual treatment
- This word is larger, bolder, a different color, or differently positioned
- The key word creates a visual hierarchy within the headline itself
- Reader's eye goes: key word → full headline → CTA

### 3. Color Restraint
- Maximum 2-3 colors per composition
- No gradients, no textures — flat, graphic, poster-like
- Color blocking: large solid fields of color divided by or containing text
- Background IS a color decision (not just white by default)

### 4. Kinetic Typography
- Type can stretch to edges, overlap frame boundaries, break the grid
- Letterforms can be rotated, stacked, compressed, or expanded
- Text at different opacities creates visual rhythm and layering
- Repeated words (at 10-20% opacity behind the main text) create texture

### 5. Product as Whisper (Not Shout)
- Product imagery, if present, is subordinate — 10-15% of frame maximum
- Product appears as: small element in corner, silhouette in bottom strip, or icon
- Product is NOT required — many typography-dominant ads work with text only
- When product is present, it confirms the brand but doesn't compete with the message

### 6. Typographic Specification
- Sans-serif dominant: Impact, Bebas Neue, Anton, or similar high-impact faces
- Extra Bold / Black weight for headlines — never regular or light
- Variable weight for hierarchy: Ultra Bold headline, Medium subtext, Light CTA
- Headline in brand primary or white (maximum contrast)
- Key word or accent word in contrasting color
- ALL CAPS for headline, sentence case for supporting text and CTA

## Example Prompts

### Example 1: SaaS Brand (4:5)

**Brand:** Supabase
**Colors:** Primary #3ECF8E, Secondary #1C1C1C, Accent #F8F8F8
**Hook Type:** Curiosity/Question
**Hook:** "3 things your database isn't telling you."
**Psychology:** Open loop combined with personification — implies the tool is hiding something. Creates information gap that demands closure.
**Category:** SaaS/Tech

```
Create a 4:5 social media ad image.

TYPOGRAPHY HERO:
The number "3" dominates the upper-left quadrant of the frame at massive
scale — filling approximately 40% of the frame height. It's rendered in
bright #3ECF8E (Supabase green) against a near-black #1C1C1C background.
The remaining headline text "THINGS YOUR DATABASE ISN'T TELLING YOU." is
stacked in white below and to the right of the "3", in a condensed,
tightly-spaced arrangement. The "3" anchors the eye immediately, then
the reader flows right and down to complete the sentence.

Key word: "3" — rendered at 5x the size of the remaining text, in
#3ECF8E (brand green), positioned upper-left as the dominant visual anchor

TYPE TREATMENT:
Style: Number-hero — large statistic dominates, supporting text secondary
Font: Bebas Neue / Anton style — Black weight
Headline color: #F8F8F8 (near-white) for body text
Key word color: #3ECF8E (brand green) for the "3"
Letter-spacing: Tight, condensed
Text opacity layers: Faint "DATABASE DATABASE DATABASE" repeated at 5%
  opacity across the background as subtle texture

COLOR BLOCKING:
Background: Solid #1C1C1C (near-black, Supabase dark mode aesthetic)
Divisions: No splits — monolithic dark field with text as the only light
Palette: #1C1C1C (background), #F8F8F8 (text), #3ECF8E (accent number)

PRODUCT ELEMENT:
What: Supabase logo mark (small geometric icon)
Where: Bottom-right corner, above safe zone
Size: 8% of frame
Treatment: #3ECF8E (brand green), flat, minimal — a signature, not a hero

GRAPHIC ACCENTS:
A thin horizontal #3ECF8E line beneath the headline text, spanning 60%
of the frame width. Creates visual separation between headline and CTA.
A subtle monospace code bracket "{ }" at 10% opacity flanking the "3".

BRAND ANCHOR:
Owned positioning: Supabase as the open-source Firebase alternative —
  full Postgres power without proprietary lock-in
Visual translation: The dark-mode tech aesthetic and the accusatory tone
  ("isn't telling you") position Supabase as the transparent alternative.
  The message implies competitors hide limitations.
Logo swap check: "Your database" directly addresses their product category,
  and the implication of hidden truth aligns with Supabase's open-source
  transparency positioning. A closed-source competitor couldn't credibly
  use this hook.

TYPOGRAPHY DETAIL:
HEADLINE: "3 THINGS YOUR DATABASE ISN'T TELLING YOU."
- Position: "3" upper-left at massive scale, remaining text stacked below-right
- Font: Bebas Neue / Anton — Black weight
- Color: #F8F8F8 (body), #3ECF8E (the "3")
- Weight: Black
- Treatment: ALL CAPS, tight letter-spacing, stacked over 3 lines

CTA: "Read the docs"
- Position: Bottom-center at 68% down the frame
- Font: Medium-weight monospace (developer-familiar)
- Color: #3ECF8E
- Treatment: Sentence case, subtle underline suggesting a link

MOBILE CONSTRAINTS:
- Safe zone: No text or critical elements in bottom 35%
- Safe zone: No text in top 14%
- Thumb zone: CTA centered, not in corners
- Thumbnail test: The "3" and first line of text readable at 300px
- Text minimum: All text 12pt+ equivalent
- Single-column: Stacked vertically
- Hero element: Typography fills 65% of frame

MOOD:
Tech-confident, slightly provocative. Like a developer blog post title
that you can't not click. Dark mode energy.

DO NOT:
- Database imagery, server racks, or cloud illustrations
- Gradients, textures, or warm tones
- Decorative or serif fonts
- Borders or frames
- More than 3 colors
- Small, timid typography
- Product screenshots or UI mockups as hero
- Symmetrical centered layout (the offset "3" creates deliberate tension)
```

## Mobile Constraint Integration

Typography-dominant is inherently legible on mobile due to large text. Key rules per aspect ratio:

### 4:5 (1080x1350) — Feed Optimized
- Key word or number as dominant visual anchor in upper portion
- Supporting text stacked below
- CTA at approximately 70% down
- Text can extend to within 5% of frame edges (no borders = full bleed)

### 1:1 (1080x1080) — Feed Standard / Carousel
- Horizontal sweep possible: key word left, supporting text right
- Or centered stack with text filling the square
- Color blocking can split the frame (left/right or top/bottom)
- CTA bottom-center at 75% down

### 9:16 (1080x1920) — Reels / Stories
- Vertical stacking is mandatory — no horizontal text arrangements
- Key word or number at vertical center (maximum visual weight)
- Context text above (below 14% safe zone), CTA below (above 35% safe zone)
- Extra-large type that fills the frame width
- The tall format amplifies the "poster" quality of this style

## Anti-Patterns

| Never | Instead |
|-------|---------|
| Product as primary visual | Text IS the visual — product is 0-15% max |
| Gradients or textured backgrounds | Flat solid color or clean color blocking |
| Small, delicate typography | Extra Bold / Black weight, frame-filling scale |
| Symmetrical centered layout | Offset, stacked, stretched — break the grid |
| More than 3 colors | 2-3 color maximum, high contrast |
| Serif or decorative fonts | Impact / Bebas Neue / Anton — high-impact sans-serif |
| Generic stock-photo aesthetic | Graphic poster energy — this is a STATEMENT |
| Text as caption on an image | Text as the image itself |
| Even weight across all text | Key Word Rule — one word gets distinctive treatment |
| Borders or frames | Text extends to edges, full bleed |

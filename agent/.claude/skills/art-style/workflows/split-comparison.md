# Split Comparison

Dual-composition ads with contrasting halves — before/after, problem/solution, us-vs-them. The product bridges the divide while the headline spans both worlds. Photorealistic mixed-media built on the 3-layer foundation: photo base, graphic design frame, typography + info.

**Prerequisites:** Hook-bank file must exist. Main Agent triggers hook-methodology skill first.

---

## Style Overview

**What it is:** A bisected composition dividing the frame into two contrasting halves — one representing the problem state (muted, desaturated, negative) and one representing the solution state (vivid, warm, positive). A photorealistic product shot straddles the split line, physically bridging the two worlds. The headline spans both halves. Per-side benefit callouts reinforce the contrast. The split edge itself (clean, diagonal, torn, or gradient) becomes a design element. Think Hims, HexClad, athletic before/after ads.

**Why it works:**
- The human eye is drawn to contrast — two opposing halves create immediate visual tension
- Before/after framing is a proven direct-response structure (the viewer places themselves on one side)
- Product-as-bridge positioning makes the brand the literal solution between two states
- Per-side callouts let you name the pain AND the benefit in one frame without clutter
- The split edge creates a natural focal point that draws the eye to the product
- Comparison framing taps into loss aversion — the "without" half motivates action

**Visual Characteristics:**

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Layout | Bisected composition — two contrasting halves |
| Split treatment | Vertical split (left/right), diagonal split, or broken split (irregular edge) |
| Left half | Problem / Before / Without — muted, desaturated, or negative tone |
| Right half | Solution / After / With — vivid, warm, positive tone |
| Product | Bridges the two halves — positioned at the split line |
| Headline | Spans the divide — text crosses both halves |
| Benefit callouts | Side-specific — one callout per half, contrasting the two states |

## Step 2: Brand Anchor Derivation

Follow the **Brand Anchor Derivation** framework in SKILL.md with this style-specific guidance:

**Principle:** In split comparison, brand specificity comes through the contrast narrative — the specific problem state only this brand solves, and the specific outcome only this brand delivers. The two halves must tell this brand's story, not a generic before/after.

**Style-specific translation (step 2):**
- The left half (problem) should depict the specific frustration this brand's ICP experiences — not a universal complaint, but the exact pain point the brand's research identifies
- The right half (solution) should showcase the specific outcome this brand enables — tied to the owned positioning, not a generic "better life"
- The product at the split line should be recognizable as this brand's specific product (shape, color, packaging) — it is the literal bridge between the two states

## Step 5: Design Principles

### 1. The Divide IS the Design
- The split line is not an afterthought — it is the primary compositional element
- The eye follows the split line to the product, which straddles the divide
- Everything radiates from the split: callouts on each side, headline crossing it, product anchoring it
- The split creates immediate narrative tension that demands resolution
- Position the split at approximately 40-60% of the frame width (never dead center — one side should dominate slightly)

### 2. Contrast Through Color Temperature
- Left half (problem): cool tones — desaturated, gray-blue, muted, flat lighting
- Right half (solution): warm tones — saturated, golden-amber, vivid, directional lighting
- The temperature shift should be dramatic enough to register at thumbnail size
- The product should carry warm tones even where it overlaps the cool side
- Never use the same color temperature on both halves — the contrast is the point

### 3. Product as Bridge
- The product physically straddles the split line — never entirely on one side
- Product overlaps into the solution half by approximately 60% and into the problem half by 40%
- The product is the largest single element at the split line (25-35% of frame height)
- The product is photorealistic, sharp, and fully saturated — it belongs to the solution world but reaches into the problem world
- Product lighting matches the solution side (warm) even where it extends into the problem side

### 4. Headline Spans Both Worlds
- The headline text physically crosses the split line — starting on one half and ending on the other
- Headline color shifts with the split: muted on the problem side, vivid on the solution side (or white throughout if both backgrounds are dark enough)
- Position the headline in the upper 20-35% of the frame, above the product
- The headline crossing the divide reinforces the connection between the two states

### 5. Per-Side Callouts
- Each half gets exactly one short callout (3-7 words) summarizing that side's state
- Left callout: names the problem, frustration, or "before" state
- Right callout: names the benefit, relief, or "after" state
- Callouts are positioned symmetrically relative to the split (same vertical height, mirrored horizontal position)
- Left callout in muted/cool color, right callout in vivid/warm color
- Callouts use smaller text than the headline — supporting, not competing

### 6. Split Edge Character
- The edge style carries meaning and should match the brand tone:
  - **Clean vertical** — precision, clinical, tech, skincare. A sharp line dividing two worlds.
  - **Diagonal** — energy, dynamism, progress, forward motion. Implies movement from problem to solution.
  - **Broken/torn** — transformation, disruption, breakthrough. The product burst through.
  - **Gradient transition** — gentle transformation, wellness, organic change. The shift is a spectrum, not a wall.
- The edge should be visible at thumbnail size — not so subtle it disappears
- On the edge itself, objects from one side can slightly bleed into the other (a leaf, a particle, a light ray) to prevent the composition from feeling like two separate images glued together

## Mobile Constraint Integration

Split comparison layouts use the divide as a natural scanning structure. Key rules per aspect ratio:

### 4:5 (1080x1350) — Feed Optimized
- Vertical split (left/right) at approximately 45/55 position
- Headline in upper 20-25% crossing the split
- Product at split line, vertically centered at 40-55% down
- Per-side callouts at 50% down, 15-20% from each edge
- CTA at 62-68% down, centered or on the solution side
- Split contrast must register in the feed thumbnail

### 1:1 (1080x1080) — Feed Standard / Carousel
- Vertical split (left/right) at approximately 45/55 or 55/45 position
- Headline in upper 20-25% crossing the split
- Product at split line, vertically centered at 45-55% down
- Per-side callouts at 50-55% down
- CTA at 70-78% down, centered
- The square format gives equal visual weight — rely on color contrast to differentiate halves

### 9:16 (1080x1920) — Reels / Stories
- **Horizontal split (top/bottom)** instead of vertical — the tall format makes left/right halves too narrow
- Problem half occupies the top 40-45%, solution half occupies the bottom 55-60%
- Split line runs horizontally at 40-45% down from top
- Product straddles the horizontal split line, centered horizontally
- Headline at 18-25% down (in the problem half, above the product)
- Top callout in the problem zone at 30% down
- Bottom callout in the solution zone at 55% down
- CTA at 60% down (above 65% safe zone boundary)
- Single-column layout — all elements centered horizontally

## Anti-Patterns

| Never | Instead |
|-------|---------|
| Equal/symmetric halves with identical visual weight | One side dominates slightly (55/45 or 60/40) to create directional pull |
| Naming competitor brands on the problem side | Generic "without" framing — plain jar, unlabeled product, status quo description |
| Both halves at the same color temperature | Dramatic warm/cool contrast — the temperature shift IS the split |
| Product placed entirely on one side | Product straddles the split line — 40/60 overlap ratio |
| Photorealistic human faces or bodies | Objects, textures, and products only — avoid uncanny valley |
| Split edge invisible at thumbnail size | Edge must register as a clear contrast boundary at 300px width |
| Both halves showing the same mood | Problem half is muted/negative, solution half is vivid/positive |
| Callouts that repeat the headline | Callouts add side-specific detail that the headline does not contain |
| Cluttered problem side with too many objects | Problem side is muted and sparse — emptiness conveys the lack |
| Product smaller than 20% of frame height | Product is the anchor at the split — 25-35% of frame height |

## Quality Checklist

### Split Structure
- [ ] Frame is divided into two visually distinct halves
- [ ] One side dominates slightly (not a perfect 50/50)
- [ ] Split edge is visible at thumbnail size (300px)
- [ ] Split edge style matches brand tone (clean/diagonal/torn/gradient)

### Left Half (Problem)
- [ ] Cool color temperature — desaturated, muted, flat
- [ ] Mood is clearly negative (frustration, stagnation, deficiency)
- [ ] Per-side callout names the specific problem (3-7 words)
- [ ] No competitor brand names visible
- [ ] Sparse and controlled — not chaotic or cluttered

### Right Half (Solution)
- [ ] Warm color temperature — saturated, vivid, directional light
- [ ] Mood is clearly positive (relief, energy, clarity)
- [ ] Per-side callout names the specific benefit (3-7 words)
- [ ] Callout is visually distinct from left callout (color, weight)

### Product
- [ ] Product straddles the split line (not entirely on one side)
- [ ] Product is 25-35% of frame height
- [ ] Product is photorealistic, sharp, and fully saturated
- [ ] Product lighting matches solution side even where it overlaps problem side
- [ ] Product is recognizable as this specific brand (Logo Swap Test)

### Typography
- [ ] Headline crosses the split line — text spans both halves
- [ ] Headline is in the upper 20-35% of frame
- [ ] Per-side callouts are vertically aligned with each other
- [ ] CTA is on or near the solution side
- [ ] All text 12pt+ equivalent at display size

### Mobile
Follow shared mobile checklist in SKILL.md § Mobile Safe Zone Rules.
- [ ] Split contrast visible at 300px width
- [ ] Product and headline readable at thumbnail size

### Composition
- [ ] Color temperature contrast is dramatic (not subtle)
- [ ] No elements from one half accidentally bleed into the other (except intentional edge details)
- [ ] The composition reads as one unified ad, not two separate images side by side

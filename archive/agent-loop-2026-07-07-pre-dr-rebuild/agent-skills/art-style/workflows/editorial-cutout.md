# Editorial Cutout

Magazine-editorial aesthetic with artistic cut-out edges, selective color treatment, and collage-influenced layered composition. Product presented like a design object in a curated editorial spread. Asymmetric, intentional, premium.

**Prerequisites:** Hook-bank file must exist. Main Agent triggers hook-methodology skill first.

---

## Style Overview

**What it is:** Product isolated with artistic cut-out edges on a layered, collage-inspired composition. Selective color treatment places the product in vivid brand colors against a muted, desaturated, or tinted background. Overlapping geometric shapes (circles, rectangles) frame and interact with the product. Paper or linen texture backgrounds add tactile quality. The overall feel is a curated magazine spread — asymmetric, design-forward, premium.

**Why it works:**
- Asymmetric editorial layout breaks the scroll pattern differently than symmetrical ads
- Selective color draws the eye to the product without environmental distraction
- Serif typography signals premium positioning and editorial authority
- Collage composition creates visual depth through flat layering (no 3D rendering needed)
- White space as a design element signals confidence and restraint
- Multiple product angles or detail views in one frame increase product comprehension

**Visual Characteristics:**

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Cut-out edges | Artistic, slightly rough (not pixel-perfect) — intentionally imperfect |
| Color treatment | Selective saturation: product in full vivid color, surroundings muted |
| Geometric overlays | Circles, rectangles, arcs overlapping and framing the product |
| Background texture | Paper, linen, or canvas texture for tactile quality |
| Layout | Asymmetric, deliberate visual tension, editorial grid-breaking |
| White space | Active design element — intentional, generous, confident |
| Typography | Serif or serif/sans-serif pairing, Title Case, editorial voice |

## Step 4: Visual Concept Creation

For each hook, build the editorial composition by answering:

### 1. What is the Hero Cut-Out?

The main product image, isolated with artistic edges:
- **Clean-edge cut-out:** Precise but slightly hand-cut feel (1-2px rough edge)
- **Rough-edge cut-out:** Deliberately torn or scissored look (more collage-like)
- **Torn-paper cut-out:** Product emerges from a torn paper layer (reveals color beneath)

Select based on brand positioning:
- Premium/luxury: clean-edge
- Creative/artisan: rough-edge
- Bold/disruptive: torn-paper

### 2. What Secondary Elements Tell the Story?

Editorial layouts support multiple visual elements in one frame. Select 2-3 from:
- **Ingredient close-up:** Macro crop of a key ingredient or material
- **Texture detail:** Surface quality of the product (weave, grain, finish)
- **Color swatch:** Branded color chips or gradient strip
- **Process crop:** Manufacturing or sourcing visual (coffee beans, botanical garden, lab equipment)
- **Before/after crop:** Transformation evidence at a detail level
- **Pull-quote card:** Key benefit text in editorial serif on a tinted shape

### 3. How Do Geometric Overlays Frame the Composition?

Overlapping shapes create layered depth without 3D rendering:
- Shapes use brand secondary or muted neutral colors at 50-80% opacity
- Shapes overlap the product cut-out slightly (10-20% overlap)
- At least one shape extends beyond the frame edge (editorial convention — implies the design continues)
- Shapes establish visual rhythm: repeating a circle 2-3 times at different sizes

### 4. Hook Psychology to Editorial Treatment

| Hook Psychology | Editorial Treatment |
|----------------|---------------------|
| Pain/Problem | **Contrast layout:** Product in full color against stark desaturated background. Pull-quote naming the problem in serif italic. Visual tension through asymmetric placement. |
| Aspiration/Identity | **Gallery layout:** Product as centerpiece with aspirational detail elements. Generous white space. Elegant serif headline in Title Case. Premium restraint. |
| Education/Value | **Feature spread:** Product with labeled detail callouts (like a magazine product review). Numbered or bulleted detail elements. Informative serif body text. |
| Social Proof | **Review card overlay:** Product cut-out with a geometric shape containing a pull-quote testimonial. Star rating element. Editorial credibility framing. |
| Transformation | **Before/After editorial:** Split composition — muted "before" left, vivid "after" right. Product bridges the divide. Serif headline spans both halves. |
| Urgency/Scarcity | **Limited edition layout:** Product large and bold, minimal secondary elements. Accent-colored geometric overlay containing the urgency message. CTA prominent. |

## Step 5: Design Principles

### 1. Asymmetry with Intention
- Break bilateral symmetry deliberately — product off-center at a rule-of-thirds position
- Visual tension from unequal weight distribution (heavy left/light right or vice versa)
- The asymmetry should feel designed, not accidental — every element has a reason for its position
- Grid-based underlying structure, but intentionally broken at key points (the way editorial magazines break the grid)

### 2. Selective Color as Visual Hierarchy
- Product in full, vivid brand colors — the most saturated element in the frame
- Background and surroundings in muted, desaturated, or monochrome tones
- Geometric overlays in muted neutrals (off-white, warm gray, soft blush, sage)
- Accent pops limited to geometric overlay elements and CTA
- The selective saturation hierarchy: product (full) > geometric accents (muted) > background (desaturated)

### 3. Layered Depth Without 3D
- Overlapping elements create the impression of layers:
  - Background texture (layer 0)
  - Geometric shapes at mid-depth (layer 1)
  - Product cut-out in foreground (layer 2)
  - Text overlapping all layers (layer 3)
- Subtle shadows between layers (2-4px, soft, directional) reinforce depth
- This flat layering is compositionally distinct from clay diorama's 3D depth

### 4. Editorial Typography
- Serif or serif/sans-serif pairing — fundamentally distinct from all other styles' geometric sans-serif
- Title Case for headlines (not ALL CAPS — editorial voice, not brutalist)
- Smaller body text with longer copy is permitted (editorial format supports reading)
- Pull-quote treatment for key benefits: larger text with quotation marks on a tinted shape
- Text wraps around or overlaps the product cut-out for an integrated, designed feel

### 5. Paper-Texture Background
- Background has tactile quality: paper grain, linen weave, or canvas texture
- Never flat digital color — always some material quality
- Neutral dominant tones: off-white, warm gray, soft blush, sage, cream
- The texture is subtle (5-10% visible) — environmental, not distracting

### 6. No Borders
- Unlike clay diorama and soft brutalism, editorial cutout uses NO border frames
- Instead, overlapping geometric shapes and white space define the composition boundaries
- Elements can extend to or slightly beyond the frame edge (editorial convention)
- The absence of borders signals a different design language

## Mobile Constraint Integration

Editorial cutout layouts leverage asymmetry for mobile scanning. Key rules per aspect ratio:

### 4:5 (1080x1350) — Feed Optimized
- Product cut-out positioned at left or right rule-of-thirds
- Headline in the opposing white space area
- Secondary elements stacked vertically in the narrower column
- CTA at 70% down, centered or aligned with the text column
- Geometric overlays can extend beyond top and side edges

### 1:1 (1080x1080) — Feed Standard / Carousel
- Horizontal editorial spread: product left-center, details right
- Or product centered with geometric overlays and text wrapped around
- Most flexible format for editorial collage composition
- CTA at 75-80% down, centered
- White space at bottom-right for visual breathing room

### 9:16 (1080x1920) — Reels / Stories
- Vertical editorial stack: headline at top, product in upper-center, detail elements below
- Geometric overlays positioned to guide the eye downward
- CTA at 58-62% down (well above 65% safe zone boundary)
- Single-column flow — no side-by-side element placement
- Secondary elements stacked below the product, not beside it

## Quality Checklist

### Composition
- [ ] Layout is asymmetric with intentional visual tension
- [ ] Product at rule-of-thirds position (not centered)
- [ ] White space used as active design element (30%+ of frame)
- [ ] At least one geometric overlay extends beyond frame edge
- [ ] 2-3 secondary elements maximum

### Cut-Out & Color
- [ ] Product has artistic cut-out edges (not pixel-perfect, not sloppy)
- [ ] Selective saturation: product in full vivid color
- [ ] Background and overlays are muted/desaturated
- [ ] Paper, linen, or canvas texture visible on background

### Typography
- [ ] Serif font used for headline (or serif/sans-serif pairing)
- [ ] Title Case (not ALL CAPS)
- [ ] Text wraps around or interacts with the product cut-out
- [ ] Editorial voice — confident, curated, premium

### Brand Anchor
- [ ] Product is visually recognizable as this specific brand's
- [ ] Secondary elements show brand-specific details (not generic)
- [ ] Logo Swap Test passes — composition is ownable

### Geometric Overlays
- [ ] 2-3 shapes overlapping and framing the product
- [ ] Shapes in muted brand secondary or neutral colors
- [ ] At least one shape extends beyond frame edge
- [ ] No borders or complete frames (shapes frame, not contain)

### Mobile
Follow shared mobile checklist in SKILL.md § Mobile Safe Zone Rules.
- [ ] Product and headline legible at 300px width

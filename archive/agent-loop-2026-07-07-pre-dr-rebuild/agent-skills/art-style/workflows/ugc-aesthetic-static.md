# UGC-Aesthetic Static

Designed to look like a screenshot from a real social media post or review. Simulated UGC elements — screenshot framing, star ratings, profile headers, testimonial quotes — create social proof through visual format. The medium IS the message.

**Prerequisites:** Hook-bank file must exist. Main Agent triggers hook-methodology skill first.

---

## Style Overview

**What it is:** Static ad images designed to simulate user-generated content — review cards, social media post screenshots, testimonial cards, or text message exchanges. The testimonial text (customer quote in conversational language) is the hero element. Product appears as it would in a real person's photo: on their counter, held casually, in contextual use. Lo-fi quality markers (slight grain, warm color cast, casual framing) signal authenticity.

**Why it works:**
- Social proof is the strongest conversion driver — people trust other people over brands
- The "screenshot" format blends into organic feed content, bypassing ad fatigue
- Testimonial text as the visual hero puts the customer's voice first
- Lo-fi aesthetic signals authenticity — looks real, not produced
- Interface elements (stars, avatars, likes) are universally recognized trust signals
- Works for any product category where social proof drives conversion

**Visual Characteristics:**

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Format | Simulated social media post, review card, testimonial card, or text exchange |
| Hero element | Customer testimonial quote in conversational language |
| Product presence | Contextual — as it would appear in a real person's photo (10-20% of frame) |
| Interface elements | Star ratings, avatar circles, like/comment counts, screenshot framing |
| Quality markers | Slight grain (5-8%), warm color cast, casual composition |
| Typography | System fonts or casual sans-serif (Inter, SF Pro) — looks social media, not designed |
| Brand CTA | Clean CTA strip at bottom in brand accent color — the only "designed" element |

## Step 4: Visual Concept Creation

For each hook, build the UGC concept by answering:

### 1. What UGC Format Best Serves This Hook?

| Hook Psychology | Best UGC Format | Why |
|----------------|----------------|-----|
| Pain/Problem | **Before/after review card** — customer describes the problem they had and how the product solved it | Pain hooks are most convincing when a real person confirms the pain existed |
| Social Proof | **Screenshot of social post with high engagement** — visible likes, comments, shares | Social proof hooks are amplified by visible social validation metrics |
| Education/Value | **Detailed review card with star rating** — customer explains specific features or benefits | Education hooks pair with informed customer reviews that teach through experience |
| Aspiration/Identity | **Instagram post screenshot** — aspirational casual photo with enthusiastic caption | Aspiration works when a real person embodies the desired identity |
| Urgency/Scarcity | **Recent review with timestamp** — "just bought this before it sold out" energy | Urgency is reinforced when other customers confirm the rush |
| Transformation | **Before/after testimonial card** — split or sequential format showing the change | Transformation claims need customer proof of the change |
| Curiosity/Question | **Social post with high comment count** — implies debate, many people engaging | Curiosity hooks gain power when many people are also curious (social interest) |

### 2. What Testimonial Text Serves as the Hero?

**Rules for writing the testimonial:**
- Conversational language — how a real person talks, not how a brand writes
- Specific detail — mention the product by name, reference a specific feature or result
- First person — "I", "my", "me" — personal experience narrative
- Imperfect grammar is fine — occasional lowercase, ellipsis, casual punctuation signals authenticity
- Length: 2-4 sentences maximum — enough to tell a micro-story, short enough to read at scroll speed
- The testimonial should answer: "What changed because of this product?"

### 3. What Social Proof Elements Amplify Credibility?

Select 2-4 from:
- **Star rating** (4.5-5 stars, shown as visual star icons)
- **Avatar** (generic but realistic avatar circle with initials or silhouette)
- **Username** (first name + last initial — "Sarah M." or "@sarahm_2024")
- **Verified badge** (checkmark or "Verified Purchase" label)
- **Engagement metrics** (likes, comments, shares — realistic numbers, not inflated)
- **Timestamp** ("3 days ago", "March 2026" — recent, adding freshness)
- **Helpful votes** ("47 people found this helpful")

## Step 5: Design Principles

### 1. The Format IS the Message
- The UGC format (review card, social post, testimonial card) communicates authenticity before the viewer reads a single word
- The layout should be immediately recognizable as a type of social content — not an ad
- The only "designed" element is the brand CTA strip at the bottom
- Everything above the CTA strip should look like it came from a real person

### 2. Lo-Fi Authenticity Markers
- Slight grain/noise overlay at 5-8% opacity (phone camera quality)
- Warm color cast — shifted slightly toward amber (phone camera auto-white-balance)
- Casual composition — not perfectly centered, not studio-lit
- Imperfect text rendering — system fonts, not brand fonts for the testimonial
- If product photo is included: casual angle, not hero-shot composition

### 3. Testimonial Text as Visual Hero
- Customer quote occupies the largest text area (40-50% of frame in text-centric formats)
- Quote in larger text than any other element
- Attribution (name, stars, date) in smaller text below the quote
- The reader should read the testimonial before they read the brand name

### 4. Product as Context (Not Hero)
- Product appears at 10-20% of frame — a supporting element
- Shown as a real person would show it: on their counter, held casually, in use
- Not studio-lit, not perfectly angled — casual, natural placement
- If the UGC format is text-centric (review card, tweet), product may be absent entirely

### 5. Brand CTA Strip
- The bottom 10-15% of the frame is a clean brand CTA strip
- This is the ONLY element that looks "designed" — it signals this is an ad, not a random screenshot
- Brand accent color background, brand name or CTA in contrasting text
- Clean, minimal — product name/offer and CTA button or text
- This strip bridges "real social content" (above) with "brand action" (below)

### 6. Interface Authenticity
- Star ratings, avatars, and engagement metrics should look like they belong to a real platform
- Use realistic engagement numbers (not "1M likes" — more like "247 likes" or "32 comments")
- Timestamps should be recent ("3 days ago", "last week") to signal freshness
- Platform-specific details (but never exactly copying a platform's UI — suggest, don't replicate)

## Mobile Constraint Integration

UGC-aesthetic is inherently designed for mobile — it simulates content native to mobile platforms. Key rules per aspect ratio:

### 4:5 (1080x1350) — Feed Optimized
- Review card layout works perfectly — card centered with margin
- Testimonial text fills 40-50% of the card
- Product photo (if present) as small element within the card
- CTA strip at bottom 12-15%
- Most natural format for review cards and testimonial cards

### 1:1 (1080x1080) — Feed Standard / Carousel
- Social post screenshot format works well — profile bar, square photo, caption below
- Or: testimonial card with rating, quote, and product image in grid
- CTA strip at bottom 12-15%
- Good for "quote card" style — large testimonial text centered

### 9:16 (1080x1920) — Reels / Stories
- Full social post screenshot — profile bar, tall photo, engagement bar, caption
- Vertical card format with more room for longer testimonials
- CTA strip at bottom (well above safe zone in this tall format)
- The tall format mimics Story screenshots naturally
- Profile bar below 14% safe zone, CTA strip above 65% boundary

## Quality Checklist

### Testimonial
- [ ] Written in first-person conversational voice
- [ ] Mentions specific results, features, or experiences (not generic)
- [ ] References the brand's owned differentiator in customer language
- [ ] 2-4 sentences — long enough for a story, short enough to scroll-read
- [ ] Imperfect grammar acceptable — signals authenticity
- [ ] Logo Swap Test: testimonial couldn't describe a competitor credibly

### UGC Format
- [ ] Format is immediately recognizable as social content (review, post, card)
- [ ] Interface elements look realistic (stars, avatar, engagement metrics)
- [ ] Engagement numbers are realistic (not inflated)
- [ ] Timestamp is recent (days or weeks, not months)
- [ ] Platform UI is suggested, not exactly replicated

### Lo-Fi Quality
- [ ] Grain/noise overlay at 5-8%
- [ ] Warm color cast on product photo area
- [ ] Casual composition (not perfectly centered or studio-lit)
- [ ] System fonts used for testimonial (not brand fonts)

### Product
- [ ] Product at 10-20% of frame (subordinate to testimonial)
- [ ] Shown in casual, contextual setting (as a real person would photograph)
- [ ] Not studio-lit or hero-composed
- [ ] Product identifiable but not the visual focus

### Brand CTA Strip
- [ ] Clean brand CTA strip at bottom 10-15% of frame
- [ ] Brand accent color background
- [ ] Only designed element in the composition
- [ ] CTA text is clear and action-oriented

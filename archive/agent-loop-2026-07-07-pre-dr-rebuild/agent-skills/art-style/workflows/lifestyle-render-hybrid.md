# Lifestyle-Render Hybrid

Atmospheric environmental rendering showing the product in an idealized real-world context. Warm, inviting scenes with natural lighting — the product lives in the viewer's aspirational life. No photorealistic humans, but the scene implies someone was just here.

**Prerequisites:** Hook-bank file must exist. Main Agent triggers hook-methodology skill first.

---

## Style Overview

**What it is:** The product is placed within a rendered atmospheric environment — a morning kitchen counter, a minimalist bathroom shelf, a cozy bedside table, an outdoor picnic spread. The environment takes 60% of the frame, the product occupies 30%, and text occupies 10%. Soft, natural-looking lighting (golden hour, window light, diffused daylight) creates warmth and desire. Depth of field keeps the product sharp while the environment blurs softly behind.

**Why it works:**
- Environmental context increases desire — the viewer sees the product IN their aspirational life
- Scene tells a micro-story without words (the coffee cup next to the supplement, the journal beside the skincare)
- Natural lighting creates warmth and emotional connection that isolated product shots can't match
- Depth of field naturally directs attention to the product without graphic design tricks
- "Lived-in" quality feels authentic — not sterile studio, not clinical product shot
- No photorealistic humans avoids uncanny valley while lifestyle context drives aspiration

**Visual Characteristics:**

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Environment | Atmospheric, aspirational, warm, natural-looking |
| Product | Sharp in foreground, 30% of frame, positioned at natural eye-level |
| Lighting | Soft natural: golden hour, window light, diffused daylight |
| Depth of field | Shallow to medium — product sharp, environment softly blurred |
| Complementary objects | 2-3 lifestyle items creating context (not competing products) |
| Color grading | Warm, golden-hour treatment across the environment |
| Text | Minimal overlay — scene communicates, text adds CTA only |

## Step 4: Visual Concept Creation

For each hook, build the lifestyle scene by answering:

### 1. What Micro-Story Does the Scene Tell?

Every lifestyle-render scene implies a moment — someone was just here, is about to be here, or this is a snapshot of a daily ritual.

| Hook Psychology | Micro-Story Moment |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Pain/Problem | **The moment before relief:** Scene shows the problem context (messy desk, exhausted morning) with the product as the solution sitting ready. Slightly chaotic environment implies the pain, product is the calm element. |
| Aspiration/Identity | **The ideal morning/evening:** Perfect ritual scene. Product integrated into an aspirational daily routine. Everything is harmonious — this is the life the viewer wants. |
| Education/Value | **The considered choice:** Product positioned alongside its ingredients or benefits (botanicals near the skincare, coffee beans near the brew). Educational through visual association. |
| Social Proof | **Someone else's ritual:** Scene shows evidence of use — an open product, a partially consumed item, a well-worn routine. The implied user is just off-camera. |
| Urgency/Scarcity | **The last one:** Product positioned with visual scarcity cues — nearly empty shelf, single item remaining, seasonal environmental elements (autumn leaves, melting ice). |
| Transformation | **Before-during-after in one scene:** Environment shows traces of transformation — the old item being replaced, the new product taking center stage, the scene transitioning from cold to warm. |

### 2. What Environment Best Serves the Mood?

Select the environment based on the emotional territory, not just the product category:

- **Kitchen counter:** Morning routine, fresh start, energy, wellness
- **Bathroom shelf:** Self-care, skincare ritual, personal attention
- **Bedside table:** End of day, rest, comfort, intimacy
- **Living room surface:** Lifestyle integration, daily presence, casual luxury
- **Outdoor setting:** Freedom, natural connection, active lifestyle
- **Workspace desk:** Productivity, focus, professional enhancement

### 3. What Complementary Objects Create Context?

Select 2-3 objects that:
- Relate to the brand's specific value proposition (not just "nice things")
- Don't compete for attention (all subordinate to the product)
- Imply the viewer's lifestyle (aspiration through association)
- Are partially visible or casually placed (not staged-feeling)

## Step 5: Design Principles

### 1. Environmental Storytelling
- The environment takes 60% of the frame — it IS the story
- Product occupies 30% — prominent but contextual (part of a world, not isolated)
- The scene should feel like a photograph of a real moment, not a studio setup
- Complementary objects are casually placed — not grid-aligned, not symmetrical
- "Someone was just here" energy — a life in progress, not a display

### 2. Natural Lighting as Emotion
- One dominant natural light source (window, door, golden hour sun)
- Direction from the side (not flat front or overhead) creates depth and warmth
- Soft shadows with warm tones (never harsh, never pure black)
- Depth of field: product sharp in foreground, environment softly blurred
- Light hits the product first — it's the brightest, sharpest element

### 3. Warm, Lived-In Color Grading
- Dominant warm tones: amber, golden, cream, soft earth
- Brand colors integrated through the product itself and subtle environmental elements
- Golden-hour color grading on the environment (even interior scenes)
- No flat color fields — everything has ambient lighting variation
- Shadows are warm (brown-gold) or cool-warm transition, never neutral gray

### 4. Depth Creates Desire
- Shallow to medium depth of field separates product from environment
- Foreground product is tack-sharp with visible detail
- Background environment is softly blurred (bokeh quality)
- This depth automatically creates visual hierarchy without graphic design
- Natural framing: table edge, shelf edge, window frame as compositional anchors

### 5. Minimal Text
- Text is the smallest component — 10% of frame
- Headline as short statement (not a full sentence — the scene communicates)
- Semi-transparent background bar behind text for legibility
- Warm-toned text that harmonizes with the scene (not harsh white or black)
- Text positioned in natural negative space within the scene (sky, wall, table surface)
- The scene should communicate the value even without reading the text

### 6. Product Placement Rules
- Product at natural eye-level within the scene (on a counter, shelf, table — not floating)
- Product facing slightly toward camera (label visible, brand identifiable)
- Product lit from the dominant light source (brightest element in the scene)
- Scale is realistic — product is the size it would actually be in this environment
- Product is IN the scene, not ON the scene — it belongs here

## Mobile Constraint Integration

Lifestyle-render scenes are naturally immersive on mobile. Key rules per aspect ratio:

### 4:5 (1080x1350) — Feed Optimized
- Scene fills the entire frame — no borders, no dead space
- Product in center or center-right at counter/table level
- Headline in upper negative space (wall, sky, blurred area)
- CTA at 68-72% down
- Depth of field creates natural hierarchy without graphic elements

### 1:1 (1080x1080) — Feed Standard / Carousel
- Horizontal scene composition — product at center or rule-of-thirds
- Complementary objects can flank left and right
- Headline overlaid on quietest area of the scene
- CTA at 78-82% down
- Can show more environmental width (counter length, shelf span)

### 9:16 (1080x1920) — Reels / Stories
- Vertical scene — tall format amplifies intimacy and immersion
- Product at vertical center, environmental context above and below
- Headline in upper zone (below 14% safe), CTA at 55-62%
- The tall format creates a "looking into the scene" quality
- Extra environmental context available (more ceiling/window above, more floor/surface below)

# The Style Grammar — the maker's whole look, not a style you reach for

The maker has been named (`layer-stack.md`, step 3). The type grammar reads the *lettering* off that maker; this document reads the **whole look** off the same maker — is this a real photo, a constructed poster, a borrowed meme, a crafted scene, a drawing? Same move, one level up.

Style is not picked. **The look is the maker's medium** — a photographer makes a photograph, a meme page makes a meme, a sculptor makes a crafted scene. Name the maker and the look mostly falls out; this grammar just tells you what each look *signals*, so you can read it back off the maker instead of defaulting to whatever the renderer hands you.

> **The look is downstream of the maker, then pressure-tested by the click.** Once the maker names the look, run it past the four style questions (`layer-stack.md`, bottom): does the click still land in two seconds (an empty stage beats a clever one), is the realism earned, is there a feed vernacular worth borrowing, and what does the comp wallpaper forbid. One maker, one look.

**The honest ceiling — the renderer renders any look, but it renders the *average* of that look.** Say "a real photo" and you get a stock photo; say "a meme" and you get a fake-clean meme; say "an illustration" and you get corporate-blob clip-art. The model owns every look and has no taste about *which version*. So every entry resolves to a **specific, named** version of the look with its real conventions — the same dig-to-the-real-thing move the type grammar makes. **"Photorealistic / high quality" is never the instruction; the named maker is.**

**Type lives inside the look.** Once the look lands, the lettering is read off the same maker via `type-grammar.md` — a real photo usually carries native or handwritten type, a poster carries condensed-caps, an editorial look carries grid-set serif. One maker decides both reads.

**A place to dig, not a menu.** Five common looks below, not the only five — read the maker and resolve to whatever the look truly is.

---

## How to read an entry

Each entry has four parts (same shape as `type-grammar.md`):

- **Signal** — what this look says to a viewer in the first half-second
- **When earned** — the maker (or claim) that makes it the honest call
- **Resolve to** — the *specific, named* version to render (the part the model won't reach for on its own)
- **The fake twin** — the generic version that wears the same clothes, and why it's forbidden

---

## Entries

The looks run from the most *found* (a real photograph) to the most *made* (a drawing), with the borrowed and crafted looks between. You don't shop this list — the maker tells you which look you're already in.

### Photographic-real — "this actually happened"

- **Signal:** true. Nobody built this for an ad; it was captured. The honest, native default. For an *impossible* scene the realism flips meaning — a real-looking photo of a turtle on a skateboard is the whole surprise.
- **When earned:** the maker is a photographer, a phone, or a documentary moment — most found scenes, testimonials, demonstrations, before/afters. Also the made-real branch (`layer-stack.md`, step 2): an invented scene rendered as a real photo, where realism *is* the surprise. This is the resting default — you need a reason to leave it.
- **Resolve to:** a *specific* photographer's version, never "a photo." Name who shot it and how — phone-HDR snapshot · studio strobe · film grain · paparazzi flash · documentary available-light — with the grain, true shadows, and slight imperfection that prove it's real. "Photorealistic" is not an instruction; it is the *output* of naming the photographer (`layer-stack.md`, step 4).
- **The fake twin:** AI-gloss — the too-clean, perfectly-lit, plasticky render with no grain or honest shadow. That is the photoreal *center*, the category average, on the forbid list. Its cousin is stock-photo genericness (the smiling model in a void).

### Constructed / poster — "the brand at full volume"

- **Signal:** loud, designed, declarative. A made object, not a captured moment — the brand turning its voice all the way up.
- **When earned:** the made-object branch (`layer-stack.md`, step 2) — the take's voice *is* the delivery vehicle (giant type is the hook), the claim is numeric or comparative (its native habitat is a graphic), the category wallpaper is quiet so constructed loudness is the contrast move, or it is a retail / offer moment. Loud must be argued, never defaulted to — **and never over a matched format's native rule:** if the take chose a format whose register is native/collected (a testimonial, a founder-POV), that register outranks "the wallpaper is quiet." Find the contrast inside the native form; do not turn the format into a poster.
- **Resolve to:** a real poster or graphic vernacular — a Swiss type poster · an OOH billboard · a sale flyer · a sports fight card · a data-viz chart · a side-by-side comparison. Real grid discipline, high ink coverage, type-as-image. (The lettering lands on condensed-caps/loud — see `type-grammar.md`.)
- **The fake twin:** a product floating on a gradient under a polite headline — the slide-deck look. A "poster" with no grid, no conviction, no real reason to be loud. Constructed without craft is worse than a plain photo.

### Borrowed — "borrowed trust"

- **Signal:** familiar, native to the feed, trusted by association. It looks like a thing the buyer already scrolls past and believes — not an ad.
- **When earned:** the buyer's feed offers a vernacular to borrow (`layer-stack.md`, style Q3) and the maker is that vernacular's native author — a meme page, a forwarded WhatsApp, a screenshot, a newspaper clipping, a retro ad. Strongest when the borrowed form's *trust* is exactly what the claim needs.
- **Resolve to:** the real conventions of the *specific* borrowed form — a real meme-page layout (Impact caps, the slightly-compressed JPEG) · a real newsprint column (halftone dots, a masthead, justified columns) · a real WhatsApp forward with its UI · a screenshot with true chrome. Dig to the *real* form; a vague "meme-style" renders as a fake meme.
- **The fake twin:** a *polished* version of a scrappy form — a crisp designed "meme" with perfect type, a clean "newspaper" with no halftone. The polish betrays the borrow and kills the trust. Faking the form's credibility markers (invented mastheads, fake forward-counts) is the honesty-gate violation from `layer-stack.md`.

### Crafted / sculptural — "care and feeling" → routes into the clay machine

- **Signal:** made with care; tactile, warm, narrative. The opposite of fast-and-scrappy — this look says *someone built this by hand, and the feeling is the point.*
- **When earned:** the claim is emotional, experiential, or story-shaped rather than a quick proof or a loud number — a hospitality brand, a school, a heritage product, a gifting moment. The want is *feeling*, not *evidence.*
- **Resolve to:** **this is where the derivation hands off — it does not resolve here.** The crafted/sculptural look is exactly what the existing **clay machine** already builds well: `../../art-style/workflows/anderson-clay-diorama.md` and `../../art-style/workflows/soft-brutalism-clay.md` (with tactile cousins `../../art-style/workflows/analog-craft.md` and `../../art-style/workflows/editorial-cutout.md`). The style grammar's job ends at "the look is crafted/sculptural"; *how* to build it — the materials, the lighting, the tactile detail — is what those workflows do. Route into them; don't re-teach it here.
- **The fake twin:** a crafted look slapped on a fast-proof claim that needed a plain photo — decoration that slows the two-second click. Craft for its own sake fails the corridor (clutter kills the click). Earn the craft with an emotional claim, or use a real photo.

### Drawn / illustrated — "concept made visible"

- **Signal:** a mind made this — a concept, a metaphor, or an impossibility *drawn* rather than shot. Reads as clever, editorial, or explanatory.
- **When earned:** the claim is best *performed* by a drawing — an abstract idea with no photographable referent, a diagram or explainer, a metaphor that's cleaner drawn than staged, or an illustrated brand whose whole identity is drawn (`layer-stack.md`, step 2). Note the boundary: an *impossible scene* defaults to photoreal (realism = surprise); choose illustration only when the drawing itself does work a photo cannot.
- **Resolve to:** a *specific* illustration vernacular — a New Yorker single-panel cartoon · a technical line diagram · a children's-book gouache · a vintage advertising illustration · a flat editorial vector. Name the illustrator's world, not "an illustration."
- **The fake twin:** generic vector "corporate Memphis" (the blob-people clip-art look) — the default the renderer reaches for when you say "illustration" with no world named. Its cousin is illustrating something that would hit harder as a real photo (drawing to dodge a hard shot).

---

The look and the type are two reads off **one maker.** Where the look lands on **crafted/sculptural**, the derivation leaves this grammar and enters the **clay machine** (the art-style workflows above); everywhere else the look resolves here and the lettering resolves in `type-grammar.md`. The spine is always `layer-stack.md`: name the maker first, and both reads fall out of it.

# PAS × real-world moment — a DR format entry

A **format** is a proven job/structure, not a look. This document carries only what is *PAS-and-native-moment-specific*: the job it does, the copy it needs, the material it requires, and what to forbid. It does **not** decide the look. The look is *derived* — by the shared method in [`../layer-stack.md`](../layer-stack.md): read the message's provenance → name one real maker → resolve every element from it (the look via [`../style-grammar.md`](../style-grammar.md), the lettering via [`../type-grammar.md`](../type-grammar.md)). **Machine, not skin:** the format is the machine (the PAS argument and its native moment); the look comes from the maker, never from a fixed skin or a brand-color lookup.

This is the first entry that names its two halves out loud — the **argument** it runs (PAS: problem → agitate → solve) and the **disguise** it wears (a real-world moment, shown native). Testimonial and founder-POV bundle the same two halves silently; here they're visible, because this is the seam we'll later split *engines* from *skins* along. For now it is one format, built and validated like the other two.

Two brands' PAS moments look nothing alike — not because the format varies, but because each is filled with that buyer's real problem, that product's real fix, and the real scene that problem actually lives in.

---

## Philosophy — the job, and the cut that builds everything below

**The job: catch the buyer inside their problem, and hand them the fix — disguised as a real moment, not an ad.** The native scene buys the scroll-stop (it reads as a real person's post, not a brand's ad); the PAS argument does the selling (name the pain they're feeling, make it vivid, show the product as the answer).

This is the **scroll-killer for the problem-aware** — the buyer who *feels* the pain but hasn't committed to a solution. Where testimonial leans on a peer's proof and founder-POV on a real person's trust, PAS leans on **recognition**: the buyer sees their own problem named, in a scene that looks like their own life, and the guard drops.

Everything PAS-and-moment-specific falls out of one cut — what is **structurally necessary**, with everything else forbidden:

- **Necessary** (these five, nothing else): the **PROBLEM** (a real, felt buyer pain, in their own terms) · the **AGITATION** (that pain made vivid — its cost, named) · the **SOLUTION** (the product as the answer to *that* problem) · the **ASK** (a plain CTA) · the **MOMENT** (the real scene the problem lives in, shown native — the disguise that makes it not-an-ad).
- **Forbidden** (the same cut's other half): the rhetorical-question opener ("Tired of X?"), a stock-photo problem-face, a studio-polished look, an invented problem or exaggerated consequence, a pretty scene carrying no problem-and-fix, the product hero-center as a packshot.

> **The feel that governs the format:** it must read like *a real person caught their own problem-moment on their phone and is telling you what fixed it* — not a brand staging a problem to sell you something. The instant the scene looks art-directed, both the disguise and the trust collapse at once.

## Match — when the cell reaches for this format

The **angle decides the format.** The founder never picks it; the cell matches it. Reach for PAS × real-world moment when both are true:

- **Buyer awareness: problem-aware is the home lane** — they feel the pain but haven't bought into a fix yet (also unaware → problem-aware, where PAS *names* a pain they feel but haven't put words to). Less for most-aware buyers, who already accept the solution and convert faster on an offer or a stat.
- **The want is "something that actually fixes *this specific thing*"** — and the product's real strength is that it solves a sharp, nameable problem that has a **real-world moment** it lives in (a time, a place, a scene).

**Awareness tunes the emphasis** (a dial, not a different format): cold → lead with the **problem stated as recognition** ("Every whey I tried left me bloated"), the moment doing heavy disarming work; warm/hot → compress problem-and-agitate, lean on the **solve and the CTA** — they already feel it.

### Material gate (hard — this gate is the format's honesty)

This format **requires a real, documented buyer problem** — a pain that exists in research, in the buyer's own language (their scar). No real pain → you cannot anchor PAS honestly; you would be *inventing the problem*, which is this format's one unforgivable sin (the parallel to a faked review or a fabricated founder). It also requires the product to **honestly solve that problem** (the solution claim sits inside the angle's proof) and a **real-world moment** the problem can be shown in (almost always available — pains live somewhere). If there is no real documented pain, route to a format whose gate you can meet — or, if none of the library molds fits, **build freestyle on the shared method** (`../layer-stack.md`). Never force a mold, and never invent the problem to fill the structure.

## The copy skeleton — named slots, each tracing to the job

A format is a **copy skeleton**: named slots, each filled from the brand's real content. Every slot traces to one of the necessary elements — if a slot can't trace, it's cut.

| Slot | Required? | Source | The necessary element it serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem line (the hook)** | required | the buyer's real pain, in their words — research-anchored, authored | **PROBLEM** — the recognition that stops the scroll |
| **Agitation line** | 0–1, flex | the pain's cost, made vivid (authored, true) | **AGITATION** — why it matters now |
| **Solution line** | required | the product as the fix (claim inside proof) | **SOLUTION** — the answer to *that* problem |
| **CTA** | required | written, plain | **ASK** |
| **The moment (scene)** | required | the real scene the problem lives in — derived | **MOMENT** — the native disguise |

**The copy is *authored*, not selected — but anchored.** Like founder-POV (and unlike testimonial's verbatim selection), the P-A-S lines are written. The discipline therefore moves to the anchor: **the problem must trace to a real, documented pain** (verbatim from the buyer's own words where research has them; authored-in-their-voice from a documented scar otherwise), and the **solution claim must sit inside the proof.** The *pain* is always true; only the phrasing is composed.

**The format bends to the material — never the reverse:**

- **Drop slots that aren't real; never pad.** No real agitation cost → cut the agitate line; problem → solution is a complete PAS.
- **The agitation must be true** — the real cost of the real problem, never a manufactured fear or a medical scare beyond proof.
- **The problem must be a specific lived experience, not a marketer's question.** "Every whey I tried left me bloated by lunch" is a person; "Tired of bloating?" is the cliché this whole format dies on (see forbid). The specificity is what a rival can't run.

## What the format owns visually — and what it does NOT

The format owns only the **job-structure** — the two rules that come from "catch them in the moment, disguised as real":

1. **The moment leads.** The eye lands on a real candid scene — the problem's habitat — first, not on a product and not on a designed headline. The scene is what reads as "a real post."
2. **The product is native to the scene.** It sits where it would really sit (on the nightstand, in the gym bag, by the kettle) — present and recognizable, never the center-stage packshot. The moment it dominates, this stops being a native moment and becomes a product ad.

**Everything else is NOT a format decision** — it is *derived* by the shared method:

- **Read the provenance → name the maker.** The message is a person showing their own problem-moment, so the provenance points at **a real person's phone photo of that scene** — the native artifact. The maker decides the rest.
- **The subject of that photo is DERIVED, not picked** (this is the heart of the real-world moment). Ask: *given this problem and this buyer, what would actually be in the real photo of this moment, and who would post it?* A sleep aid's 1am nightstand · a cat treat's cat mid-refusal · a road-trip snack's cupholder · a daily-protein buyer's gym bag. Human, hand, pet, object, scene — all open; the problem and the buyer decide. **NOT a fixed menu, NOT "a person using the product."**
- **Resolve the look in [`../style-grammar.md`](../style-grammar.md)** → **photographic-real** (a real phone snapshot — named device, real light, grain, true shadows); **the lettering in [`../type-grammar.md`](../type-grammar.md)** → **system/native** (the IG-story caption hand, typed over the photo). One maker decides both — a *designed brand headline* over a real photo is two images stapled together (the v6/v7 fail).
- **Default to native.** A real phone snapshot beats a styled set; polish is the exception, only for a brand whose whole positioning is polish.
- **The honesty line:** a *real* moment shown as a real photo is truthful and native; a staged "candid," a stock problem-face, or an AI-gloss render is the forbidden version.

## What this format hands the shot spec

After matching and filling, the cell carries forward the back-half contract (`../shot-spec.md`). The format pre-fills the **content**; the shared method fills the **maker/treatment**:

- **Claim** — the promise the solution makes (the fix for the named problem).
- **On-image copy (placed)** — problem line · agitation line (if used) · solution line · CTA. (Authored; problem verbatim from the buyer's words where research has them.)
- **Objects** — the **scene** (the problem's real habitat, named in full) · the real **product**, native to the scene (bound to reference) · nothing styled-in that a real person wouldn't have there.
- **Action** — a caught real moment native to the scene (the shake set down mid-gulp, the bag half-unzipped on the bench). A real verb, not a posed state — and never a posed "sufferer."
- **Composition** — the moment leads, the product sits native (the two job-rules above); the text overlays story-style (problem largest/top, solve + CTA bottom); the rest from the maker.
- **Product binding** — the real reference; what must stay true (colorway, wordmark, badges, the exact spec number — pixels outrank prose); reproduce 100% identical, exclude listing overlays.
- **Staging record** — the **maker** named from the provenance (a real person's phone photo of the derived scene), carried verbatim from the derivation (not chosen here).
- **Must-show** — the real-world scene · the problem line · the real product (native) · CTA.
- **Forbid** — the job's other half (below), compiled into positive decisions.

Then the back half runs unchanged: **compile-verbatim → bind reference → render once → we look.**

## No worked example — on purpose

A fully-resolved example (setting, light, props, and CTA all named) would act as a **template**: the next run reaches for *that surface* instead of deriving its own, and the swap test fails by construction — it is precisely how a prior render's specifics get cloned onto an unrelated brand. This entry teaches the **moves**, not a picture of one output. Derive every element from *this* brand's real provenance via [`../layer-stack.md`](../layer-stack.md); the finished look is the *output* of that derivation, never an input borrowed from here.

## Forbid / anti-patterns (the other half of the job-cut)

| Never | Why it isn't the job | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| the **rhetorical-question opener** ("Tired of bloating?") | the platonic PAS cliché — a marketer's voice, an ad on sight | the problem as a real lived line ("Every whey I tried left me bloated") |
| a **stock-photo problem-face** (model clutching their stomach) | a generic sufferer is the opposite of a real moment | the problem's real habitat, or a real candid tell — never a posed sufferer |
| a **studio-polished / packshot** look | reads as an ad; kills the native disguise | a real phone snapshot of where the product lives |
| a **pretty scene with no problem-and-fix** | a cute moment with no payload is a brand post, not DR | the scene must carry the P-A-S; the payload is mandatory |
| an **invented problem or exaggerated cost** | fabricating the pain is fraud (the material gate) | the real documented pain, the true cost |
| a **medical / fear claim** beyond proof | scaremongering past what's true | only the consequence the proof/customers support |
| "**Say goodbye to** [problem]" / a spec-dump solve | machine cadences on the floor list | the fix in a real person's plain words |
| the **product hero-center**, large | a packshot, not a native moment | product sits native in the scene |
| a **designed brand headline** over the photo | two images stapled together (v6/v7) | the native caption hand — one maker |
| **manufactured urgency** ("only today!") | fake scarcity is on the floor | a plain CTA |

## Checklist — before the render

- [ ] Problem traces to a **real documented pain** (the buyer's words/scar), not invented
- [ ] **P-A-S present**; the solution claim sits inside the proof
- [ ] Problem is a **lived line, not a rhetorical question** ("Tired of X?" is the floor)
- [ ] **Subject/moment DERIVED** ("what's really in this scene, who'd post it"), not menu-picked
- [ ] **Payload gate passed** — the scene carries problem + fix, not a cute moment alone
- [ ] **Maker named** = a real person's phone photo; look = photographic-real (named device/light); type = native caption (one maker, not a designed headline)
- [ ] Product **bound to the real reference**; spec fact verified (24g, not 29g); listing overlays excluded
- [ ] Product is **native to the scene**, not a hero packshot
- [ ] **Every element swept and named** — device, angle, light, surface, type, CTA (nothing vague)
- [ ] **Floor cleared** — none of the whey category clichés, no styled flat-lay
- [ ] Reads like **a real person caught their own moment**, not a staged ad
- [ ] Nothing **invented**; nothing from the forbid list
- [ ] **Generated fresh** from the prompt + product reference (not edited off a prior render)

**The swap test (the cell's bar):** could a rival run this with a logo swap? With the specific bloating scar + TWT's real easy-on-the-stomach strength + the real gym-bag moment — no. With "Tired of bad protein? Try ours" over a stock gym photo — yes, and it fails. **Genericness lives in the content (the specific true pain + the derived real moment), not the structure.** The P-A-S skeleton is the proven floor; the real documented problem + the derived native scene are what make it un-swappable. You need both.

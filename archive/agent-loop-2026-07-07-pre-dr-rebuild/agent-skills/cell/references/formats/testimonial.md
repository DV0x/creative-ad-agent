# Testimonial — a DR format entry

A **format** is a proven job/structure, not a look. This document carries only what is *testimonial-specific*: the job it does, the copy it needs, the material it requires, and what to forbid. It does **not** decide the look. The look is *derived* — by the shared method in [`../layer-stack.md`](../layer-stack.md), the same for every format: read the copy's provenance → name one real maker → resolve every element from it. **Machine, not skin:** the format is the machine (the job and its copy); the look comes from the maker, never from a fixed skin or a brand-color lookup.

Two brands' testimonials look nothing alike — not because the format varies, but because each is filled with that brand's real customer words, real product, and the real artifact those words live in.

---

## Philosophy — the job, and the cut that builds everything below

**The job: peer proof closes the sale.** A stranger trusts another buyer like them more than they trust the brand. So the ad steps back and lets a real customer's specific words do the selling. The brand's voice goes quiet; the evidence talks.

Everything testimonial-specific falls out of one cut. Decompose the job into what is **structurally necessary** — and everything that isn't necessary is forbidden. The same cut builds the skeleton *and* the forbid list.

- **Necessary** (these four, and nothing else): the **PROOF** (a real customer quote) · its **CREDIBILITY** (an attribution — who said it, where) · the **REFERENT** (the product, plus the one specific claim the quote is about) · the **ASK** (a plain CTA).
- **Forbidden** (the other side of the same cut): the decoration the job does *not* need — fake star rows, stock smiling faces, "as seen in" press bars, *invented* review-card chrome, walls of quotes. None of these make peer proof more believable; most make it *less*.

> **The feel that governs the format:** the ad must read like proof someone *collected* — a real review, shown as the real thing — not a poster the brand *designed*. The moment it looks polished, the viewer stops believing the quote is real.

---

## Match — when the cell reaches for this format

The **angle decides the format.** The founder never picks it; the cell matches it. Reach for testimonial when both are true:

- **Buyer awareness:** problem-aware → most-aware — they are *evaluating*, and want reassurance before they commit.
- **The want is social proof:** "does this actually work for people like me?" The brand's real strength is something buyers *doubt until a peer confirms it.*

**Awareness tunes the emphasis** (a dial, not a different format): cold/skeptical → the proof leads with the **doubt named and then resolved** ("I used to bloat — not anymore"); warm/hot → it leads with the **result**, plainly, and the CTA carries more weight.

### Material gate (hard — this gate is the format's honesty)

This format **requires ≥1 real verbatim review in the proof field.** No real customer line → you **cannot** pick testimonial. A fabricated testimonial is not weak creative; it is fraud — the one unforgivable sin of this format. If the proof has no quote, route to a format whose gate you *can* meet — or, if none of the library molds fits, **build freestyle on the shared method** (`../layer-stack.md`). Never force a mold whose gate you can't honestly meet, and never pick this and invent the quote to fill it.

## The copy skeleton — named slots, each tracing to the job

A format is a **copy skeleton**: named slots, each filled with the brand's real content. Every slot traces to one of the four necessary elements — if a slot can't trace, it's cut.

| Slot | Required? | Source | The necessary element it serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hero quote** | required | verbatim from proof | **PROOF** — the one line that does the selling |
| **Supporting quote(s)** | 0–2, flex | verbatim from proof | reinforce with a *different axis* of proof |
| **Source chip** | required (one per quote) | the channel the proof names | **CREDIBILITY** — who said it, where |
| **Product + spec chip** | required | real product image + one verified fact | **REFERENT** — what the quote is about |
| **CTA** | required | written, plain | **ASK** |

**Almost all of it is selected verbatim.** The quotes, the source attributions, the one spec fact — straight from the proof, *zero invention.* Only the CTA is written, plain, in the brand's voice. Testimonial is the **most-verbatim** format in the library; that is the source of its power and the reason the material gate is hard.

**The format bends to the material — never the reverse:**

- **Slots flex in amount; the structure stays rigid in order.** Three real quotes or one — fill what's real, **drop empty slots, never pad.** A "fill every slot" reflex is exactly what makes a model fabricate.
- **A supporting quote must add a NEW axis,** not restate the hero (digestion → taste, not digestion → digestion).
- **The supporting quote must be honest to the maker.** This is the gate that bit us: if the maker is *one real Amazon screenshot*, it can only hold Amazon reviews — a second quote from the brand's website **cannot** share that frame (that artifact never existed). A cross-source supporting quote **moves to the post copy** (still true, off-image), or you change the maker. Never fake the artifact to fit the copy. (See the maker honesty rule in `../layer-stack.md`.)

## What the format owns visually — and what it does NOT

The format owns only the **job-structure** — the two rules that come from "peer proof closes the sale":

1. **The proof leads.** The review/quote is the hero of the frame; the eye lands on it first.
2. **The product is the *referent*, not the hero.** It corroborates the quote — present and recognizable, never the center-stage packshot. The moment the product dominates, this stops being a testimonial and becomes a product ad.

**Everything else is NOT a format decision** — not the type, not the light, not the setting, not the layout chrome. Those are *derived*, by the shared method (`../layer-stack.md`):

- **Read the provenance → name the maker.** For a testimonial the proof almost always names a real review channel, so the provenance points straight at an artifact: a verbatim Amazon / Google / Trustpilot review → the maker is **that review, shown as the real screenshot** (its stars, its Verified-Purchase tag, its reviewer, its date), often pinned on a real use-photo of the product. The type, the chrome, and the light then fall out of that one maker — you do not pick them.
- **Default to native.** A real review screenshot beats a designed "testimonial card" — it reads as collected, not made. A polished card is the exception, only for a brand whose whole positioning is polish.
- **The honesty line:** a *real* review shown as a real review is truthful and native; *inventing* stars, chrome, or a count is the forbidden version. The quote is real → showing it as the real artifact is honest.

*(This replaces the old per-format "look tiers" table, which jumped straight to brand colors and skipped the maker. The maker is the thing that was missing.)*

## What this format hands the shot spec

After matching and filling, the cell carries forward the back-half contract (`../shot-spec.md`). The format pre-fills the **content**; the shared method fills the **maker/treatment**:

- **Claim** — the promise the hero quote proves.
- **On-image copy (verbatim, placed)** — hero quote · source chip · spec fact · CTA. (Cross-source supporting quote → post copy, not the image.)
- **Objects** — the real product (bound to reference) · the review artifact it lives in · nothing else.
- **Action** — minimal / none; a testimonial is a *held artifact*, not a caught verb. (The rare format where the drawability bar is met by a still arrangement.)
- **Composition** — proof leads, product corroborates (the two job-rules above); the rest from the maker.
- **Product binding** — the real reference; what must stay true (colorway, wordmark, badges, the exact spec number — pixels outrank prose).
- **Staging record** — the **maker** named from the provenance, carried verbatim from the derivation (not chosen here).
- **Must-show** — hero quote verbatim · its source · the real product · CTA.
- **Forbid** — the job's other half (below), compiled into positive decisions.

Then the back half runs unchanged: **compile-verbatim → bind reference → render once → we look.**

## No worked example — on purpose

A fully-resolved example (setting, light, props, and CTA all named) would act as a **template**: the next run reaches for *that surface* instead of deriving its own, and the swap test fails by construction — it is precisely how a prior render's specifics get cloned onto an unrelated brand. This entry teaches the **moves**, not a picture of one output. Derive every element from *this* brand's real provenance via [`../layer-stack.md`](../layer-stack.md); the finished look is the *output* of that derivation, never an input borrowed from here.

## Forbid / anti-patterns (the other half of the job-cut)

| Never | Why it isn't the job | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| **invented / faked** stars or chrome | a fabricated artifact is a lie | show the *real* review as the real thing |
| stock-photo smiling faces | a generic happy face is the opposite of a *specific real* customer | the real reviewer's name/metadata, or none |
| "As seen in" / press-logo bar | press ≠ peer proof; that's a different format | a peer's words, in a peer channel |
| two reviews from two platforms in one screenshot | that artifact never existed | one source per artifact; the other → post copy |
| quote walls (4+) | reads as desperate; nobody reads it | one hero + at most two that add a new axis |
| product center-stage, large | that's a packshot, not a testimonial | product corroborates; the proof leads |
| paraphrased / polished quotes | a cleaned-up quote stops sounding real | verbatim, the customer's own phrasing |
| invented quote to fill a slot | fraud; the material gate exists to prevent this | drop the slot, or change format |
| a designed brand-poster headline | reads as *made*, not *collected* | the maker's native type (a screenshot's own font) |

## Checklist — before the render

- [ ] Hero quote is **verbatim** from the proof field
- [ ] **Material gate passed:** every quote traces to a real, named source
- [ ] **Maker named from the provenance** (the real review artifact), not a brand-color lookup
- [ ] **Copy is honest to the maker** — one source per artifact; cross-source quotes → post copy
- [ ] Product **bound to the real reference**; spec fact verified (24g, not 29g); listing overlays excluded (ribbons/badges that live on the website, not the physical pack)
- [ ] Proof leads; product corroborates (not center-stage)
- [ ] **Every element swept and named** — camera, angle, light, surface, type, CTA (nothing vague)
- [ ] Reads like **collected proof**, not a designed poster
- [ ] Nothing **invented/faked** (stars, chrome, counts); nothing from the forbid list
- [ ] **Generated fresh** from the prompt + product reference (not edited off a prior render)

**The swap test (the cell's bar):** could a rival run this with a logo swap? With `"No bloating at all"` tied to TWT's real pack and real Amazon review — no. With vague praise (`"great product!"`) — yes, and it fails. **Genericness lives in the content, not the structure.** The standard skeleton is the proven *floor*; the brand's real quote + real artifact is what makes it un-swappable. You need both.
